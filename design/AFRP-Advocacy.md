## 2. Advocacy as objects

Advocacy on the platform is five objects, each already shaped by another note where one exists, and one register that is new.

### 2.1 The position (the issue)

A **position** is what the Federation says on an issue: one row on the Government Affairs Committee's workspace (CW2's decision queue, P1 §3.4), carrying the issue (a short title and the dated trigger — a bill by number, a policy, an event), the ask (what the Federation asks an office to do, in one sentence per ask), the committee's dated act adopting it (the decision item with the seats present, P1's pattern: the decision is recorded, the deliberation is not), the statements and letters issued under it (P6 rows; P3 LT rows), the alerts sent under it (§2.4), the contacts and meetings held under it (§2.2), its state (proposed, adopted, active, closed, withdrawn) and the drive folder for the committee's own papers (D66).

**Who approves a position.** The record does not say. The committee proposes; practice is that the President signs what goes out; 7.9.3 presupposes "official approved" communication without saying who approves. P6 raised this as *Q-80* for statements and letters and this note carries the same question to the position itself: whether a position is the committee's to adopt, the President's, the Executive Committee's or the Board's, and whether the Legal Advisor's review is required for a position on legislation. Until answered the platform holds the **position approver seat as a register row with no value**; a position may be drafted and adopted by the committee and shown as "adopted by the committee; Federation approval not recorded", and **no statement, letter or alert issues under it** until the approver row is filled and its act recorded — exactly P6 rule 2 (*design choice, following P6*). The 2020 minutes' instinct that positions are the Federation's, taken through governance, and the register entry's sentence ("adopted through governance, not a committee's own") are consistent with this; neither is a rule.

**The position's subject.** P6's *subject* field — *programme*, *legislation*, *candidate* (P6 §6; distinct from P6 §2's *kind*, proactive or reactive) — is carried on the position and inherited by everything issued under it. *Candidate* is refused as a subject on any position (the Federation's letters, its by-laws and D65 speak of legislation and officials, never of candidates; a 501(c)(4) may engage in some campaign activity but it is not what the committee is constituted for, and the platform will not model it without a Board decision — *design choice, labelled*; *Q-132*). *Legislation* is the subject that feeds the lobbying record (§2.6).

**The Federation's "positions" sentence.** One letter says the Federation does not take formal positions on final political outcomes; other letters urge a vote on a named bill. P6's *Q-81* asks the Board whether it has a positions policy. The platform carries the policy, once stated, as a register text shown beside every position of kind *legislation*; it refuses nothing on content on AFRP's own positions (P6 rule 10).

### 2.2 The contact with an office

A **contact** is one dated interaction between the Federation and a public office: a letter sent, a call, a meeting (in Washington, in a district office, at a club, online), a convened meeting with an executive-branch official. It carries:

- **the office**, as a row on the **institution registry** — the office itself (a House or Senate office by state and district, a State Department bureau, a White House office), with the office's public address and its own general channel. The office row is an institution, never a person. **The platform does not hold the office-holder's or a staffer's personal data as member data and holds no profile of any person in the office.** A staffer's name and office e-mail — which the tracker holds today — are a *professional contact at an institution*, on P6 §3's media-contact shape (name, institution, role, professional channel, source, dated), on the institution registry under the office, readable by the committee's seats only, never in any member audience, never exported, and withheld after an inactivity parameter the committee sets (the same parameter shape as the other contact records; *Q-70* asks whether the Federation wants staffer rows at all or only the office's general channel);
- **the position** it was made under (§2.1), or "general relationship" where no position applies (the 2020 goal of a yearly contact with every foreign-affairs staffer);
- **the kind** (letter, call, meeting, convened meeting, event invitation) and **the date**;
- **the ask** made (read from the position's asks; one or more);
- **the outcome**, as a coded value the committee defines on a register row (met, declined, no reply, signed, will consider, referred) with the date; **never a free-text note about a person** (the club plan's rule for follow-ups, Breeze Parity §4 r2, applied to offices: the "Outreach Updates" and "Other Notes" columns of the tracker are not imported and have no field). Where the committee needs to record *what was said*, it is a document in the committee's drive folder linked from the contact (D66), under the committee's own confidentiality, and the platform holds the link;
- **who acted for the Federation**: the committee seat, the member lead (§2.3) or the participant (for a Day of Action meeting), as a seat or a member record, with the member's consent to be recorded as having met an office for the Federation (R27's consent shape; the member sees every contact that names them);
- **the event**, where the contact is a meeting the Federation organised: the meeting is an **event** on the events module scoped to the programme (B2), and a district meeting hosted by a chapter club is that event with a **host club role** — the club's officers see the event, its attendance and its count on their statement of the Federation's work in the club (club plan §7, §10), never the office's data, never the contact row (*design choice*; the club plan §7.1 already names "a district meeting is an event role" for Congressional Outreach);
- **the stance** the tracker records per office: not carried. A stance is an opinion about a person's office, it goes stale with each Congress and each staffer, and the committee's own 2020 minutes say not to write off any office. The outcome of each contact is the record; a view that sums outcomes per office is a count the committee reads, not a label the platform stores (*design choice*).

**What never enters.** The tracker's free-text columns; any note on an office-holder or staffer as a person; a staffer's personal (non-office) channel; a legislator's voting record or scorecard (those are the partners' published tools, linked from the position, never stored); anything about a member's contact with an office that the member did not consent to record.

### 2.3 The outreach register — the tracker replaced

The spreadsheet is replaced by a **register view over §2.2's contacts**: one row per office, computed, showing the last contact, the open ask, the outcome of the last contact under each active position, the member lead where one is assigned, and the next follow-up. It is the Government Affairs Committee's workspace page (CW2) and nothing else: no export, no print, no copy to a spreadsheet (the platform's export refusal, Breeze Parity §2; the club plan §9.3).

**The member lead.** The tracker gives each office a member who "owns" the relationship. On the platform the member lead is a **roster assignment from the volunteer interests note (P7 §4's assignment)**: the committee's chair, as the seat that owns the programme's scope, asks a member who has opted in to an *advocacy* interest term (a term P14 asks P7's vocabulary to carry under Q-83, with no *touches minors* flag) to take an office, the member says yes on their own record, and the assignment is a dated row on the contact register with the office and the position, ended when the member or the chair ends it. A member lead sees the contacts for their office and nothing of other offices; the committee's seats see all. A member lead's follow-up to an office is a contact row the lead records (§2.2) — the follow-up object of the club plan is *about a person on the roster* and is not used for offices (*design choice*: the two words should not collide).

**First load.** The 2022–2023 tracker is evidence for the office rows and the dated contacts with their outcomes, typed by the committee as contacts under a *historical* flag; the staffer columns are not loaded (Congresses and staff have turned over since; *Q-70*); the free-text columns are not loaded. The spreadsheet stays on the drive as the archive (D66).

### 2.4 The alert — members asked to act

An **alert** is the committee's ask to members under an adopted and approved position: the one-pager (a drive link, D66, or the message body), the asks, how to find one's own representative (a link to the public lookup), and the offer of support (a committee member or expert to join a meeting; the office's channel to request it). It is a **message through the communications desk** — class *programme invitation* (the member is invited to act in a programme, and may decline the class), to the segment the committee names (all consenting members; a state or district filter where the directory's city and state under display consent allow it — the register entry's "advocacy is organised by district, so it relies on the consented member directory"), consent-filtered at build time with the withheld named to the sender (S1, R27, the `communications` workflow). The alert is linked to the position; the platform records that it was sent and to how many (a count under the floor, S7), and **nothing about which member acted on it** unless the member records their own contact (§2.5).

Whether an alert is *programme invitation* or *marketing* is a reading of the `communications` classes this note makes (*design choice*; the same question P12 §2.9 raised for alumni newsletters, *Q-120*). A non-member contact record (D60; P4) with a consent for advocacy news may be in the segment (the `communications` workflow already names "advocacy alerts … to non-member contacts" as a could-share); a contact who applied to the Day of Action and gave consent at the door is one such row (P12 §2.2).

### 2.5 Members' own advocacy

A member's own letter, call or meeting with their own representative is **the member's act, not the Federation's**. The record is silent on whether the Federation invites it, templates it, records it or counts it. Today the alert asks for it and offers help, and the committee learns of it only if the member tells the lead. This note raises the question and designs the smallest honest shape:

- the alert (§2.4) may carry a **template letter** the committee writes, as a drive link or a message attachment, under the programme-invitation class; the member edits and sends it themselves, outside the platform; the platform sends nothing to an office on a member's behalf (*design choice*: the Federation's name is the position's; a member's letter in their own name is theirs);
- a member may **record their own contact** (§2.2) on their own record — the office, the date, the kind, the outcome — with a dated consent that the committee may see it against the position; the committee sees it as a contact; the member may withdraw the consent and the row closes, never deleted (R27's pattern). Nothing is inferred from an alert's send; a member who does not record is not shown as "did not act";
- counts of members' recorded contacts per position and per state are the committee's report figure (§2.7), under the floor.

*Q-133* asks whether the Federation wants to invite members' own advocacy at all under a programme invitation, whether a template under the Federation's position is the Federation's speech or the member's, and whether a member's recorded contact is lobbying activity of the Federation for §2.6's record. Until answered: alerts may carry a template (it is a message, which the record allows), the member's own contact row is **built but shown to the committee only with the member's consent**, and no member's contact is counted in §2.6.

### 2.6 The entity rule, and the lobbying-cost record

**D65 applied.** Every cost of Government Affairs, Congressional Outreach, the Day of Action and the Federation's statements posts to **AFRP's General Fund** on the funds ledger: the committee's budget line (P1 §3.6: the Government Affairs Committee's line is the General Fund's, D65), the Day of Action's programme line (R38's shape; D55), the hotel block and venue costs, reimbursements (P12 §2.6), a distribution service for a statement (P6 §2). The ledger **refuses by name** any advocacy line whose holding entity is ARFECF or ARFHSN: the refusal names D65 and the programme. A reimbursement row, a supplier agreement, a budget line or a statement cost that selects an affiliate entity does not save. There is no override (the Hub's non-overridable list; Delivery Status slice 4b). A gift to the Federation designated for advocacy is AFRP's receipt, non-deductible as every AFRP gift is (the archive's own note: 501(c)(4) contributions are not deductible), and carries no deductibility language (P9 §6's receipt rule by entity).

**The lobbying record — a register the CPA reads, not a rule the platform applies.** D65's condition is the Legal Advisor's confirmation of the 501(c)(4) limits. Two outside rules make the *record* matter, and neither is the platform's to decide:

- The IRS says a 501(c)(4) "may further its exempt purposes through lobbying as its primary activity without jeopardizing its exempt status", that campaign intervention must not be its primary activity and may be taxed under section 527(f), and that an organisation that lobbies must "either provide notice to its members regarding the percentage of dues paid that are applicable to lobbying activities or pay a proxy tax" (IRS, *Social welfare organizations*). The notice-or-proxy-tax rule is IRC 6033(e); the proxy tax is reported on Form 990-T (IRS, *Proxy tax*). The Schedule C instructions carry the exceptions: substantially all dues nondeductible (the 90 per cent test, with a small-dues threshold), or in-house lobbying of $2,000 or less with no political expenditures (IRS, *Instructions for Schedule C (Form 990)*, Part III). **Whether any exception applies to the Federation is the CPA's and the Legal Advisor's call** (P6 *Q-81*); what the platform owes them is the figure the question turns on.
- The Lobbying Disclosure Act defines a lobbyist as a person who makes more than one lobbying contact and whose lobbying activities are 20 per cent or more of their time for the client in a quarter, and exempts an organisation with in-house lobbyists whose lobbying expenses do not exceed an indexed quarterly threshold; an organisation with no employee meeting the lobbyist definition does not register (House Clerk, *Lobbying Disclosure Act Guidance*). Whether a federation whose contacts are made by volunteers and by a part-time staff coordinator ever meets the definition is **not concluded here**; the guidance is cited so the Legal Advisor can answer it from the record the platform keeps (*Q-134*).

So the platform keeps a **lobbying activity register** — labelled on screen as "for the 6033(e) and LDA questions; the Legal Advisor and the CPA say what applies" — with one row per cost and one row per hours entry:

- **cost rows**: every General Fund posting tagged to a position of subject *legislation* or to a contact with an office under such a position (a statement's distribution, a trip to Washington, the Day of Action's event costs where the day's meetings carry a legislative ask, a one-pager's printing), read from the ledger, with the position and the kind; a cost under a position of kind *programme* (a cultural briefing; a thank-you event) is shown in a separate total; the committee's chair may re-tag a posting with a dated reason;
- **hours rows**: time spent by paid staff on contacts and positions of subject *legislation*, typed by the staff member or the Executive Director per month, as hours against a position, with no content (the LDA's 20 per cent test is about staff time; volunteers' time is **not** recorded, because they are not employees and because the platform records nothing about a member it was not asked to — *design choice*; *Q-134* asks whether the Legal Advisor wants volunteer time counted at all);
- the register totals per tax year and per quarter, by subject, and the share of total General Fund expenditure, computed and labelled "computed; not a determination"; the CPA reads it when preparing the return and the dues notice if one is needed; the Legal Advisor reads it for D65's confirmation.

Nothing in this register changes what a member owes or what a screen refuses. It is the record D65's condition needs in order to be confirmed, and the workflow's open item ("how lobbying activity is recorded for tax reporting is not stated") answered as a register, not a rule.

## 3. The Day of Action

The Day of Action is **one cycle of P12's selection engine** (P12 §2.1) running into **one event** (P12 §2.7) under the advocacy programme; nothing in P12 is restated here. This section names where the Day of Action plugs in and what is particular to it.

**The cycle.** Opened by the programme's deciding body — the Day of Action has no vetting sub-body; the register entry names a programme director and the President with the Government Affairs Committee, and afrp.org a Day of Action Committee; the platform seats whichever the committee names on CW1 with "practice" as the appointing text (P12 §2.1). The entity is AFRP's General Fund, read from the register (D55, D65), so money lines raise. The calendar is the programme overview's (preparation in August, applications from 1 September, review in October, two trainings, the event in mid-November — *committee practice, August 2026*), held as the cycle's dates. The form version is the committee's questions (interest, involvement, experience with officials, views on the political situation and on what the government should do, what the applicant hopes to learn, speaker preferences — *the 2024 and 2025 application structure*). The answers on political views are applicant-chosen answers shown to the deciding body only, under the retention clock, never copied anywhere (P12 §2.2's rule, written for exactly this form; under P12 §2.2's reading of D61, Q-118).

**Eligibility.** P12 §2.3's two Day of Action rows stand as written: adults; first-time attendees aged 21 to 35 who are members have costs covered and everyone else attends at their own expense (afrp.org); first-timer computed from the alumni record (D61) and attested for years not loaded. Who may apply, whether a non-member may, whether the age band gates the place or only the costs, what "first-time" counts (the May partner session included or not), and who selects by what criteria are **P12's *Q-115***, carried and not re-asked. **Money.** The cost rule rows (P12 §2.6): the hotel block and part of first-timers' travel (afrp.org; the reimbursement files), with **no amounts in this note**. The club statement line *Day of Action travel* (club plan §5.2; D57): AFRP → first-time attendee, raised on acceptance, recorded against the participant's club **for the count only, no club money** — the club's seat (if any) and the clubs page see "attendees from this club" and no amount. Reimbursement rows post to AFRP's General Fund programme line; **an affiliate entity on any Day of Action row is refused by name (D65)** — P12 §2.6 already says so for this programme and SEL4 builds it. The hotel room-block contract is a *supplier* agreement on P11's agreements register, signed for AFRP under 11.1.1's Executive Committee and Legal Advisor approval, with the legal review as a dated row (the 2026 practice).

**The event.** The three days in Washington as an event with sub-events (the arrival dinner and orientation, the panel and press-club lunch, the training afternoon with mock meetings, the day of congressional meetings, departure — *the 2026 programme grid*), the two trainings as earlier sub-events, the accepted participants as the roster, attendance taken (B2). **Each congressional meeting on the day is a contact row (§2.2)** under the Day of Action's position (the year's asks, which the 2024 prep sheet shows as a written list — *practice*), with the office, the date, the asks and the coded outcome, recorded by the programme director or by the participants' delegation lead; this is what the files do not hold today and what §2.7 reports. ## 5. Rules

1. Every advocacy activity and every public statement is AFRP General Fund activity; the ledger refuses by name any Government Affairs, Congressional Outreach, Day of Action or statement cost whose entity is ARFECF or ARFHSN (**D65**; D55; the refusal has no override — Delivery Status slice 4b).
2. The Government Affairs Committee is constituted by the paragraph under **By-Law 8.15** ("Governmental Affairs Committee"), its chair and members appointed by the Board; its seats are CW1 rows with the Board's dated act (the paragraph under 8.15; P1 §2.2); every member a member in good standing (**8.1.2**).
3. A position is adopted by the committee's dated act and approved by a seat the Board names; the approver row has no value until the Board sets it and nothing issues under an unapproved position (*design choice following P6 rule 2*; **7.9.3**'s "official approved"; *Q-80* carried).
4. Statements and letters under a position are P6's and P3's objects and keep P6's rules; the *subject* field is inherited from the position; *candidate* is refused as a subject on a position (*design choice*; *Q-132*).
5. A contact with an office records the office, the date, the ask and a coded outcome; it never holds a free-text note about a person, a stance, a scorecard or an office-holder's personal data (*design choice*, extending Breeze Parity §4 r2 and **R27**'s principle to offices).
6. An office is an institution on the registry; a staffer, if held at all, is a professional contact at that institution on P6 §3's shape, never a member record, never in a member audience, never exported (**D60**'s shape; P6 rule 5; *Q-70*; D60 is written for the Arabic term and its use for an office contact is a reading, raised as one question for governance with P4's Q-70).
7. A meeting the Federation organises is an event (B2); a district meeting at a chapter club is that event with a host club role, and the club sees the event and its counts and never the contact (*design choice*; club plan §7.1).
8. The outreach register is a computed view with no export, no print and no copy (the platform's export refusal, Breeze Parity §2; club plan §9.3).
9. A member lead is a P7 roster assignment from an *advocacy* interest, ended never deleted, scoped to their office (P7 §4's assignment; **R27**).
10. An alert is a programme-invitation-class message, consent-filtered, with the withheld named to the sender and counts under the floor (**S1**, **R27**, **S7**; the `communications` workflow); which member acted on it is never inferred.
11. A member's own contact with an office is recorded only by the member, on their own record, and shown to the committee only under the member's dated consent, withdrawable, closed never deleted (**R27**; *design choice*; *Q-133*).
12. The platform sends nothing to a public office in a member's name; a template is the member's to edit and send (*design choice*; *Q-133*).
13. The Day of Action runs as one P12 cycle and one event; its eligibility rows are P12 §2.3's; past participants are alumni and past applications never enter (**D61**); its travel line is the club statement's *Day of Action travel* type, count only, no club money (**D57**; club plan §5.2); its hotel contract is a supplier agreement approved under **11.1.1**.
14. The lobbying activity register records costs and paid-staff hours against positions of subject *legislation*, totals them per tax year and quarter, and labels every figure "computed; not a determination"; the CPA and the Legal Advisor say what applies under IRC 6033(e) and the LDA (*design choice*; D65's condition; P6 *Q-81*; *Q-134*).
15. Volunteers' time is not recorded (*design choice*; *Q-134*).
16. Advocacy lives in AFRP's scope only; no affiliate store holds a row of it (**D65**; **R39**).
17. The drive holds the committee's papers, the letters' texts and the spreadsheet tracker as archive; the platform holds the rows and the links (**D66**).
18. Every refusal names its rule.
