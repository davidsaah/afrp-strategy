## 3. The award

### 3.1 The rotation and the forfeiture reading

**What the text says and does not.** 10.1.1 gives a rotation "between these three regions", a three-year bar on returning to a region, and one forfeiture sentence: "If a region **elects not to host** a Convention during its turn in the rotation, it will forfeit the right until the next rotation." It does not say who determines whose turn it is, what happens when a region fields no bid at all or only bids that fail the fifty-for-five test, or what "until the next rotation" means when two regions forfeit in the same cycle. The 2026 Assembly acted on the Legal Advisor's reading that a region with **no qualified bid** has, in effect, elected not to host, so the turn passed to the next region. That is a reasonable reading and it is a reading: "elects not to host" can also be read as requiring a region's clubs to decline, which a region with no qualifying club cannot do. Under R40 the platform holds both and decides neither (**Q-240**).

**What P8 already builds.** HB1 shows each bid's region under 10.1.1 and the three-year check; HB2 shows forfeiture and leaves the Assembly to decide what follows. This note adds the object HB2 needs to show it honestly:

- **The rotation turn.** For each Convention year a row on the events register: the region whose turn it is (as a register row the office sets from the text, never computed from a club's ARFECF region; P8 §2), the last three host regions, the bids received for that year with their eligibility results from HB1, and a **turn state**: *bids qualified*, *no qualified bid*, *declined by the region* (a dated statement from the region's clubs, if any is made), *forfeited* (only by the Assembly's dated act), *passed to region X* (only by the same act). The platform never moves a turn by itself.
- **The reading cited.** When the Assembly acts on a reading of 10.1.1 — as in 2026 — the act records the reading's source (the Legal Advisor's written opinion, a drive link, D66) beside the clause, labelled *reading*, never *rule*. A later year's turn row shows that an earlier reading exists and does not apply it automatically.
- **The vote.** 9.1.1(2) requires delegate voting for "the Convention city, where a contest exists"; where one qualified bid stands, 9.1.2 allows voice or raised hands. HB2's floor motion therefore takes its **mode from the count of qualified bids**: two or more → a weighted delegate vote on the floor as built; one → a voice or hands vote recorded with its outcome; none → no motion, the turn state *no qualified bid* and whatever the Assembly decides on the record. Only Regular Members in good standing vote (10.1.1), so the floor's roll is the certified roll (§6).
- **What the Assembly may not do on the floor.** The 2026 advice that the Assembly could not vote a different city than a qualified bid is the Legal Advisor's; the platform records it as the reading attached to that year's act. Placing a city "in the rotation without a competing bid" (the 2029 path) is either an amendment of 10.1.1 under 16.1.1 or something else; that is Q-223, and until it is answered the platform offers only the text's path: a bid in writing by the Mid-Year, tested under HB1, decided by the Assembly.
- **The hosts through 2030.** The recorded hosts are award records with their source (the Assembly's minutes, where on file) and the turn state the office can attest. Whether the schedule fits 10.1.1's three-year bar is Q-89; the rows show what the text would say and change nothing.

### 3.2 A fourth hosting model: the Federation as host, abroad

*Since 4 October 2026 (D99 review), marked unoperated in the manner of P23 §9.7:* the Federation has never hosted a Convention abroad, and the checklist this section follows is at "hold / feasibility only". The template below, its gate rows, advisory threshold, review clocks, six-outcome decision and delegation row are **designed; not built until Q-223 is answered.** Nothing below is deleted; the slice that would build it (CV2's part for this template) waits on Q-223.

P13 §7 offers three templates (host city, hybrid, Federation as central host) and the Mid-Year. The 2026 checklist describes a fourth: **a Convention outside the United States, hosted by the Federation itself if need be, gated by a requirements checklist with stop conditions.** It is not adopted (status "hold / feasibility only"; Q-223), so the platform offers it as a **template with every gate's threshold blank**, exactly as P13 offers the other three, and adopts none of its content.

**The template's rows** (on the agreement row of P13 §2, class CONV68, with the host recorded as *the Federation* and, where a local body takes part, that body as a party under P11 §3):

- **Gate rows.** One row per gate group — Board, legal and financial approval; security assessment; counsel; insurance (event, evacuation, cancellation, per-attendee); cancellable contracts; government liaison and advisory monitoring; entry and attendee requirements; transport and access; venue, security and medical readiness; final readiness — each with an owner seat, *evidence required* (a drive link and a filed date, D66), a state (*not started*, *in progress*, *met*, *not met*) and the date last reviewed. P13 §2.8's evidence pattern, extended.
- **The advisory threshold.** A typed field holding the government travel-advisory level at or above which the Convention may not go ahead, and the current level with its date, entered by the owner seat from the State Department's published advisory (four levels, from "exercise normal caution" to "do not travel"; Sources). The checklist says the threshold is one "no Board vote may waive". Whether the Board adopts that — a self-binding rule the platform would then enforce by refusing a GO while the current level is at or above the threshold — is part of Q-223. Until it is adopted the field is blank and the platform shows the level beside every decision and refuses nothing.
- **The review clocks.** Readiness reviews at the intervals the checklist sets, as dated items on the owner's queue; an overdue review is shown to the Board, never assumed passed.
- **The decision.** A dated act with one of six outcomes — GO, GO WITH CONDITIONS (each condition a row with an owner and a clock), HOLD, RELOCATE, POSTPONE, CANCEL — the authorising body or seat, and the next review date. The written stop authority the checklist asks the Board to give is a **delegation row**: which seat may HOLD or CANCEL between Board meetings, by the Board's dated act; blank means only the Board's own act decides.
- **What the decision does downstream.** RELOCATE opens a new agreement version (P13 §2.9) for the new place; POSTPONE moves the event dates and re-runs D7's disclosure gate and the refund terms; CANCEL closes registration and opens the refund run under the refund terms published before opening (§5.3). Dues paid with registration are membership dues for the year (5.1, 5.2's "separate from, and in addition to"); the platform records them as dues and the cancellation's refund run does not touch them. Whether a member may ask for dues back when the Convention is cancelled is not written (Q-244, which this note extends to cover it); registration refunds follow the published terms; a deficit reads from the agreement (Q-129). What happens to the **Annual Meeting** — the elections, the budget, the amendments — when the Convention it is "held during" (10.1.1) does not happen is not in the text (**Q-244**); the platform shows the meeting as *not held* and holds every process that waits on it.
- **Publication gates for a Convention abroad.** Registration may not open until the refund terms are published on the ticket page (the checklist; alongside D7's gate) and the written risk disclosure is attached to the registration, acknowledged by each adult registrant as a dated consent row, and by the person acting for a minor under R6. These are the checklist's own terms; they apply only to an event started from this template, and only once Q-223 adopts them. Until then the template carries them as rows labelled *checklist* and the platform shows them unmet.
- **The hedge.** A large Mid-Year in a Convention city in the same year is an ordinary Mid-Year under P8 and 10.2.1 (which limits the Mid-Year to "the USA"); the platform needs nothing new for it.

Whether only an affiliated club may bid and whether the Federation may host with no bidding club is Q-91; which models a bid may propose is Q-94; this template joins them under Q-223.

### 3.3 The Mid-Year

P8 owns the Mid-Year's bids, its decision and its meeting; P13 its agreement. The 2020–21 guideline, now on file, has the Executive Committee review applications received by February of the year before and announce the selection at the general meeting, club presidents present their case at the previous Mid-Year, and a sharp fee rise is approved in writing by the Executive Committee; 2026–27 practice had the Board choose after quotes, the Federation sign the hotel contract and the Executive Committee set the fee (Q-92, which the research round extended). Nothing here changes P8's design: the decision row names the body that made it. Two points belong to operations and are built in this note's slices: the Mid-Year's **membership gate** (§5.2, Q-225) and the **fee approval** — a fee that the guideline would call a sharp rise is a decision item on the Executive Committee's queue (CW4) with its written approval as a dated act, labelled *guideline*, offered and not required until Q-92 is answered. The Mid-Year's money that does not reach the General Fund (its revenue line is at zero; a separate Mid-Year account was reported by the Federation's treasurer in earlier years) is Q-92's sponsorship-and-account part, carried.

## 4. The host agreement

P13 is the design; HB3 and HA1–HA6 are its slices. This note adds nothing to the agreement's terms except three items the 2026 close showed, each of which P13's own rules already govern:

## 5. Registration and the desk

### 5.1 The product set, and the unified registration

The worksheet (P13 §2.4) already lets a host define a base registration "with or without banquet" and sub-events whose inclusion is *mandatory*, *optional* or *mandatory until full*. The 2027 decision — a unified registration including the banquet, decided by the Board in May 2026 — is that worksheet with the banquet sub-event **mandatory**. The platform builds it so, with three consequences it states on the event:

- **The banquet seat is still inventory** (R26). A unified registration allocates a banquet seat from the banquet sub-event's capacity; when the hall is full the registration product is sold out too, unless the event's inclusion rule is *mandatory until full*, in which case later registrations carry no seat and the ticket page says so before payment. Which rule applies is the host's and the office's choice on the worksheet; the platform offers both and picks neither.
- **Banquet-only tickets** (the guideline: at the host's discretion, for the Mid-Year) are a separate product only where the worksheet defines one.
- **Who set the fee.** The registration fee is a product price set by a dated act: the Board's (the template: fees set by the Board at the Mid-Year; 2027: the Board), the Executive Committee's (the Mid-Year in practice), or the body the agreement names. The act's number is on the product; a price with no act behind it cannot publish (*design choice*, from P13 §2.2's "set by the Board's dated decision item").

A ticketed event is non-deductible entirely and a gift beyond the ticket is receipted separately by its entity (D9). The Federation is a 501(c)(4); its registration fees are not charitable gifts in any case, and a gift beyond the ticket to an ARFECF or ARFHSN purpose is receipted by that entity through D8's catalogue.

### 5.2 The membership gate

5.2 says payment of dues "may be a condition to registration"; the template makes it one; the register's board-confirmation item keeps it absolute for attendees (with exhibitors and press as non-attendee credentials). At least one 2026 Mid-Year registrant was not asked; whether the rule reaches the Mid-Year and every sub-event is Q-225. The platform does not decide that by default. It makes the gate a **parameter of each event, with its source**: *membership required for every registrant who qualifies (5.2; template)*, or *not required (the body's dated act)*. A Convention event's parameter is pre-set from 5.2 and the template and labelled so. **A Mid-Year or a stand-alone sub-event cannot publish until its parameter is set with a source** (*design choice*: a publication gate, D7's pattern, so that the 2026 failure cannot happen silently and no default is chosen). When the gate is on, every registrant in a household registration who qualifies for membership is checked against standing at checkout; one who is not in standing is offered dues in the same payment (dues and registration as separate lines, never one product: 5.2's "separate from, and in addition to"); one who does not qualify for membership pays the 5.2 fee or donation where the agreement states one, waivable only by the President's dated act (*template*).

### 5.3 The per-registration Relief Fund amount

**What the records say.** The template keeps a per-registration amount for the Relief Fund **out of the proceeds** — it is part of the Federation's take, not an addition to the registrant's price; it has been deposited after each settlement as one line; whether it continues after 2025 is the Board's (Q-126); the fund's holding entity is not stated (Q-109), and the books point to ARFECF. *Since 3 Oct 2026:* D83 names AFRP as the holding entity; the books' ARFECF booking is a reconciliation item (the books follow D83, or the Boards revisit), and any receipt's deductibility text waits on the CPA (D9).

**What the platform does.**

- The amount is a **term of the agreement** (P13 §2.2, "per-registration fund amounts"): an amount per registrant, the registrant classes it reaches, and the fund. Without the term — or until Q-126 is answered for Conventions after 2025 — the platform computes nothing and the statement prints *not stated*.
- **Under the 2022 template it is an allocation of the Federation's money, not a gift by the registrant**; whether later agreements keep that shape is Q-243. The registrant receives no gift receipt for it in either case until Q-243 and Q-109 are answered (D9: the ticket is non-deductible). Whether a future agreement instead adds it to the price as a visible donation, and whether the ticket page shows it, is not written (**Q-243**); a donation added to the price would be a gift beyond the ticket under D9, receipted by the fund's entity, which is Q-109.
- **Accrual and posting.** Each paid registration in a class the term reaches accrues the amount on the convention class as a payable to the fund; the count is frozen at the close (R43's pattern) and the statement's line 3 (P13 §4) posts it as **one dated line**, as practice does. Because the fund has no named entity, the line posts to the liability "the entity to be named" with no deductibility claim and the Relief Fund posts nothing (P9-J01; CG5); when Q-109 is answered the liability transfers as one dated act. *(Since D83, 3 Oct 2026, the entity is AFRP: the line posts to AFRP's Relief Fund.)* The Educational Fund Treasurer's report of the fund then shows the Convention line from the ledger (the `relief-disbursement` workflow; Q-174).
- **The burn-rate view.** The General Fund Treasurer's multi-year projection is a report, not a rule: the platform can show the per-registration line by Convention year for the fund's history; it does not project future Conventions' amounts, because each is a term of an agreement not yet signed.

### 5.4 The desk and the run

- **Check-in** at the Federation's desk. Check-in staffing is an event parameter (practice 2026: Federation staff at the desk, the committee's September 2026 decision; Q-130 asks whether the committee's guidance is adopted), with host volunteers on a **time-boxed desk grant** for the event's dates (the convention workflow's credentials pattern): find a registration, mark present, print a badge, issue a wristband for access-controlled sub-events. Badges and QR codes are **produced by the platform from the registration**, so the host needs no guest-list export to print them (§8).
- **Walk-ins.** A walk-in registers at the desk through the same door as anyone else: membership gate, dues, registration, payment; the desk cannot register anyone outside the gate. A walk-in fee is a product only where the worksheet defines one (the committee raised it as possible). On-site registrations are counted on the history row (§11).
- **Deadlines and exceptions.** Registration and seating deadlines are product date rules, refused by name after the date, with "no exceptions, Board members included" applied where the agreement or the event states it (P13 §2.4; the committee's rule). Ticket transfers are an event parameter (practice 2026: through the office only), each recorded as a dated act on the registration.
- **Non-attendee credentials.** Exhibitors, vendors (§7.3), press and contracted staff hold credentials of their own class, not registrations, so the membership rule stays absolute for attendees (board-confirmation item 1).
- **The room block.** The hotel contract is an obligation row with evidence (P13 §2.8), signed by whichever side the agreement says — the host in the template, the Federation for the 2027 Mid-Year in practice, and then a Federation agreement under 11.1.1. The platform holds the booking link and, if the host reports it, the block's pickup as a dated count; it does not book rooms.

## 6. Delegates and the vote

The engine exists. The live floor carries exact-fraction weights, freezes extraction when voting opens on a motion (S4) and runs on a frozen roll (R12); the elections console runs digital ballots with inclusion receipts. P5 §3.8 designs the certification that produces the roll (SC4) and §3.9 the mailed Deputy President ballot (SC5). What the Convention element adds is the **bridge** from certification to the floor, at the desk.

1. **The franchise and the roll** — P5 §3.8 as designed: the 30 April snapshot (5.3, 8.12.5) with D1 D; the Membership Committee's per-community lists by 10 May (8.12.6); the Selection Committee's certification (9.3.5) freezing the roll, with the community cap (9.1.4).
2. **The delegation.** The text gives each club or community "the right to send … a delegation" (9.1.3) and is silent on how the delegates are chosen. P18 §6.4 designs the selection as an act of the club's officers; the Legal Advisor's description to the Board in May 2026 has the community's certified members "vote on delegates". The record holds the first and practice describes the second, and neither is in the text. The platform therefore records each delegation as **a dated act of the club with its method stated** — *officers' selection*, *members' vote*, or *other, as the club's own by-laws provide* — and the date and evidence (minutes, a drive link). It refuses nothing on the method and computes the weights the same way whichever method was used. How a community's delegate vote is held and recorded is Q-37, carried; the conflict with P18 §6.4 is raised with it (§13).
3. **Presence.** 9.1.3's weights count delegates "sent to the Convention", and an independent voter is a certified member "attending". The desk's check-in (§5.4) marks a delegate or member **present** for the floor; only a present delegate carries a weight and only a present certified member may extract. The weight is certified members divided by present delegates, computed and never typed (P18 §6.4; Electronic Voting §7). When weights fix is D5, reopened; the floor's S4 freeze is as built.
4. **Quorum.** 10.1.5's twenty-five paid members is the Annual Meeting's quorum and the platform shows the count present against it. A delegate quorum is not written (Q-37); the platform computes none.
5. **What runs on the floor.** Contested elective offices and the Convention city (9.1.1(2)); amendments (9.1.1(3); 16.1.1's two-thirds delegate vote, with the sixty-day submission and forty-five-day distribution shown as clocks on the amendment register); any issue on which fifteen per cent or more of the club delegation chairpersons polled ask for delegate voting (9.1.1(1), a poll the presiding officer records). Everything else by voice or hands (9.1.2), recorded as an outcome. The Deputy President's ballots are opened on election day under 9.2.3 (SC5).
6. **The restatement.** Every step here rests on the 2024 text. The 2026 restatement (Q-33) would drop weighted delegates and make Convention votes advisory; MASTER-PLAN §1d item 2b gates the Convention element's governance work on the Board's outcome, and this note's CV4 carries the same gate.

### 7.3 Packages, booths and the ad book as products

- **A sponsorship package** is a product on the event, or on a programme's sub-event inside it (the Endowment's golf outing at the Mid-Year), naming **the entity that receives and receipts it** (D9, D55) and the purpose it is allocated to (D8's catalogue). A package benefiting an ARFECF purpose is receipted by ARFECF (D9, D8's catalogue); whether AFRP collects it as ARFECF's agent is Q-232; practice (2026) routes it through ARFECF's processor profile. A package for the Convention itself is AFRP's under D1 G (D55's test, the register entry). Which entity receipts a package sold through the Federation's portal when it benefits an affiliate's fund is Q-92, carried.
- **Entries that come with a package** — the 2026 practice of registering sponsors' golfers or guests "at no charge" so that refunds do not arise — are **complimentary registrations linked to the sponsoring payment**: each is a registration at zero with the package's payment as its source, counted as a registration, passing the membership gate like any other, and never a second sale of the same seat.
- **A vendor booth** is a product sold to a vendor (an exchange, never a gift), with the vendor holding a non-attendee credential (§5.4). Booth money taken through the portal is Federation-held under the convention class and settled to the host under the agreement (P13 §3); booth money the host takes off-platform is the host's and appears only in its final accounting (P13 §4's footnote).
- **The ad book** is P13 §2.7: one per family and to larger advertisers; no solicitation outside the host's community; no solicitation at the Convention for unapproved causes.
- **A national partnership** offered to the Federation (the hotel group's approach) is a Federation agreement on CG2 (kind *supplier* or *sponsor*), approved under 11.1.1, never a host's.

### 7.4 What the tax law asks, and the record has not answered

Corporate sponsorship of an exempt organisation is excluded from unrelated business income only as a **qualified sponsorship payment**: a payment for which the sponsor receives no "substantial return benefit" beyond the use or acknowledgement of its name or logo; **advertising** — a message that promotes the sponsor with comparative language, prices, endorsements or inducements — is a substantial return benefit (IRC 513(i) and its regulations; IRS guidance in Sources). A paid page in the ad book and a booth are exchanges; a lanyard bearing a logo may be an acknowledgement; a package with entries, a table and an advertisement mixes all three. Separately, a 501(c)(4) above a gross-receipts threshold must state in each fundraising solicitation that contributions to it are not deductible as charitable contributions (IRC 6113). Whether the Federation's Convention sponsorship packages, ad-book pages and booths are treated as sponsorship or advertising, which part of a package is a gift receipted by an affiliate, and whether the Federation's Convention solicitations must carry the 6113 statement, are for the outside accountants and the Legal Advisor (**Q-242**). Until answered, the platform carries a **kind** on each sponsorship product (*acknowledgement only*, *advertising*, *mixed*, *not stated*) entered by the office, prints the kind on the statement, and claims nothing.

The 2012 sheet shows that direction was once ordinary; the platform's design makes the Federation collect, so it should be rare, and it is the same object.
