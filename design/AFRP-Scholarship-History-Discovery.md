# Finding forty years of scholarship recipients
### A discovery playbook

**Date:** August 2026
**For:** whoever is going to go looking
**Mode:** design and planning. No buildout.

---

## 0. Start here — the archive you already have

**Hathihe Ramallah is almost certainly your recipient list.**

If the magazine has been announcing and congratulating scholarship recipients
for decades — and magazines like it nearly always do — then **the back issues
are a dated, systematic, near-complete record of who received what and when.**
Better than any binder, because:

- It was published on a schedule, so gaps are visible rather than silent.
- It was proofread, so names are more likely to be spelled as the family spells them.
- It is dated by construction — the issue *is* the year.
- Copies exist in more than one place. Members keep them. Clubs keep them.
- It often carries the *named* scholarship, the institution, and sometimes a photograph.

Before commissioning any search of filing cabinets, **have someone spend a day
with a stack of back issues.** If recipients appear there, the discovery problem
is largely solved and the remaining work is transcription rather than
archaeology.

This is also why the magazine work and this work belong together: digitising the
archive produces the alumni list as a by-product.

---

## 1. The urgency, stated plainly

**The people who remember the 1980s will not be available indefinitely.**

Paper survives badly but predictably. Institutional memory disappears without
warning — a past committee chair moves, downsizes, or dies, and forty years of
context goes with them. Nobody knows which box mattered until it is gone.

Of everything in this project, **this is the only part with a deadline nobody
set.** The platform build can slip a year with no permanent loss. A former
scholarship chair clearing out a garage cannot.

**Recommendation: do the inventory (§3, Phase 1) in the next few months, before
and independent of any software.** It is phone calls and a spreadsheet. It does
not wait on the build, the bylaws, or the CPA.

---

## 2. Where the records actually are

Ordered by likely yield per hour spent.

| # | Source | What it gives | Likely completeness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Magazine back issues** | Name, year, often scholarship and institution | **High** — systematic and dated |
| 2 | Committee files from recent chairs | Applications, decisions, amounts | High for recent years, patchy before |
| 3 | **Convention programs** | Awards presented, often with photographs | High for award years |
| 4 | Annual reports | Totals, sometimes names | Medium |
| 5 | **Treasurer records — cheque registers, bank statements** | Payee, amount, **exact date** | High, and it is the source that *proves* the money |
| 6 | Named-fund donor files | Recipients of specific named awards, often in correspondence | Medium, high value |
| 7 | Past chairs' personal files | Everything, unpredictably | **Most fragile — chase first** |
| 8 | Thank-you letters from recipients | Names, institutions, and warmth worth quoting | Scattered |
| 9 | Local club newsletters | Recipients from that club | Varies by club |
| 10 | Institution records | Rarely accessible; do not rely on it | Low |

**Cross-referencing pays.** The magazine gives you the name; the cheque register
gives you the amount and the exact date; the convention program gives you a
photograph. Any one of the three is enough to start, and all three together give
you a record nobody can dispute.

---

## 3. A four-phase plan

### Phase 1 — Inventory (2–3 weeks, no software)

Find out what exists before deciding what to do with it.

1. List every person who has chaired or served on the scholarship committee as
   far back as anyone can name.
2. **Phone them.** Ask one question: *"Do you still have anything from your time
   on the committee, and where is it?"*
3. Ask the same of past treasurers and magazine editors.
4. Check the national office, and ask the two or three largest clubs.
5. Record what you find in one spreadsheet: source, years covered, format,
   condition, who holds it, and how fragile it is.

**Output:** you know the shape of the problem. Often this alone reveals that one
person has the whole thing in a box.

### Phase 2 — Capture the highest-yield source (4–8 weeks)

Usually the magazine.

- Scan or photograph the relevant pages. **Photographs on a phone are fine** —
  legibility matters, archival quality does not.
- Transcribe into the tier-1 template (§4).
- Work backwards from the present, not forwards from 1952. Recent years are
  easier, more complete, and more useful for recruiting — and you may find you
  have enough before reaching the hard years.

### Phase 3 — Corroborate (2–4 weeks, overlapping)

- Reconcile names against treasurer records to fix amounts and dates.
- Fill gaps from convention programs and committee files.
- Mark uncertain entries as uncertain rather than guessing. **An honest
  "amount unknown" is worth more than a plausible wrong number.**

### Phase 4 — Import, match and trace (software)

- Import tier 1. Match against current members.
- Work the review queue with someone who knows the families.
- Trace the unreachable through the family tree.

Phases 1–3 need no software at all. **Do not wait for the build to start them.**

## 4. The tier-1 template

Everything you need to begin, and nothing you do not:

| Column | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `full_name` | **Yes** | As printed. Do not normalise or correct spelling |
| `award_year` | **Yes** | The academic or award year |
| `amount` | No | Leave blank if unknown. **Do not estimate** |
| `scholarship_name` | No | Named award, if stated |
| `institution` | No | |
| `field_of_study` | No | |
| `club` | No | Their local club, if known |
| `source` | **Yes** | e.g. `Hathihe Ramallah 1998 no.3 p.14` |
| `notes` | No | Anything odd, uncertain, or worth a human's attention |

**Two rules that matter more than they look:**

1. **Record the name exactly as printed**, including spellings you believe are
   wrong. A 1998 typesetter's "Yakoub" is evidence about how that family's name
   was rendered, and the matcher is built to handle transliteration variants. If
   you silently "fix" it, you destroy the evidence *and* the match.
2. **Always record the source.** Three years from now, "where did this come
   from?" must have an answer. It is also what lets two conflicting records be
   adjudicated rather than argued about.

---

## 5. What "good enough" looks like

**Name plus year is enough to start a conversation.** That is the whole bar.

| If you have | You can |
|---|---|
| Name + year | Reconnect, and reconstruct the alumni list |
| \+ amount | Report total program impact |
| \+ scholarship name | Tell a donor family who their award funded |
| \+ institution and field | Segment, and match mentors to current scholars |
| \+ graduation | Measure outcomes |

Applications, scores and committee minutes add **almost nothing** to recruiting
and are usually unrecoverable. Skip them unless someone is writing a history of
the program.

---

## 6. Two things worth doing while you are in there

**Record an oral history.** While you are phoning past chairs anyway, ask them
to talk for twenty minutes about the program in their era — who it helped, what
it cost, what they remember. Some of it will be publishable in the magazine, and
all of it is the context no spreadsheet carries. **This is the part that is
genuinely irreplaceable**, and the call is already happening.

**Note the named scholarships as you go.** Which existed, when, funded by whom,
and for what purpose. Named-fund criteria are frequently undocumented, and an
undocumented criterion is the most common reason a named award sits unawarded
for years. You may recover a donor's intent that nobody currently knows.

---

## 7. Rough effort

Assuming a few hundred recipients across roughly forty years:

| Phase | Effort | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Inventory | 2–3 weeks part-time | One organised volunteer with a phone |
| 2 · Capture | 4–8 weeks part-time | One or two volunteers, or a paid transcriber |
| 3 · Corroborate | 2–4 weeks | Someone with treasurer-record access |
| 4 · Import and match | 2–3 weeks | Staff, once the software exists |

**Phase 1 is the one that cannot wait.** Everything after it can be scheduled.

---

## 8. What I need back to size the software

1. **Roughly how many recipients** — dozens, hundreds, or thousands? Changes
   whether match review is an afternoon or a project.
2. **How far back** does the program go, and how far back does the paper go?
3. **Does the magazine name recipients?** If yes, §0 is the plan and the rest
   is a fallback.
4. **Do treasurer records survive**, and how far back?
5. **Are there named scholarships whose criteria nobody can now state?**
6. **Who will do Phase 1?** It needs one organised person, not a committee.

---

*Design and planning only. Nothing here requires software to begin — and the
inventory should not wait for any.*
