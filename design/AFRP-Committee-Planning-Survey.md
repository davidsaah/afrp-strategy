# The annual committee planning survey

### The Deputy President's yearly questions to every committee, as a cycle whose answers land on the programme register and the budget, not in a folder

**Design note P2 · 1 October 2026 · for the Hub's `committees` (P1), `programs` and `reporting` modules**

**The source.** In March 2025 the Deputy President, as chair of the Strategic Plan Committee (By-Law 7.3.3), sent every committee and programme chair a one-page form, "The Value of Strategic Planning" (SWOT25, the crosswalk's row A7). Its letter frames the exercise as a SWOT analysis and a review of purpose, three-to-five-year goals, strengths and weaknesses, budget and staffing; the form itself asks eight things: the committee or programme's name; the chair and co-chairs; its purpose and what it is trying to achieve; which of its business processes have been successful and which not; whether it is structured to achieve its purpose; short-term goals; long-term goals; an estimated annual budget. It carries no SWOT grid. Six responses are on the drive (the Exchange Mission, Camp Ramallah, Project Hope, Women to Women, the Selection Committee, and the form); their substance is already on the programme register as `strategy_needs` lines marked "2025 SWOT". FED2's committees and programme report (P1 §4.2) asks three of the same questions (mission, short-term goals, long-term goals) six-monthly, and FED2 §H asks each chair for an annual plan with goals, timelines and outcomes. The Strategic Planning Committee restarted on 10 September 2026 asking for a plan built on outcomes and data (SP26; D42, D44), and its draft plan is the text of record until adopted (D44); the eight goals are published for it to confirm (D52).

**Precedence (D41).** The 7.3.3 duty is the by-law; the survey is the committee's instrument, not a rule; the form's questions are the committee's to change. Nothing here is a decision. The crosswalk's "yearly" is this note's reading of a survey run once, in 2025 (*Q-59*).

---

## 1. What the record already holds

**Built.** The programme register with its eight headings per plan (master plan slice P1: what it is in its own words, who it is for, the operating year, the five rungs, the organiser, money, the handoffs, what is missing), each section sourced and revisable by the committee with attribution, a revised section surviving the re-seed; the typed budget line per programme (Program Architecture §3; crosswalk H5); the funds ledger. Not built: any committee object (P1), any survey object, and the budget as a document the platform holds — the General Assembly's approved budget is a drive document today (D55 cites it as the evidence for holding entities).

**Decided.** D42 (the four branches as the planning frame), D44 (the committee's draft is canonical until adopted), D52 (the goals for the committee to confirm), D55 (a programme's entity is the fund that budgets it), D66 (the drive is the archive), D67 (the year-one baseline).

**Historically.** One survey, March 2025, six responses on file; the Federation's committee overview sheets of August 2026 ask mission, key outcomes and a budget per committee and were mostly blank when read; the September 2026 Selection Committee meeting agreed that chairs get defined budgets and report to the Board, and that the programme director reminds chairs monthly before the Mid-Year. The Finance Committee proposes the annual budget to the Board at the General Assembly (8.7.1) on a June-to-May fiscal year (8.7.2).

**The shape of the problem.** The 2025 answers were read once by one person and filed. The same questions are asked again by FED2's report, by the overview sheets and by the programme plan, each in its own place. The survey should be asked once a year in one place, and each answer should land where it is used: purpose and goals on the programme plan, the budget estimate on the budget line, the process and structure answers on the committee's workspace and in the Strategic Plan Committee's roll-up.

---

## 2. The cycle

A survey cycle is an object the Strategic Plan Committee opens once a year for every active body (P1 §2.1): a year, an open date, a due date, the form version, and one response per body. The Deputy President opens it (7.3.3 gives the chair the committee; the 2025 letter was the Deputy President's), the staff liaison's reminders run as follow-ups to each chair (P1 §3.5), and the cycle closes on its due date with the responses it has. When the cycle should open is the committee's to say; the Finance Committee's budget cycle (8.7.1, for the General Assembly in July) wants the budget estimates before it drafts, and the Mid-Year in January is where chairs already report (10.2.1 D), so the window between the two is the natural one — offered, not set (*Q-59*).

## 3. The form

The 2025 form's eight fields, as fields rather than a page of prose, each with where its answer lands:

| Field (2025 form) | Type | Lands on |
|---|---|---|
| Committee or programme | read from the body | — |
| Chair and co-chairs | read from the seats (P1 §2.2) | — |
| Purpose; what it is trying to achieve | text | the programme plan's "what it is in its own words"; for a non-programme committee, the body's mission on its workspace (P1 §3.1) |
| Processes that have been successful; not successful | two text fields | the committee's workspace; the roll-up |
| Is the committee structured to achieve its purpose | yes / no / partly, with text | the roll-up; a *no* opens a decision item on the body's own queue (P1 §3.4) |
| Short-term goals | a list, each with a target year | the programme plan's "what is missing" and the plan's goals (FED2 §H); the committee's workspace |
| Long-term goals (three to five years) | a list, each with a target year | the same; the roll-up under the goal and branch the chair tags |
| Estimated annual budget | an amount with a note, in the body's entity's currency of account | a *proposed* line against the body's budget line for the coming year, labelled proposed, never the budget |

Two additions the record asks for and the 2025 form did not hold, offered for the committee to accept or strike: a SWOT grid (four short lists), since the letter named it and the form omitted it (*Q-60*); and, for a programme committee, the branch and the goal (D42, D52) each long-term goal serves, so the roll-up reads by branch. The form is versioned; a cycle names its version; the committee edits the next version, not a past one.

**Landing, not copying.** An answer that lands on the programme plan is a revision of that section attributed to the chair and the cycle ("from the 2027 survey"), in the mechanism slice P1 already gives committees; the plan's previous text stays in the section's history. The budget estimate lands as a proposed line the Finance Committee sees beside the current line and the ledger's actuals; adopting it is the budget's act (8.7.1), outside the platform until the budget is a platform document, and the proposed line never becomes the line by itself. The goals land as the body's plan items with target years, which FED2's six-monthly report (P1 §4.2) then reads back, so the survey is written once and reported from twice.

**Prefill.** The next cycle opens with the body's last answers prefilled and the differences shown when submitted, so the Strategic Plan Committee reads what changed, not what was retyped.

## 4. Who sees what

The chair and co-chairs of a body see and answer their own response, and see their last one. The members of the body see the submitted response. The Strategic Plan Committee's seats and the Deputy President see every response and the roll-up; the President ex officio (7.2.1). The Executive Director sees the responses of AFRP's appointed committees (7.9.2; P1 §3's default; P16 Q-56). The Finance Committee sees every budget estimate and nothing else of the responses. The Board sees the roll-up when the Strategic Plan Committee refers it (P1 §3.4), and the General Assembly sees what the committee's plan says. An affiliate's bodies answer under their own entity's lens, and their responses reach the roll-up only if the affiliate's Board says so (*Q-61*). A member sees nothing of a response; the public site shows a programme's purpose and goals only as the programme register already shows them. No response names a member or holds anything about one; it is about the body.

## 5. The roll-up and the committee's draft plan

The Strategic Plan Committee's view is every body's answers for the cycle in one table — purpose, the two goal lists by target year, the structure answer, the budget estimate against the current line — sorted by branch (D42) and by the goal each long-term goal serves (D52), with the previous cycle's answer beside each. From it the committee writes its draft plan, which is the Google Doc of record until adopted (D44); the platform does not write the plan. What it does: the roll-up is frozen at the cycle's close as a dated record (the packet's pattern, P1 §4.3) and filed on the drive (D66); the goals tagged to a branch are the committee's evidence for the branch's priorities; the budget estimates summed by entity are the first figure the Finance Committee sees; and the "not structured to achieve its purpose" answers are the committee's own list of bodies to look at — crosswalk A5's "evaluate programmes; review or retire" has its input here, and the retirement itself is the Board's act on the programme's lifecycle (Program Architecture §6).

## 6. Rules

1. The cycle is opened by the Strategic Plan Committee's chair, the Deputy President (7.3.3); the platform opens none by itself (*Q-59* for the date).
2. The form's questions are the committee's; the platform holds the 2025 eight as version 1 and the additions as a proposal (*Q-60*).
3. An answer lands on the programme plan as an attributed revision, never as an overwrite without history (slice P1's mechanism).
4. A budget estimate is a proposed line and never the budget; the budget is the Finance Committee's proposal and the General Assembly's adoption (8.7.1).
5. A response is about a body and holds nothing about a person (*design choice*, for the reason P1 §3.3 gives: the platform keeps decisions and tasks, not opinions).
6. A chair reads their own body's responses; the Strategic Plan Committee reads all; the Finance Committee reads the estimates only (*design choice*, Four-Lens §6).
7. The roll-up is frozen at close and filed on the drive (D66); the draft plan is the committee's document (D44).
8. Every refusal names its rule.
