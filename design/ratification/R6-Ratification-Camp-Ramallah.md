## Why this is in front of you

The camp screens — parent application, selection console, roster, camperships —
are being built now. They need a stated rule for **who may act for a child**, and
until this week the platform had none: it was inferring one, which is not

Four authority paths are settled: **a parent by household membership, a named
guardian, either of two parents when separated, and a per-event delegate
identified at event setup** — the chaperone list for a trip or a session.

## Question 1 — The line between supervising a child and releasing one

The document splits these deliberately, because they are not the same authority:

**Supervising is designated by AFRP.** When you set up a session or a trip, you
name the adults responsible — staff, chaperones, screened volunteers. Parents are
**told** who those adults are, on the registration surface, before the event.
They do not approve the list. That is right: supervision is AFRP discharging its
own duty of care, not a parent lending out their authority.

**Releasing a child is authorised only by a parent or guardian.** Only a parent,
a named guardian, or either separated parent may put a person on the list of
those permitted to collect a child. **Camp staff can never add anyone to that
list — including themselves.**

**The decision:** confirm that split, and confirm the hard edge on the second
half. The specific case to think about is the awkward one: a session is ending,
an adult presents themselves saying a parent sent them, and the parent cannot be
reached. Under the document as drafted the child is **not released**, and the
refusal is not a judgement about that adult — it is that the list is the only
authority and the list does not name them.

## Question 2 — A court-appointed guardian

Recorded in the platform's open-question register, verbatim, and routed to you:

> Camp pickup authority reads the household and delegation model. **A
> court-appointed guardian is neither parent nor delegate.**

**Proposed answer:** a court-appointed guardian is simply a **named guardian** —
the second of the four paths — established by the appointing order instead of by
the household record. Recorded by staff against the child, carrying a reference
to the order and the date it was recorded, never asserted by the person
themselves. On that reading the question is a **recording procedure**, not a
missing rule, and no new authority path is needed.

## Question 3 — Does camp need powers the document does not have?

"Acting for a child" is broken into seven named powers: see, enrol, transact,
consent, supervise, release, and emergency medical. **Is anything camp does
missing from that list?** Medication administration and off-site activity consent
are the two most likely gaps — if either is a distinct decision at camp rather
than part of the general consent given at registration, it should be its own
power rather than something staff infer.
