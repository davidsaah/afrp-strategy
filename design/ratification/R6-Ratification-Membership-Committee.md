## Why this is in front of you

The membership platform has never had a stated rule for **who may act for a
minor** — enrol them in a programme, pay for them, answer a consent question on
their behalf. The rule was being *inferred* from how the software would have to
behave, which is the platform legislating for families. Three separate pieces of
work were stopped rather than build on an inferred rule about children.

A document now states it. Four authority paths are settled: **a parent by
household membership, a named guardian, either of two parents when separated,
and a per-event delegate identified at event setup.** Two questions in it are
yours.

---

## Question 1 — Is the power set right?

The document treats "act for" not as one permission but as **seven named
powers**, so that a parent and a chaperone on a day trip are not handed the same
thing:

| Power | Permits |
|---|---|
| see | View the minor's record |
| enrol | Register them for an event or programme |
| transact | Pay dues, fees or camperships for them |
| consent | Give or withdraw consent held for the minor |
| supervise | On-site responsibility during a named event |
| release | Collect the minor, or authorise their departure |
| emergency | Authorise emergency treatment when no parent is reachable |

A parent, a named guardian and either separated parent hold **all seven**. A
per-event delegate holds **supervise only**, plus release or emergency where a
parent has specifically named them for that event.

**The decision:** is that division right, and in particular — should a per-event
delegate ever be able to *see* a child's record or *enrol* them in something
else? The document says no. That is the conservative reading and it is the one
the platform will enforce unless you say otherwise.

## Question 2 — A minor who belongs to two households

Recorded in the platform's open-question register, verbatim:

> After a remarriage a minor belongs to two households with different clubs,
> different directory choices and different pickup authority. **By-Law 4.3.1
> says nothing about minors in two households.**

The question has three separable halves, and two of them are answered without
needing you:

- **Pickup** does not follow from household membership at all. It runs through a
  list a parent or guardian writes. Both households' parents can write to it.
- **Directory visibility** resolves to the more protective of the two households'
  settings wherever they differ. Minors are already excluded from every
  directory listing, so this is a narrow question of household-level display and
  the protective reading is the safe one.

**The half that is yours: which club is the minor attached to?** That carries
dues and, eventually, franchise consequences, and By-Law 4.3.1 is silent.

The platform's current behaviour, pending your decision, is to **hold both
attachments and flag the person as an open question** rather than pick one. That
is deliberate — a silent pick here would quietly move a child's dues and
eventual vote from one club to another.

## What happens either way

Work on the household screens and the camp registration surfaces is proceeding
now, building against the document above. Everything derived from an unratified
answer is **labelled a proposal on the screen itself**, in the same way the
platform already labels the 48 provisional by-law readings. Your decision either
removes a label or changes a rule; it does not stall the build.
