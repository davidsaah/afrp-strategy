## What this is, and what it is not

The membership platform has never held a stated rule for who may act for a minor.
It was inferring one from how the software needed to behave — which is exactly
the fault this project refuses elsewhere — so three pieces of work were stopped
rather than built on it. The attached document now states the rule.

**Four authority paths are decided by AFRP** and are not in question here: a
parent by household membership, a named guardian, either of two parents when
separated, and a per-event delegate identified at event setup.

**The provisions below were drafted on general youth-protection practice by
people who are not lawyers.** They are marked provisional in the document itself
and are in front of you before anything is built on them, not after. Three of
them touch custody, consent, or the platform's posture when documents conflict.

---

## 1 · Supervision disclosed, release consented (§3)

The document treats two things differently that an operational reading would
merge:

- **Supervision** is designated by AFRP at event setup — staff, chaperones,
  screened volunteers — and **disclosed** to the household. Parents are told, not
  asked. The reasoning: supervision is the organisation discharging its own duty
  of care, not the exercise of delegated parental authority.
- **Release** — collecting a child, or authorising their departure — may be
  granted **only by a parent or guardian**. An event organiser cannot add anyone
  to a release list, including themselves.

**For your review:** is disclosure sufficient for supervision, or should the
platform capture an affirmative parental acknowledgement of the supervising
adults? And is parental designation the correct and sufficient instrument for
release, given AFRP operates across many states?

---

## 2 · Emergency medical authorisation (§3)

Drafted as an **explicit authorisation given by a parent or guardian at
registration**, event-scoped, permitting authorisation of treatment only when no
parent or guardian is reachable. It is deliberately *not* inherited from
supervising a child, and *not* held by a per-event delegate unless a parent named
them for it.

**For your review:** is a registration-time authorisation the right instrument,
and what must it actually say to be relied on? This is the provision most likely
to be drafted wrongly by non-lawyers, and the one most likely to be tested.

---

## 3 · Restrictions, and the platform's posture when documents conflict (§4)

The document makes a recorded restriction — a custody order, protective order or
equivalent — **override every authority path, including "either of two
parents."** A restriction is:

- entered by staff against the specific adult-and-child pair, never inferred from
  the family tree, the household record, or a member's own assertion;
- carrying a reference to the document relied on, who recorded it, and when;
- dated, liftable only by the same route, with the history preserved.

**The platform does not adjudicate custody.** It records what it was shown and
enforces that. Where documents conflict, are ambiguous, or are described but not
produced, the platform **refuses the action, says a restriction is on file that
it cannot resolve, and routes the question to you** — and never states the
substance of the restriction to the person being refused.

**For your review, and this is the question I would most like answered:** is
"refuse and route" the correct posture? It is the conservative choice and it is
consistent with how this platform handles every other silence in the record. But
refusing a parent access to their own child's record is not a neutral act, and
there may be a duty to act rather than to pause. If there is a category of case
where the platform must do something other than refuse, it needs naming now.

Related: is staff-entered, document-referenced recording an adequate standard for
establishing a restriction, or does AFRP need a defined intake — who may record
one, what must be seen, what is retained?

## 4 · A court-appointed guardian (§6)

The platform's own open-question register carries this, routed jointly to you and
Camp Ramallah:

> Camp pickup authority reads the household and delegation model. **A
> court-appointed guardian is neither parent nor delegate.**

**Proposed:** a court-appointed guardian is the **named guardian** path,
established by the appointing order rather than by household membership —
recorded, document-referenced, dated. On that reading the question resolves to a
recording procedure and no new authority path is needed.

**For your review:** does that hold, and does the order's own scope need to be
carried onto the record? A guardianship may be limited in ways the platform's
seven powers do not currently express.

## Two things worth knowing about how this will be used

**Nothing waits on you to start.** Work on the household and camp surfaces is
proceeding against the document now, with everything derived from an unratified
answer **labelled a proposal on the screen itself**. Your review changes rules
and removes labels; it does not unblock a stalled project. But the sooner it
arrives, the less is built on a reading you would have written differently.

**Every exercise of authority is logged** — who acted, for whom, under which
path, when, and which power. If that record needs a defined retention period, or
needs to be shaped for a particular kind of later inquiry, that is worth telling
us before it is built rather than after.
