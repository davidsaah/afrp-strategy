**In the text, as a summary of what §2–§5 cite.** Article XVII constitutes a nine-member Selection Committee that screens, vets and approves every individual recommended for a vacant Federation seat (17.1.1), is slated by the Executive Committee from club presidents' recommendations and chosen by the Board (17.1.2, 17.1.3), serves staggered terms with a six-year cap (17.1.3), advertises and recruits, reviews every application, ranks the top three, interviews them for every position except the Deputy President, and for the Deputy President recommends the slate put to the membership (17.1.4). Article IX gives the same committee the certification of members' voting eligibility (9.3.5, 9.1.4), the receipt of Deputy President candidacies (9.3.4(3)), the mailing and numbering of the ballot (9.2.1, 9.2.2), the investigation of campaign complaints (9.4.2) and the disqualification of dues paid by another in exchange for a vote (9.4.3). Two committees the 2024 text no longer constitutes keep duties in it: the Election Committee chooses the CPA or agency (9.2.1), receives the sealed envelopes with the Credentials chair (9.2.3) and directs the draw from a bag (9.2.4); the Credentials Committee certifies the Deputy President candidates by 31 March (9.3.4(3)) (Divergence item 1). A separate five-member "Board Selection Committee", appointed by the President, exists only to recommend an Executive Director (7.9.4, 7.9.7, 7.9.8) (Divergence item 2).

**In FED2 §G (the committee's 2021 proposal, status open).** The consolidation of Nominations, Credentials and Elections into one committee, with its rationale ("volunteers screening volunteers"); the standardised process — announcements through the Magazine and the Federation's social media stating that applicants must be members in good standing; a letter of intent for the Deputy President (the proposal's dates are the pre-2021 ones, 1 November and 31 December, which the 2021 amendment moved to 28 February and 31 March); candidate profiles (résumé, CV or bio) collected by the chair and distributed to the members; the full committee comparing profiles to define the top three; an eligibility test of good standing *and consistent attendance at Mid-Year Meetings and Conventions in the preceding three years*; interviews and a debrief; for the Deputy President, a recommended slate. The chair's and a member's job descriptions: the chair reports to the President and the Board, attends the Mid-Year and the Convention and presents the committee's reports at both the business meeting and the General Assembly; confidentiality is the chair's duty for the whole committee; three-year terms. Two things in §G are not in Article XVII: the attendance criterion, and the chair's reports to the General Assembly. Two things in Article XVII are not in §G: the Deputy President among the seats vetted (17.1.1 adds it), and "guarantee deadline compliance of submissions" (17.1.4).

**Historically (the intakes, role-level).** Until 2021 three committees ran the cycle: a Nominations Committee proposed candidates for the offices elected on the floor, a Credentials Committee of seven including the General Treasurer certified the voters from the Membership Committee's per-community lists (the old 8.11, deleted in 2024) and certified the Deputy President candidates, and an Election Committee chose the CPA and ran the count; credential counts were read into Board minutes each spring, and the Deputy President was elected by mailed ballot returned to a CPA. The 2021 amendment package consolidated the three into the Selection Committee and moved the Deputy President calendar; the Selection Committee presented its first appointments to the Board in the 2021–22 year; from then the practice is that the President appoints chairs and the Selection Committee vets. A 2022 General Assembly amendment that would have given the Board power over the Selection Committee's members' terms and its chair was not passed. In August 2026 the committee's own overview sheet listed four active members, one as chairman, against the nine of 17.1.2; its September 2026 strategy meeting moved selection from "top of mind" nominations to a structured process — club presidents as the front door for nominations, the chair attending the presidents' meetings at least twice a year to state the needs and set deadlines, CVs collected, eligibility verified, openings announced on social media rather than in print — with the guidance documents and the calendar of mandatory Mid-Year and Convention business still pending. For voters, the office prepares one "voting members" workbook per club each June from the member database (one per club, carrying dates of birth), and no document says who certifies them, what the cut-off is or what the quorum rule is (Q-37). In 2025 the General Assembly nominated three members for Board at-large seats; in 2026 the convention's election for two at-large seats was not completed and the Board extended the sitting members' terms by a year by acclamation. Election results by name are in minutes; voter rolls and ballots have never been in the folders read.

## 2. The committee as a body

The Selection Committee is a `Body` on P1's register (CW1): a standing committee (Const. Art. VIII §1; 8.2.1), constituted by Article XVII, reporting to the Board (FED2 §G: "reports to the President and Board of Directors"), with the President ex officio (7.2.1). Nothing in this section is new; it fixes what CW1 seeds for this body so that the workflow has an actor.

**Seats.** Nine (17.1.2). The seat's authority is a Board act on an Executive Committee slate (17.1.2); the slate is drawn from one or two recommendations per club president, submitted by the President to the Executive Committee (17.1.3). So a Selection Committee seat is created in CW1 only with three dated acts behind it: the club president's recommendation (a row the club president files, P18's officers form being the model), the Executive Committee's slate (a decision item on its queue, CW4), the Board's choice (a Board decision, CW4). A seat typed without them is shown "no appointing act recorded", as DIR2 shows the four designated Board seats. **Design choice:** the platform does not refuse a seat the office types from the overview sheet for the first load, because the committee has existed since 2021 and the acts are in minutes on the drive; it labels the seat with the drive link (D66) and leaves the act fields empty.

**Terms.** The first cohort's one-, two- and three-year terms, then three years (17.1.3, read with FED2 §G's "term: three years"); reappointment to a second term; "service may not exceed six years (two consecutive terms)" (17.1.3). CW1 computes the end date from the seat's start and refuses a third consecutive term by name (P1 §2.2, rule 4). The text does not say whether a partial term counts toward the six years, or whether a break resets the count; the platform counts consecutive service in whole and part and shows the arithmetic (*Q-73*).

**Composition tests.** "No more than two individuals from the same city" (17.1.2): a refusal at slate time, on the city of the member's record, named by clause. Diversity "in geography, age, gender, family/clan and experience level" (17.1.2) is a standard for the slate, not a test the platform scores: the slate page shows the city count against the two-per-city rule and, if the Executive Committee asks, the age bands P1 §2.6 already offers; it shows nothing on gender or clan, which the member record does not hold for this purpose and the platform will not infer (R40; D30's principle that no decision reads the tree). *Design choice, labelled.*

**Eligibility of the committee's own members.** 8.1.2: good standing in the Federation during the prior year and in the chapter club if any, kept throughout the term; 13.2 for an elected officer and 8.1.2 for a committee member mean a lapse is a lapse of the seat, which CW1 already shows as a follow-up and does not perform (P1 §3.5). FED2 §G adds the chair's three years of progressively responsible involvement and a member's good standing; both labelled *FED2 §G*.

**The chair.** Appointed by the President (8.1.3, "except as otherwise specified"; Article XVII specifies nothing about the chair). The 2022 General Assembly declined to move that to the Board. The chair's reports to the Board at the Mid-Year and the Convention (FED2 §G) are CW3 report rows on this body.

**Not this body.** The five-member Board Selection Committee of 7.9.4 and 7.9.8 is a separate Board committee on P1's register ("Board committee", P1 §2.1), created for one hire; nothing in this note applies to it, and the Hub must not resolve "Selection Committee" in 9.x and 8.12.6 to it (Divergence item 2, provisional reading adopted here as a design choice because 17.1.1's mandate names the seats and the Deputy President and 7.9.4's names the Executive Director).

**The two phantom committees.** The Election Committee and the Credentials Committee have duties and no constitution (Divergence item 1). The platform does not create them. Each duty is a *step* in §3 with its by-law actor named as the text names it, and a seat field "who performed this: ___" that the office fills from the body that did it in that year, labelled "no constituting text". If the President creates either committee under 8.1.1, it becomes a created committee on P1's register and the step's actor resolves to it. *Q-74* asks whether they are to be created, folded into the Selection Committee by amendment, or left.

## 3. The cycle as objects

One object, the **selection cycle**, per Federation year (the 2024 by-laws' elections sit between the Mid-Year and the Convention; FED2 §E counts the Federation year July to July, P1 §3.5). A cycle holds vacancies, calls, applications, eligibility checks, interviews, slates, a certification run, the ballots that derive, and results. Every row is dated, attributed to a seat, and names its rule. The cycle is on the Federation lens under the Selection Committee's workspace (CW2), with the reads §5 gives.

### 3.1 The vacancy

A vacancy is a row CW1 creates when a seat ends (term end, resignation, removal, death, a lapse under 8.1.2 or 13.2 that the body acts on) or when a new seat is constituted, and the list of them is the committee's intake (P1 §5; 17.1.4 "advertise and recruit"). The row carries the body, the role, the authority that fills it and its clause (P1 §2.2's table), the term, whether 17.1.1 reaches it, and the date it falls vacant. The Deputy President's seat is a vacancy every year (7.2.2, 7.3.1: the Deputy President succeeds to the Presidency); the four Members at Large fall vacant two a year (6.5.1); the Executive Committee's elected seats fall vacant each Convention (7.1.2); standing-committee seats fall vacant yearly (8.1.3) unless the body's rule says otherwise (8.5.1's three years; 17.1.3); the Fundraising Committee's one seat per region each year (8.5.1); the three Scholarship Committee seats elected at the Convention on the 2015 policy's schedule (D58).

**Which vacancies 17.1.1 reaches.** The clause lists "standing committee chairs, committee members, Executive Committee members, Deputy President, and Constitution-ratified program chairs per the Constitution". It does not list the Members at Large (6.5.2 has them file their own names), the Historian (6.2.2, Board-elected), the designated Board seats (6.6.1, 6.7.1, 6.8.1, appointed by their bodies), the Scholarship Committee's three elected seats (an ARFECF body, D58), the Legal Advisor or the four treasurers and secretaries as such (they are Executive Committee members under 7.1.1, so reached by that phrase), or created and programme committees (P1's Q-75). The platform marks each vacancy *reached by 17.1.1* or *not reached* from the body's register row, with the reading cited; where the reading is open (Members at Large; created committees; programme committees) the row says "reach not written" and the cycle still runs the vetting steps for it if the committee chooses, recording that it did (*Q-75* carries both the floor question and the reach question, because they are one question: what 17.1.1 gates).

### 3.2 The call for applications

A **call** is a dated announcement for one or more vacancies: the position, the text of the vacancy's eligibility (§3.4, drawn from the clauses, not retyped), the term, the deadline the committee sets (17.1.4 "guarantee deadline compliance of submissions"), the by-law deadline where one exists (28 February for the Deputy President, 9.3.4(3); the Mid-Year for Members at Large, 6.5.2), and the channels. Channels under the record: the Magazine (FED2 §G; 6.5.2 "Hathihe Ramallah Magazine will publicize this in advance" for the Members at Large; 9.5.2 for a candidate's own article), the Federation's own communications (the comms desk under the programme-invitation class, workflow `communications`; the September 2026 practice of social media over print), and the club presidents' meeting (the September 2026 practice: the chair states the needs and the deadline at the Council twice a year — a Council meeting agenda item, CW2). The statement "applicants must be Federation members in good standing" (FED2 §G) is printed on every call from 8.1.2 or 9.3.1 as the vacancy's clause gives it. A call is a comms-desk send with the Selection Committee chair's seat as sender; it goes to members only, never to a contact list, because the eligible are members (8.1.2, 9.3.1).

### 3.3 The application

An **application** is one member's dated submission to one call (or, for the Deputy President, the letter of intent of 9.3.4(3); for a Member at Large, the submission of the name at the Mid-Year of 6.5.2; for any committee seat, the club president's recommendation of the September 2026 practice or the member's own). It holds:

- the applicant as a member record (never a free-typed name; the applicant must be a member to apply, 8.1.2, 9.3.1 — a non-member's approach is a contact, not an application, and is refused with the clause; *since 2 October 2026:* the contact record note reads this as no role, so nothing is kept beyond the refusal (P21 §2, a *design choice*; the contact record itself is DRAFT under Q-70));
- the vacancy and call;
- how it arrived: self, club president's recommendation (with the president's seat and date), the Selection Committee's own recruitment (17.1.4), or "from the floor" (§3.7);
- the applicant's statement (free text the applicant writes about themselves, the only free text in the cycle, under the applicant's own control) and a link to the profile the committee collects (résumé, CV or bio, FED2 §G) held on the drive in the committee's folder under the committee's confidentiality (D66; P1 §3.3's rule that the platform minutes no opinion about a person);
- for the Deputy President: the date of receipt at headquarters and the postmark, because 9.3.4(3) is a postmark rule ("by registered letter or in person"; "any submission postmarked later than February 28th shall not be considered") — the platform records the paper's arrival and does not replace it until the by-law is amended (D4's pattern: the text is the law);
- the applicant's consent to the committee's reading of their record for the eligibility check (§3.4), as a dated consent row (R27);
- states: received, late (refused by name with the clause and date), withdrawn, checked, interviewed, ranked, slated, not slated, nominated from the floor, elected, appointed, closed.

**Who sees it.** The applicant sees their own application and its state, never the ranking. The Selection Committee's seats see the applications to the vacancies in the cycle. The President sees them ex officio (7.2.1) — and, for appointed seats, as the appointing authority (8.1.3). The Board and the Executive Committee see the slate (§3.6), not the pool. Club presidents see the state of their own recommendations and nothing of other clubs'. The Executive Director's 7.9.2 read of appointed committees' day-to-day operations reaches the cycle's clocks and counts, not the applications. Nobody else; no directory, no report, no count by name below the floor (S7).

**Retention, under D61's principle.** D61 keeps programme applications, essays and résumés out of the platform for past cycles and runs future cycles in the platform. Applied here: the application object above is the platform's; the profile document stays on the drive and is linked (D66); when the cycle closes, the application's structured rows are kept as the record of the committee's decision (a seat's "vetted on" date, P1, needs its source), the applicant's statement is deleted on a parameter with no value, and the drive link is kept as a link only — the committee's own retention rule on the drive is outside the platform (D61's second paragraph for the camp forms, the same shape). No application from before the platform is imported (D61). *Q-76* asks the parameter and who deletes on the drive.

**What FED2 §G adds, labelled.** "Consistent attendance at Mid-Year Meetings and Conventions in the preceding three years" is an eligibility criterion in §G and in no by-law. The platform shows attendance from the events module as a fact beside the check (B2 attendance; Convention and Mid-Year registrations) and does not mark it met or not met until a text makes it a criterion (*Q-77*). The same for the chair's "three years of progressively responsible roles".

**What the check refuses.** A criterion read from anything but the member's own record and the registers: no inference from the tree (D30), no reading of a club's Breeze export (C6's rule), no free-text about the applicant. A check on a non-member. An outcome typed by hand: the committee may record a human finding where the table says so, as a dated attributed row, and may not overwrite a computed criterion — if the record is wrong, the record is corrected (a standing correction through the ledger, a seat through CW1) and the check re-runs with both results kept.

### 3.5 Interview and ranking

For every position except the Deputy President, the committee ranks the three top candidates "based on merits" and interviews them with the full committee, then debriefs (17.1.4). The platform carries: the shortlist as a dated ordered list of up to three applications (the number from 17.1.4; fewer if the pool is smaller; a parameter if a Board rule changes it); an interview as a meeting of the body (CW2) scoped to the shortlist, with the applicant as an invitee, attendance taken; the debrief as a decision item on the committee's queue (CW2 §3.4) whose outcome is the slate (§3.6). What the platform does not hold: scores, notes, opinions, the content of the interview — P1 §3.3's rule, and FED2 §G's confidentiality duty on the chair, point the same way; those stay in the committee's drive folder under its confidentiality (D66). The ranking is a fact of the committee's decision, not a computed figure; the platform records it as "ranked 1–3 by the committee on <date>" and no more.

### 3.6 The slate and the recommendation

The **slate** is the committee's dated recommendation for one vacancy or a set: the applications it approves (17.1.1 "approving every individual recommended"), in rank order where the text asks for one, with the eligibility check attached to each. Who receives it depends on the seat's authority, read from P1's table:

- an appointed seat (8.1.3 standing committees; 7.3.3; the Young Leader paragraph; 8.15.1): to the appointing authority — the President, with the Committee on Appointments' consultation recorded where it happened (8.1.3, 8.11.1), or the Board where the text says so; the appointment is the authority's dated act and CW1 creates the seat from it, with the "vetted on" date filled from the slate;
- the Executive Committee's elected seats and the Fundraising Committee's regional seats (7.1.2, 8.5.1): to the General Assembly as the candidates on the ballot the voting module runs at the Convention, with the floor path beside it (§3.7);
- the Deputy President: "the committee shall make its recommendations for the candidate slate to be presented for Election to the membership" (17.1.4) — the certified candidates (9.3.4(3)) become the names on the 9.2 ballot (§3.9);
- the Members at Large: the General Assembly elects by secret ballot (6.5.2) from the names filed at the Mid-Year; whether the committee's slate gates them is the reach question (§3.1, Q-75);
- the Selection Committee itself: its own slate comes from the Executive Committee to the Board (17.1.2); the committee does not vet itself, and the platform refuses to open a cycle in which a sitting member's own seat is in the committee's pool (*design choice*, from 17.1.2's allocation of that slate to another body).

A slate, once made, is frozen as a dated record (R43's pattern); a later change is a new slate with the first kept. The slate is what the Board and the Executive Committee see; the pool is not.

### 3.7 Floor nominations — Divergence item 9, not resolved here

**What the text allows.** 9.3.1 (amended 7 July 2012, unchanged in 2024): a candidate for any elective office except the Deputy President may be "nominated by the Selection Committee or from the floor", and either must be in good standing after the election and throughout the term. 9.3.2: a member "seeking to be nominated and elected" must be present at the Annual Meeting, with the President's exception. 6.5.2: a Member at Large candidate submits their own name at the Mid-Year, and the General Assembly elects by secret ballot. 7.1.2: the Executive Committee's members are elected during the General Assembly. 9.1.1(2): delegate voting is required for elective offices where a contest exists.

**What the 2024 text changed.** Article XVII, new in 2024, makes the committee "responsible for screening, vetting and approving every individual recommended for a vacant Federation seat" (17.1.1) and has it "rank three top candidates" and interview them (17.1.4). It did not amend 9.3.1, 9.3.2, 6.5.2 or 7.1.2, and says nothing about the floor. The 2024 drafters replaced "Credentials" with "Selection" in 9.1.4, 9.2.1, 9.2.2, 9.3.4(3), 9.4.2 and 9.4.3 and left "or from the floor" in 9.3.1 — which reads either as deliberate (the floor path survives beside the slate) or as an omission (17.1.1's "every individual" was meant to close it). The Divergence note's provisional reading is the first; the committee's 2021 proposal ("volunteers screening volunteers", "the vacancies are purely internal") reads toward the second. Both are readings; neither is a rule.

**What the platform does until it is decided.** The voting module's ballot for a contested elective office carries two labelled candidate sources: *slated* (from §3.6, with the eligibility check attached) and *from the floor* (a nomination the presiding officer records on the live floor during the General Assembly, 9.3.1, with the member's standing read at that moment and 9.3.2's presence read from registration). A floor nominee's row shows "not vetted under 17.1.1" and the ballot proceeds; the platform does not refuse the nomination and does not hide the label. If the General Assembly or an amendment decides that the committee's approval is a prerequisite, the floor source is switched off for the seats the decision names, by a register row citing it. If it decides the floor survives, the label stays and nothing else changes. This is the honest state and the only one the record supports (R40). *Q-75*, for the Constitution Committee: a clarifying amendment under 16.1.1, which the Divergence note already flags as high priority, and which belongs in the same package as the Article IX amendments D4 and D5 need.

### 3.8 Certification of members and delegates

Three certifications, in order, each a frozen dated record on the cycle (R12, R43):

1. **The franchise.** A member is eligible to vote for the Deputy President, the Members at Large, the Scholarship Committee's elected seats and the Executive Committee only if their application and dues were received at the office by, or postmarked by, 30 April (5.3, 8.12.5); only dues paid from the applicant's own personal or business funds count (8.12.4; 9.5.5 restates the prohibition on paying another's dues, families excepted; 9.4.3 makes dues paid for a vote grounds to disqualify the member, the votes and the candidate). The platform computes the 30 April snapshot from the ledger with D1 D (standing from collection or postmark, so a club's late remittance does not disenfranchise), carries the own-funds attestation the payment door already takes, and shows each refusal with its clause (the franchise notice LT-R3, P3, is the warning before the date).
2. **The lists.** "Soon thereafter" the Membership Committee prepares a membership list in alphabetical order for each community and furnishes copies to the Selection Committee and the Executive Assistant by 10 May; by the same date the custodian of the applications turns them over to the Selection Committee for verification and gets them back (8.12.6). The platform produces the per-community lists from the 30 April snapshot, dated, for the Membership Committee's chair to release to the Selection Committee's seats on the committee lens — the release is the Membership Committee's act (8.12.6), recorded; the verification is the Selection Committee's act (8.12.6, 9.3.5), recorded per community with any member it removes or adds and the clause for each. The list reaches the Selection Committee, the Executive Assistant (8.12.6) and, with the Executive Committee's approval, other members of the Federation and ARFECF (8.12.7) — the 8.12.7 release is a decision item on the Executive Committee's queue (CW4) and the only path by which the list leaves the two committees; no export otherwise (Directory Formats rule 6). The 2026 practice of a workbook per club carrying dates of birth is replaced: the platform's list carries name, community and certification, never a birth date (R7's reasoning; a minor is not a member of the franchise).
3. **The certified roll and the community caps.** "Membership and voting eligibility must be certified by the Selection Committee" (9.3.5); "the number of votes that may be cast by any community shall be limited to the number of persons whose membership had been certified by the Selection Committee" (9.1.4). The Selection Committee's certification of the verified lists freezes the roll the voting module already freezes at a record date (R12) — the record date becomes the certification date, and the cap per community is the certified count. From the roll each club's delegation is selected by its officers (club plan §4.1 step 6; P20-D3; *since 2 October 2026:* the text is silent on how delegates are chosen (9.1.3), and the Legal Advisor's description to the Board in May 2026 has the community's certified members vote on them, so the Convention operations note (P22 §6, CV4) records each delegation as the club's dated act with its method stated (*officers' selection*, *members' vote*, or *as the club's own by-laws provide*) and refuses none; the method is Q-37), each delegate's weight computed as certified members divided by delegates sent (9.1.3), never typed, and an independent voter's extraction deducted (9.1.3), with D5's open question on when weights fix left open. **Q-37 is answered in part by the text and the rest stays open:** the text says the Selection Committee certifies (9.3.5, 9.1.4) and the Membership Committee lists (8.12.6); the text does not give the certification a deadline, does not say how a member challenges a refusal (the old 8.11.3's "Board decision final" was deleted in 2024, Divergence item 1), and does not say the quorum rule for the delegate vote (10.1.5's twenty-five paid members is the Annual Meeting's quorum; delegate quorum is not written). The platform runs certification as above, treats a challenge as a decision item for the Selection Committee with the Board as the escalation by analogy to 9.4.2 (the Divergence note's provisional reading, labelled, *Q-78*), and computes no delegate quorum.

The merge queue stays a gate on certification (Electronic Voting §2, built): a community's list cannot be certified while a probable duplicate in it is unresolved, and the refusal names the member's cluster to the Selection Committee and the rule to the member.

### 3.9 The ballot — D4's transitional channel

**The Deputy President (9.2).** Within 30 days of receiving the certified list, the Selection Committee sends each certified member a ballot with the candidates' names and a pre-addressed return envelope to a CPA or independent agency chosen by the Election Committee (9.2.1); the member votes for one, seals it in the BALLOT envelope inside the larger envelope, and mails and/or emails it to reach the CPA postmarked no later than 10 June (9.2.2); envelopes and inserts are numbered as the Selection Committee designates and it decides the ballot's details (9.2.2); the sealed envelopes stay with the CPA and are turned over to the Election and Credentials chairpersons on written request before the Convention and opened on election day (9.2.3); the highest count wins irrespective of percentage, a tie by a draw from a bag under the Election Committee (9.2.4); a ballot marked for two is disqualified (9.2.5).

Under D4 the platform runs this in full as the transitional channel and labels it so on every screen. The objects: the ballot run (dated when the list was received; the 30-day clock; the mailing date, which the Divergence note's item 3 shows must be no later than about 17 May for a lawful window before 10 June — the platform warns, with the arithmetic, and does not pick the date); the numbered envelope register (an inclusion status per certified member, not a receipt, R14); the CPA or agency as an institution-registry row with the Election Committee's choice recorded (§2's phantom-committee field); the receipt log the CPA keeps (postmark for paper; for an emailed ballot the Divergence note's item 4 reading — the receipt timestamp, the attachment held sealed — labelled provisional, *Q-79* for the Legal Advisor, since an emailed secret ballot to a CPA is a secrecy question the text did not think about); the written request and hand-over (9.2.3) as dated acts; the opening on election day as a count the certifying officer records with the CPA's attestation (Electronic Voting §5's pattern applied to paper); the tie as "tied; draw under 9.2.4" handed to the chair. The digital ballot the engine already has is offered beside it only when the 9.2 amendment is ratified (D4; the register's amendment package item 1) — until then it is shown as the adopted direction, not as a channel (Delivery Status "Governance posture").

**Other contested offices (9.1.1(2)).** On the live floor as built: delegate voting with the weights of §3.8, secret for officers, the floor source of §3.7 labelled, a tie handed to the chair with no rule (Divergence item 6: 9.2.4's bag is the Deputy President's only; Robert's Rules under 12.1 fills the gap, labelled). **Uncontested offices:** the text says delegate voting is required "where a contest exists"; where none exists the platform offers a voice vote or show of hands (9.1.2) and records the outcome; the 2026 draft's "unopposed candidate is deemed elected" is not the 2024 rule and is not applied.

### 3.10 Results and the seats that derive

A certified result (hash, certifying officer, turnout; never a voter-to-token link) closes the vacancy and creates the seat on CW1 with the result as its authority (P1 §2.2: "an election result from the voting module"), the "vetted on" date from the slate where the candidate was slated, and "from the floor, not vetted" where not. The seats that derive: the Deputy President (and, a year on, the President by 7.2.2 and 7.3.1 without a vote); the Executive Committee's elected members (7.1.2), who are Board members by 7.1.1; the Members at Large (6.5.2), Board members by 6.2.2; the Fundraising Committee's regional seats (8.5.1); the Scholarship Committee's three (D58, on ARFECF's register); appointed seats from the President's or the Board's act on the slate. DIR2 reads the seats from CW1 the next day; the leadership directory shows the successor and the vote the seat carries (Directory Formats §4). The Historian's election by the Board (6.2.2) is a Board poll, not part of this cycle. Results by name are a public fact of the General Assembly (the minutes); the platform publishes them to members on the Federation lens and to nobody signed out (D28).

## 6. Rules

1. The Selection Committee is a nine-seat standing committee slated by the Executive Committee from club presidents' recommendations and chosen by the Board, with no more than two from one city, staggered terms, and six years at most (Const. Art. VIII §1; 8.2.1; 17.1.2; 17.1.3). Its chair is appointed by the President (8.1.3). The five-member Executive Director selection committee is a different body (7.9.4, 7.9.8; Divergence item 2, *design choice*).
2. The committee screens, vets and approves every individual recommended for a vacant Federation seat in the classes 17.1.1 names; the reach beyond those classes is not written (17.1.1; *Q-75*).
3. An applicant is a member; a non-member's approach is refused with the clause (8.1.2, 9.3.1).
4. Every eligibility criterion is computed from the member's own record and the registers, cited by clause, with undetermined routed to the committee; a human finding is a dated attributed row and never overwrites a computed one (R40; the table in §3.4).
5. The Deputy President's letter of intent is a postmark rule the platform records and does not replace (9.3.4(3)); the Board's approval is a poll with a fifteen-day clock (9.3.4(3), 6.2.4, 7.9.10).
6. The committee ranks three and interviews them for every position except the Deputy President; the platform records the ranking as the committee's act and holds no scores, notes or interview content (17.1.4; P1 §3.3; *FED2 §G* confidentiality).
7. A slate is frozen when made; a later change is a new slate (R43's pattern, *design choice*).
8. The committee does not vet its own slate (17.1.2; *design choice*).
9. A contested elective office's ballot carries slated and floor candidates with the source labelled; the floor source is not refused until a text closes it (9.3.1, 9.3.2, 6.5.2, 7.1.2 against 17.1.1; Divergence item 9; *Q-75*).
10. The franchise is the 30 April snapshot on dues from the member's own funds, with standing from collection (5.3, 8.12.4, 8.12.5; D1 D).
11. The Membership Committee lists by community and releases by 10 May; the Selection Committee verifies and certifies; the certified count caps each community's votes; the roll is frozen at certification (8.12.6, 9.3.5, 9.1.4; R12, R43).
12. The list leaves the two committees only on the Executive Committee's approval (8.12.7); it never carries a birth date (R7's reasoning, *design choice*); no export otherwise (Directory Formats rule 6).
13. The merge queue gates certification (Electronic Voting §2; built).
14. Delegates are selected from certified members, weighted by computation, with extraction deducted; when the weight fixes is D5's open question (9.1.3, 9.1.4; S4; D5).
15. The Deputy President's ballot runs as 9.2 writes it, labelled the transitional channel, with the mailing-window arithmetic shown and no date picked (9.2.1–9.2.5; D4; Divergence item 3).
16. A tie in the Deputy President's ballot is a draw from a bag; a tie on the floor is handed to the chair with no rule (9.2.4; Divergence item 6; Electronic Voting §6).
17. A result creates the seat on the committee register with the result as its authority and the slate's date as its vetting (P1 §2.2; this note §3.10).
18. Applications from before the platform never enter; the profile document stays on the drive; the applicant's statement is deleted on a parameter with no value after the cycle closes (D61; D66; *Q-76*).
19. Dues paid by another for a vote disqualify the member, the votes and the candidate on the committee's finding; the platform records the finding and the Board's secret-ballot decision and performs nothing (9.4.2, 9.4.3; 13.1).
20. Every refusal names its rule.

**Corrections the Hub's record should take in the same pass.** `AFRP-Electronic-Voting.md` §1, §2 and §7 cite the pre-2024 numbering: 8.11 (Credentials Committee), 8.11.2 and 8.11.3, 8.15.4, 25 May, 20 June, 28 February as the dues deadline. In the 2024 text the certifier is the Selection Committee (9.3.5, 9.1.4), the lists are due 10 May (8.12.6), the postmark is 10 June (9.2.2), the franchise date is 30 April (5.3, 8.12.5), and the old 8.11 is deleted. The design holds; the citations do not, and a build session reading that note alone would build the wrong calendar. The Rules Register's governance rows should gain 5.3, 8.12.4–8.12.7, 9.1.1–9.1.4, 9.2.1–9.2.5, 9.3.1–9.3.5, 9.4.1–9.4.3 and 17.1.1–17.1.4 where not yet cited; P1 already asks for 17.1.1–17.1.4.
