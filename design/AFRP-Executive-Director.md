## 1. What the record already holds

**In the text.** The Board "may seek and retain" an Executive Director, whose duties, terms and hiring process are the by-laws' to set, and who "shall reside in the Headquarters metro area" (Const. Art. V §3). The Executive Director performs "the duties as set forth within the terms and conditions of employment as defined by the Board" (7.9.1); is an at-will employee who implements Board policy, works with the President and the Executive Committee, oversees programme progress against the Constitution and By-Laws, oversees the day-to-day operations of the appointed committees, works with the Chapter Clubs on Federation projects, and assists the Finance, Membership and Fundraising Committees with corporate, foundation and grant campaigns (7.9.2); has "access to all A.F.R.P. and Chapter Club records", oversees "the maintenance and safeguarding of all records", coordinates "all official approved A.F.R.P. communication that will be disseminated from the A.F.R.P. office", runs the online newsletter, public relations, marketing and media, and "along with the Board of Directors will decide who will represent the A.F.R.P. with respect to any and all media interaction" (7.9.3); is selected, interviewed and recommended by a five-member Board Selection Committee appointed by the President, hired by the Board, with terms developed by that committee and approved by the Board (7.9.4); signs the employment documents on hire (7.9.5); is evaluated quarterly by the President with the Executive Committee, with a salary recommendation to the Board at the Annual Meeting (7.9.6); on a vacancy, the Board first votes on need and on whether the General Fund can pay, and the candidate agrees in writing to work from the Headquarters office (7.9.7); the President appoints the selection committee, which recommends the candidate and the negotiated contract, approved by polling the entire Board (7.9.8); other employees are selected by the Executive Committee and approved by the Board (7.9.9); a poll passes by simple majority of votes cast, abstaining and non-voting members not counted (7.9.10). The Executive Director and the Executive Assistant are non-voting members of the Board (6.2.2) and of the Executive Committee (7.1.1), and are not elected (7.1.2). The Executive Assistant is hired by the Federation on résumé and interview, is funded by the Finance Committee, "maybe combined with the Executive Director position as required" (7.10.1), works with the Executive Director on Board-approved business, oversees daily office operation with the Executive Director, takes the minutes of the Board and the Executive Committee and maintains and safeguards the records (7.10.2), is at-will (7.10.3), evaluated on the same cycle (7.10.5), polls the Board (6.2.4), sits on the Membership Committee (8.12.1), receives the per-community membership lists by 10 May (8.12.6), keeps the membership applications "in a safe place" and releases the list only on the Executive Committee's approval (8.12.7), and receives proposed amendments and forwards them to the Constitution Committee's chair (16.1.1). The Legal Advisor is available to the Executive Director for consultation (7.8.1). The Council of Chapter Club Presidents "shall work along with the Executive Director in implementing any A.F.R.P. Chapter Club or other programs" (8.4.1). The Executive Director chairs the four Fundraising sub-committees (8.5.2–8.5.5). The Federation reimburses both employees' travel and lodging to the Mid-Year (10.2.1 F). The Executive Secretary — an elected officer, not staff — has "charge and custody of" all Federation files and records and takes minutes in the Executive Assistant's absence (7.4.1).

**Historically (the intakes, role-level).** The role has been on Board agendas since at least 2017 and in the by-laws since the 2012 amendments; the Federation ran a search in 2009–10 and introduced an Executive Director at the 2010 Convention; an internal appointment to the role was announced in late 2021 with three goals (membership, fundraising, the new database) and a performance-measures agreement to be made with the selection committee; the archive is inconsistent on whether that appointment carried the title; a budget line for the post was carried for several years and not spent; in 2026 the Board approved a job description and salary range, the post went up in September, a recruitment packet went to the Board and a recruiter was considered, with a target of the start of 2027 and a committee's hope of onboarding before Christmas. The office today is a programme director and a programme coordinator, working in the CRM and the shared drive, with functional mailboxes for the office and for several programme committees. **What the note adds.** None of this is a platform object. The Hub cannot tell an Executive Director from any other staff user, cannot show that a read of a club's names happened for a reason the Board named, and cannot show the Board what its employee did with the access 7.9.3 gives. This note makes the seat, its grants, its audit line, its reports and its succession objects a build session can carry, and leaves to the Board every choice the record leaves to it.

## 2. The seat

### 2.1 One seat on P1's register

The Executive Director is a **seat** on the committee register (CW1): `(person, role, body)` dated, with the authority that filled it. Its bodies are the Board (6.2.2, role *non-voting*) and the Executive Committee (7.1.1, role *non-voting*); its appointing act is the Board's poll under 7.9.8 (§6); its vote property is *no vote*, cited 6.2.2 and 7.1.1, and the leadership directory shows it so (P19 §4; P1 §2.5). The same seat is *staff liaison* (P1 §2.2's role) on every AFRP appointed committee the by-laws give it day-to-day oversight of (7.9.2) and *chair* on the four Fundraising sub-committees (8.5.2–8.5.5), each a seat row on that body with 7.9.2 or 8.5.x as the authority — created when the body is, not typed. The Executive Assistant is a second seat on the same pattern (§4). Nothing else in the Hub names the person: the leadership directory shows the seat with its holder and dates, as it shows every seat, and the clubs page, the committees page and the Board packet name the seat.

**Why a seat and not a role.** The Hub's `national_staff` role is a bundle of permissions with no body, no date, no appointing act and no end. A seat has all four, which is what 7.9.4's at-will employment, 7.9.7's vacancy process and P1's succession pattern need: the grants in §3 are attached to the seat and end with it (§7), and the audit line in §3.2 names the seat, so a future Board can read what the office did under which holder without the platform ever having stored an opinion about anyone.

### 2.2 The grant bundle, and who sets it

Four-Lens §6 makes a grant `(person, role, scope)` and keeps the five sensitive permissions out of every bundle. The Executive Director's seat carries a **grant bundle**: the roles in §3, at the scopes §3 names, as one register row with the authority for each line. Two authorities, and only two, may put a line in the bundle:

- **a by-law duty**, cited by clause — the lines in §3 marked "7.9.2", "7.9.3", "8.4.1", "8.5.x" and so on, which exist the day the seat does; and
- **a Board decision**, as a dated decision item on the Board's queue (P1 §3.4) — the 2026 job description's terms are such a decision (7.9.1: the duties are "as defined by the Board"), and any addition to the bundle later is another.

The President's quarterly evaluation (7.9.6) evaluates performance and does not change the bundle; a line the President wants added goes to the Board as a decision item. The role-granting act itself — who holds `platform_admin` and applies the Board's decision to the seat — is the exercise of `role:grant`, a sensitive permission (Four-Lens §6.2) that the Executive Director's bundle never contains for its own seat (§3.3, rule 5); who holds it today is the project owner, and who should hold it when the office is staffed is a question (*Q-144*).

*Design choice, labelled:* the bundle is shown to the Board in its packet (CW4) once a year and whenever it changes — the seat, each line, its authority and its date — so that "the Executive Director has access to everything" is never true by default and is visibly true only where a text or the Board said so.

### 3.2 The 7.9.3 read of club records — the shape of the Q-44 answer

The question is open and this note does not close it; it gives it a shape the Board and the Legal Advisor can accept or amend, so that the build has one thing to build when they do.

**The text.** "Have access to all A.F.R.P. and Chapter Club records" (7.9.3). The clubs page as built shows the Federation a club as counts, never names (Breeze Parity §3; slice B1). Four-Lens §6.3 (August 2026) let `national_staff` "read every club roster" without export. The later document governs the page, and the by-law wins over both (D41): the Executive Director's access to a club's records exists. What the platform needs is not a switch but a **purpose**.

**The named-purpose read.** A read of a club's names by this seat is an act, not a view. The seat opens it by naming: the club; the purpose, chosen from a list the Board adopts (the by-law duties offer the list — *implementing a Federation project with the club* (7.9.2, 8.4.1); *a membership, fundraising or grant campaign the committee is running with the club* (7.9.2); *safeguarding the records* — a migration readiness check (C6), an incident (P15), a merge the queue raised (the registry); *a Council or Board request*, with the decision item cited; *a request from the club's own president*); the rows it will read (the roster, the officers' contact details on the form, the club's statement lines); and a period, after which the grant lapses by itself. The platform writes the **audit line** before the first row renders (seat, holder, club, purpose, rule "7.9.3", period, date-time, the rows read as a count and, for a single record, its id) and shows the read on the club's own page to the club's president and secretary the same day ("the Federation office read this club's roster on <date> for <purpose>") — *design choice, labelled: the club sees every such read, because 8.4.1 makes the clubs the Executive Director's partners and a read nobody can see is the thing the counts-only rule was built to prevent.* The Council sees, at its meetings, the count of reads per club per period and nothing else; the Board packet carries the same count. A read outside the list, or with no purpose, is refused by name ("7.9.3: a named purpose is required; Q-44") — which is journey P18-J13's guarded state today and its *meets* state the day the Board adopts the list.

**What the read never permits.** Export (bulk export is refused for everyone, P19 rule 6; P15-J07 logs the refusal); a minor's name or details beyond what R7 and R6 allow a Federation reader; a free-text note; any write to the club's objects; reading a second club under the first club's purpose; keeping a copy — the platform shows and does not download. The officers' personal contact details on the form are read under this same act, not freely: the form is filed with the office (3.2) and the office may read it to *run the filing* (mark received, write to the president through the seat channel) without a 7.9.3 act; reading a president's home address to post something to it is the letters register's own operational purpose (P3), audited there.

**Who decides the list.** The AFRP Board, with the Legal Advisor (Q-44's owners), as a decision item on the Board's queue; recorded as a D-number by the governance agent; carried in the Rules Register as a parameter. Until then the refusal stands and the clubs page is counts for this seat as for everyone (Q-44's detail now carries this shape).

### 3.3 What the Executive Director can never do

Listed once, so that no module needs to re-derive it and every refusal can cite this section and its clause:

1. **Vote** — on the Board, the Executive Committee or any body where the seat is staff liaison (6.2.2, 7.1.1); the vote property on the seat is *no vote* and the voting module's eligible sets never include it. A poll the Executive Assistant's seat issues under 6.2.4 is issued, not answered, by that seat (7.9.10 does not count non-voting members).
2. **Adopt a decision** — a decision item on any body's queue is decided by the body; the seat may *refer*, *prepare* and *record what the body decided* (P1 §3.4). The register's D-numbers are the governance agent's and the Board's.
3. **Change standing** — standing is computed from the ledger under D1 and D6; the seat corrects a payment record with evidence and the standing follows. Approving membership (Article IV; the Membership Committee and the Board) and certifying voters (8.12.5–8.12.6; the Selection Committee) are not the seat's.
4. **Delete** — nothing in the platform is deleted by anyone (the registry's rule; P15 §4): a person becomes a closed record; a ledger entry is never deleted; an audit line is never edited. The seat's "safeguarding" duty (7.9.3) is exercised through P15's retention schedule, whose deletion acts are the custodian's on the schedule's attested rows, never ad hoc.
5. **Grant itself anything** — `role:grant` on its own seat is refused structurally; a change to the bundle is a Board decision applied by whoever holds `platform_admin` (*Q-144*).
6. **Sign for the Federation** — contracts are the President's (7.2.1) or the Board's; the Convention agreement is the President's or the Liaison's (P13); cheques the treasurers' (7.5.1, 7.7.1). The seat *coordinates* official communication (7.9.3) and signs a letter only where P3's register seats it as signatory.
7. **Read an affiliate's store** — R39; §3.4.
8. **See under the floor** — S7 applies to the seat as to every reader; the floor exists to stop arithmetic naming a person and the office is not exempt from arithmetic (*design choice*).

## 7. Succession and the end of grants

7.9.4 and 7.10.3 make both positions at-will; 7.9.7 says what the Board does when the position is vacant. On the platform:

- **A seat ends on a date**, recorded by the Board's or the Executive Committee's act (a resignation accepted, a termination minuted, a contract's end), never by the platform's inference (P1 §2.2's pattern). Every grant in the bundle ends with the seat — sessions ended, the audit line written (the slice 4b rule: a grant ended by a recorded act is never re-granted from the environment).
- **The functional seats of §5 fall vacant**, not to a successor: the office map shows each as vacant until the Executive Director's seat (or, while vacant, the President under 7.2.1 as principal executive officer) assigns it. The data custodian seat (P15) and the staff-liaison seats are the ones a Board must not leave empty, and the committees page says so.
- **Nothing is personal.** No audit line is deleted; no letter sent is unsent; a follow-up assigned to the seat is reassigned, not closed. The successor reads what the seat did because the seat did it.
- **The vacancy is a decision item** on the Board's queue the day the seat ends, citing 7.9.7 ("the Board of Directors shall vote to make a determination as to the need and necessity of filling the position; and … whether the general fund has the funds"), so that the first act of a vacancy is the by-law's and not an improvised one.
- **The combined position** (7.10.1) separates cleanly: ending one seat does not end the other.
- **A hand-over** is the P18 §3.3 pattern for a club treasurer applied here: the successor gets the seat, never a copy of anything; the drive's folders are the drive's permissions, outside the platform.

## 8. The maintainer question (Q-15)

Q-15: "Who maintains and pays for the platform after launch? No document names a maintainer or an annual budget line. The previous system could not be maintained once its contractor left." A reading places the platform — the Federation's record system — under that oversight, which would make the Executive Director the *owner* of the store register, the person the Board holds accountable for backups, restore tests, the vendor plans and their expiries, and the budget line. It would not make the Executive Director the *technical* maintainer: the 2026 description asks for fundraising, financial management and office organisation, not operations of a hosted application, and the Digital Infrastructure Committee's subcommittee, a contractor or the hosting provider's support are the record's candidates for the technical responder (P15 §2.3).

## 9. Rules

1. The Executive Director and the Executive Assistant are seats on the committee register, non-voting on the Board and the Executive Committee, each with a dated appointing act and an end date (By-Laws 6.2.2, 7.1.1, 7.9.4, 7.9.8, 7.10.1; P1 §2.2).
2. A seat's grants are a bundle whose every line cites a by-law duty or a dated Board decision; no line exists by default (7.9.1; Four-Lens §6; *design choice* for the bundle as a register row).
3. The five sensitive permissions are never in the bundle by inheritance; `role:grant` on the seat's own bundle is refused structurally (R39; Four-Lens §6.2; *design choice*).
4. A read of a club's names by the Executive Director is a named-purpose act with an audit line written before the first row renders, visible to the club the same day and counted for the Council and the Board; without a purpose on the Board's list it is refused by name (7.9.3; Q-44; Breeze Parity §3; *design choice* for the club's visibility of the read).
5. The suppression floor applies to the seat as to every reader (S7, R43; *design choice*).
6. The seat votes nowhere, adopts no decision, changes no standing, deletes nothing, signs nothing for the Federation that P3 or the by-laws give to another seat, and reads no affiliate store (6.2.2, 7.1.1, 7.9.10; Article IV; D1, D6; the registry's rule; 7.2.1, 7.5.1, 7.7.1; R39).
7. The Executive Assistant's acts are the text's: minutes, polls, custody and release of applications on the Executive Committee's recorded approval, receipt of lists and amendments (7.10.2, 6.2.4, 8.12.6, 8.12.7, 16.1.1).
8. The release of the membership list under 8.12.7 is the directory under its consents, never an export (R27; P19 rule 6; *design choice*).
9. The office is a set of functional seats with holders, shown to the Board; a function nobody holds is vacant, not assumed (7.10.2; *design choice*).
10. The hire's record on the platform is the Board's decision items, the Selection Committee as a body, the poll and the seat; no application, interview or evaluation content enters the platform (7.9.4–7.9.8; D66; D61's principle, *design choice*).
11. Residence and the written agreement to work from Headquarters are facts the Board records on the seat, not fields the platform validates (Const. V §3; 7.9.7; *design choice*).
12. A seat ends by a recorded act; its grants end with it; nothing it did is deleted; the vacancy is a 7.9.7 decision item the same day (7.9.7; P1 §2.2; slice 4b's rule).
13. The same person holding an ARFECF seat holds it in ARFECF's scope under ARFECF's by-laws; nothing crosses (R39; ARFECF 2015 by-laws).
14. Where the record is silent — the purposes list, the office map, the maintainer, the signatory, the combined position — the platform refuses or shows "no rule on file" and routes the question to the named owner (R40).
15. Every refusal names its rule.
