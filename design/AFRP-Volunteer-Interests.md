# Volunteer interests and rosters

**In the PR papers (proposals, 2018–2021), as the press note read them.** A Media and Public Relations Committee whose chair recruits the members; up to a handful of media spokespersons who answer in the President's absence; a bank of storytellers; writers with samples; social-media volunteers; a rotating committee member on watch; a media list built with the clubs. The Arabic Program's committee decided in August 2026 to recruit native-speaking conversation partners through club presidents; the Preservation Project's committee asked presidents in 2021 for volunteers and photographs. Nowhere in the working files is there a list of what members are willing to do, a volunteer roster kept from year to year, a record of volunteer hours, or a written screening rule for any role outside the camp.

**The shape of the problem.** Every programme and every committee recruits by asking the people it already knows, and each keeps its own sheet. The Federation has no way to ask the whole membership once, keep the answers, and let a committee draw from them without seeing who said no. The record already has the parts — a consent store, a message class for invitations, scoped seats, a rule that declines stay invisible — and lacks the object that joins them: the member's own statement of what they are willing to be asked.

## 2. Interests on the member's own record

**The object.** An *interest* is a row on the member's record: a term from a controlled vocabulary, a dated opt-in, the scope the member allows (the Federation and its affiliates; one named programme; one named club; any of these), an optional note of availability (the B2b blockout dates, generalised to "not before" and "not after"), and a withdrawal date when the member withdraws it. An interest is the member's own statement, entered by the member on the member lens (or by the office from a signed paper form, with the source recorded, as the join wizard already records a consent's source). It is never inferred from attendance, from a seat held, from a gift, or from the tree.

**The vocabulary.** The Federation sets the list; the platform holds it as data with a date and an authority, not as code. The terms the record names today, gathered from the sources above: *spokesperson* (PR 2018; 7.9.3), *storyteller* (PR 2018), *writer* (PR 2018; the Magazine), *club correspondent* (the club procedure 2025; Magazine club seat), *social media* (PR 2018), *translator or interpreter* (the Arabic Program; the Medical Mission), *conversation partner* (Arabic committee, August 2026), *driver* and *chaperone* (camp committee; the Exchange Mission), *counsellor* (camp), *mentor* (Leadership Ramallah, August 2026; RBPN's five-year plan, crosswalk F7), *event help* (the B2b roles: set-up, door, kitchen, banquet), *medical mission volunteer* (ARFHSN, by profession), *oral-history interviewer* and *archive help* (Preservation), *committee service* (17.1.4; the committee interest form), *host committee* (the Convention and the Mid-Year; P8), *advocacy* — a member lead for a public office (P14 §2.3; the committee's 2020 minutes). Each term carries a *touches minors* flag (§5) and the entity or programme that owns it. A term the Federation has not adopted does not exist on the form. *Design choice:* the first list is the one above, offered to the Board or the Executive Director to adopt or cut (Q-83); nothing is live until it is set.

**Consent, not a field.** Opting in is a consent to be *asked* — a fourth consent beside R27's three (*design choice*, extending R27; the Rules Register row should gain it), scoped to a purpose, dated, withdrawable at any time, with the withdrawal a new row (R27's retraction rule). It is not a consent to be listed: whether an interest *shows* in the directory's browse axis is the member's separate display consent on that field, exactly as the Network Plan's "willing to help" axis is a displayed field under consent. A member can therefore be willing to be asked by the camp committee and show nothing to anyone. A member with no interest rows is simply not on any roster; the platform infers no willingness from silence (the communications workflow's "no consent rows means no" for invitations, club plan §9.2, a design choice under Q-46).

**Minors.** A person under eighteen may be a camper or a counsellor-in-training, never a volunteer on a roster. An interest cannot be recorded on a minor's record; a household adult cannot record one on the minor's behalf (R6's seven powers do not include it, and this note adds none). At eighteen the person is asked directly (D22's pattern, D33). *Design choice from R6 and R7, not a new rule.* The camp's CIT category stays the camp committee's, inside the camp module, under the Network Plan's finding that a sixteen-year-old CIT is "structurally unscreenable" and is controlled by supervision rules, never by a screening record.

**What the member sees.** Their own interests, with dates; every roster they are currently on because of them (§3), and who runs it; every ask they have received (§4), and what they answered; a withdraw button on each row that takes effect at once. They never see a count of how many others are on a roster, and never another member's interests except as the directory shows them under that member's display consent.

## 3. Rosters

*Since 4 October 2026 (D99 review), a word, a design choice:* in this note a *roster* is an **interest list**: the people whose own interest rows match a term and a scope, computed and never typed. Elsewhere in the record the word means something else: a body's seats (the Board's 6.2.2 roster, P1), the invited attendance list of a meeting (P8), a club's members (the club notes; Breeze Parity), and a programme's participants (P12). Read every "roster" in this note, and in VI2 and VI4, as "interest list"; a slice building it names the object so. The *yes* that puts someone on a slot is an assignment (§4), the dated role of `AFRP-Platform-Primitives.md` (P29 §4). No rule changes.

**What a roster is.** A *roster* is a list of people whose interest rows match a term and a scope: the Federation's spokesperson pool (term *spokesperson*, scope Federation), the camp's chaperone roster (term *chaperone*, scope the camp programme), a club's event-help roster (term *event help*, scope that club), the Magazine's writers' bank. A roster is computed from interests, never typed; a person is on it because they opted in to that term at that scope and for no other reason. Adding a person to a roster by hand is refused with the rule named. A roster is never a group the member did not choose (Breeze Parity §4, nothing automatic without a named rule; club plan §3.2, a follow-up is a task about a person, never a note). Elsewhere "roster" means a body's seat list (P1), an event's invited or accepted list (P8, P12) or a club's membership (the club plan); in this note it means only the interest-derived list.

**Who runs a roster.** The seat that owns the term's scope: the Executive Director for Federation-scope terms (7.9.3 for media; 7.9.2 for appointed committees); a programme committee's chair or coordinator for programme-scope terms (the committee each register entry names); a club's officers for club-scope terms (club plan §3.2, §4.2). The roster is read under that seat's lens and nowhere else (Four-Lens §6: a grant is scoped; a programme seat does not grant the club lens and a club seat does not grant the programme lens). The club's volunteers' lead for a programme (club plan §3.4; C4) reads the programme roster filtered to their own club.

**What a roster shows.** Name, the interest's date, the scope, availability as yes or no for a given date (never why — P20-F2's design choice), the screening state where the term touches minors (§5: current, expired, none; never content), and the member's contact *through the broker* under their contact consent. It never shows a decline, a withdrawn interest, a member who withdrew, or anyone's interests beyond the one term. The roster is filtered by standing: a lapsed member stays on it for the directory grace (D6, slice 7b) and then drops, because the Federation does not ask non-members to act in its name (By-Law 8.1.2 for committees; *design choice* for the rest, Q-84).

**Rosters the record already names, and their owner.** The spokesperson pool (Press Workflow §4; Executive Director with the Board); the committee interest list, which is a roster with term *committee service* handed to the Selection Committee as 17.1.4's recruiting intake and to P1's vacancy list (§6); the camp staff roster, which is the camp committee's and lives in the `camp` module with the screening record (§5); the Arabic conversation partners and the Preservation volunteers, each a programme roster with a per-club lead (club plan §7.1); the Medical Mission's invited roster (programme register; a term by profession, with the credentialing question carried, not answered); event volunteers per event (B2b), which draw from the club's or programme's roster and from the event's own sign-up link; the Magazine's correspondents, which are **not** a roster but seats from the officers form (club plan §4.1 step 2) — a club names one correspondent; a *writer* interest is the pool the Magazine's editor may ask beyond that seat.

**Counts.** Each roster reports a count per club and per programme to the Federation roll-up under the floor (club plan §10; S7), and the year-one baseline takes "volunteers per club" and "mission volunteers" from here (D67; club plan §7.1 count column).

## 4. The ask

**An ask is an invitation.** When a roster owner wants help, they compose an ask to the roster or part of it: what, when, where, for whom, and how to answer. It goes out under the *programme invitation* class on the communications desk (workflow `communications`; Letters and Onboarding §2) to consenting channels only, with the withheld stored by name on the desk and never shown to the roster owner as anything but a count. A Federation-scope ask is operational for nobody: it is an invitation, and a member who muted invitations does not get it.

**The answer.** Yes, no, or no answer, each a dated row on the member's own record. *Yes* puts the member on the event's volunteer slot (B2b assign/invite) or the pool for that ask, and the roster owner sees the name. *No* and *no answer* are recorded on the member's record and **nowhere a committee or a club can see** — the member-lens rule as it stands (`experiences.yaml`, member lens; Program Experience Architecture §3 rule 2). The roster owner sees how many said yes and who; the count of asks sent; nothing else. A roster never sorts by responsiveness and no surface derives one (the Network Plan: do not build an engagement score). Three asks with no answer do not change the interest; only the member withdraws it.

**The assignment.** An **assignment** is the dated row a roster owner records when a *yes* is put to a slot, a class or an office (P12 §3.4; P14 §2.3): the member, the thing, the period, ended by the member or the owner, never deleted.

**Reminders and blockouts.** Once a member says yes to an event slot, reminders are operational messages for that slot (P20-F1) and respect the channel consents; an SMS reminder is refused to a member who said no to texts. A blockout date set by the member refuses an assignment on that date (P20-F2); the ask itself still goes out, because an ask is a question and the member decides.

**Thanks.** The record holds no thank-you moment for volunteers (D10's four moments are for contributors). *Raised, not designed:* Q-85.

## 5. Screening where a role touches minors

*Since 4 October 2026 (D99 review), marked unoperated in the manner of P23 §9.7:* outside the camp the Federation has no written screening rule, and none is on file for the camp's volunteers either (the Decisions Register's conflicts table, Q-86's premise). The screening rows below for every role other than the camp's (club child check-in, the Exchange Mission's chaperones, a club youth event, a mentor) and the Medical Mission's credentials are **designed; not built until Q-86 and Q-87 are answered.** The camp's own screening step reads the row as §5 says. Nothing below is deleted.

**What the record says.** The camp plan's findings, taken as decisions by that plan: a paid CIT is an employee and parent visibility flips off; the ACA background-check standard "starts at 18", so a minor CIT is controlled by supervision rules in the roster (never counted in ratios, never alone with minors), not by a screening record; the FCRA disclosure must be stand-alone and "bundling it into the volunteer application is the most-litigated defect in this area"; California AB 506 reaches youth-serving organisations that are not camps; no biometrics on minors. The camp workflow's step: "screen staff on the check and credential expiry". D61: no form about a minor enters the platform. The Federation has no written screening rule for any role outside the camp — not for club child check-in, not for the Exchange Mission's chaperones, not for a club youth event, not for a mentor. *Since 2 October 2026 (research round):* for the camp itself, no written screening, training or reporting policy is on file (Q-86, Q-165). The camp workflow's screening step is the record's design, not a description of practice.

**What the platform holds.** For every interest term flagged *touches minors*, and for every event volunteer slot or programme seat on an event or programme whose participants include minors, the platform holds on the person's record a *screening row*: the kind of check (as the Federation names it — a criminal-record check, a sex-offender registry check, a reference check, a training certificate), the date it was completed, the date it expires under the Federation's rule, who recorded it (a seat), and the check's provider as a name. **It never holds the content or the result of a check**, the consent form, the report, or any note about it — only that a check of that kind is on file as of a date, exactly as D61 holds "whether the health form is on file" and never the form. A roster shows the state (current, expiring, expired, none) and nothing more. A person with no current screening row cannot be assigned to a slot or seat that touches minors; the refusal names the rule and the kind of check missing. The office may record a screening row; a programme committee's chair may for their programme; nobody may record one for themselves.

**The disclosure and the authorisation.** The platform does not take the FCRA disclosure or the authorisation: both are the Federation's paper (or the provider's) and stay out of the platform, so that the stand-alone rule cannot be broken by a form the platform generated. The platform may hold *that* the disclosure was given on a date, as a row of the same kind. *Design choice, following the camp plan's finding.* Whether screening a volunteer is "employment purposes" under the FCRA — which decides whether the stand-alone rule and the adverse-action steps bind the Federation for unpaid roles — is a question the record does not answer and this note could not confirm from a primary source (Sources); it is for the Legal Advisor (Q-86).

**What is silent.** Which roles must be screened beyond the camp's; the kinds of check and their validity period; who pays; whether a club running a youth event is under the Federation's rule or its own state's; what happens at a result the Federation does not like (an adverse decision is a human act outside the platform, and the platform records only that the screening row was not created). *Q-86, Q-87.*

## 6. The spokesperson seat, and the committee interest form

**The spokesperson (P6).** The *spokesperson* roster is the pool Press Workflow §4 asked this note for. The seat itself stays what P6 made it: a seat on the committee register (CW1), appointed by a Board act on the Executive Director's recommendation (7.9.3), dated, for a term, with the count a parameter. This note adds the pool the seat is filled from (members who opted in to *spokesperson*), a *trained on* date on the interest row recorded by the Executive Director when the member completes whatever briefing the Federation sets (the endowment campaign's talking points are one such briefing, at role level), and a filter so that the appointing surface lists only trained members of the pool. An inquiry routes to the seats (P6-J07), never to the pool.

**The committee interest form.** The reviewer's question — general to the back end, or routed to the committee — is answered by the record's own shape rather than by a choice: the form is the *committee service* interest (optionally naming one or more bodies the member would serve on, from the register's list), and the roster it produces is read by the Selection Committee, whose by-law duty is to recruit and screen for every vacant seat (17.1.1, 17.1.4), and by the President as the appointing authority for standing committees (8.1.3). A committee chair does not read it, because chairs do not appoint under the 2024 text (8.1.3, except where a by-law says otherwise — Committee Workspace §2.2). The vacancy list (CW5) and this roster meet on the Selection Committee's intake, which is P5's slice SC1, so until SC1 lands the Selection Committee reads the roster as a list and the hand-off is by hand. A member's expression of interest is **not** a candidacy, a nomination or an application: 17.1.4's applications, CVs and rankings are the Selection Committee's packet and never enter the platform (the scholarship's binding rule 2 by analogy; D61's pattern). Club presidents' recommendations under 17.1.3 are a separate path the platform records only as a dated referral naming the president's seat.

## 7. Hours, and what else the record does not hold

The working files hold no volunteer hours anywhere, and no document asks for them; grant reports the affiliates file ask for participant counts and "success stories", not hours. The platform therefore records none by default. The question is real — a Care grant's narrative, an in-kind valuation for the auditor, a thank-you — and it is raised, not designed (Q-88). If the Board wants hours, the record is the volunteer's own entry against an event slot or a programme, confirmed by the roster owner, visible to the member and as a count to the roster owner, and never a ranking.

Also not in the record, and raised: a volunteer agreement or code of conduct (nothing is on file for any programme but the camp's counsellor handbook, which is the camp committee's); a reimbursement rule for volunteers (the camp's adults waive it; nothing else is written) — a statement line only if a programme writes a term (club plan §5.2); insurance cover for volunteers outside the camp (the camp's policy is the only one found).

## 8. Rules

1. An interest is the member's own statement, recorded by the member or from their signed form with the source kept; never inferred from attendance, a seat, a gift or the tree. *Design choice; R27's consent model.*
2. Opting in is a consent to be asked: dated, scoped, withdrawable, the withdrawal a new row (R27). Showing an interest is the separate display consent on that field (R27; Network Plan).
3. A minor holds no interest row and sits on no roster; the question is put to the person at eighteen (R6, R7, D22, D33).
4. A roster is computed from interests and never typed; a person added by hand is refused with the rule named. *Design choice; Breeze Parity §4.*
5. A roster is read only by the seat that owns its scope, under that seat's lens (Four-Lens §6); a club's lead reads the programme roster filtered to their club (club plan §3.4).
6. An ask is a programme-invitation-class message to consenting channels (workflow `communications`; Letters and Onboarding rule 7). A decline or a silence is recorded nowhere a committee or club can see (member lens, `experiences.yaml`; Program Experience Architecture §3). No surface ranks members by response.
7. Contact runs through the broker (Network Plan; Directory Formats rule 5); no roster payload carries an address, phone or email.
8. A term that touches minors requires a current screening row before assignment; the platform holds the kind, dates and recorder of a check and never its content or result (D61's pattern; the camp plan's findings; *design choice*). The FCRA disclosure and authorisation stay outside the platform (*design choice* on the camp plan's finding).
9. A lapsed member leaves every roster after the directory grace (D6; 8.1.2 for committees; *design choice* for the rest, Q-84).
10. The spokesperson seat is appointed under 7.9.3 from the trained pool (Press Workflow §4; CW1); the committee-service roster is read by the Selection Committee and the President (17.1.1, 17.1.4, 8.1.3), never by a chair.
11. Each roster reports counts per club and programme under the floor (S7; D67).
12. Every refusal names its rule.
