Congressional Outreach

Year-round advocacy for Palestinian rights at every level of government and across party lines: members meet their own elected officials, and the committee briefs State Department and DHS officials. An issue becomes a statement, a member alert and meetings that are logged.

In service today
An automated meeting tracker on Microsoft Forms, SharePoint and Power Automate (2025–26), which replaced the Excel tracker; letters and statements in Word and PDF; e-mail alerts to members; town halls on Microsoft Teams; a constituent-stories website in development.

Who it's for

AFRP members; the committee arranges meetings with a member's own elected officials and offers a committee member or expert to join.

The operating year

Reactive, as issues arise: a statement or letter signed by the President, a member alert with a one-pager, then calls and meetings logged. In 2025–26 the committee also ran virtual and in-person events, a Hill visit, town halls co-hosted with other village associations (each with an 'action' segment asking members to contact their representatives on a named bill), and coalition work with Palestinian-American organisations. The Excel tracker (last edited 2023) was replaced by the committee with an automated meeting tracker (Q-183). No written procedure.

The five rungs

Not stated in the record for this programme. The five rungs are hear, show up, take part, give or serve, and lead (D25).

Who runs it

The Government Affairs Committee. Advocacy positions are the Federation's, adopted through governance, not a committee's own. Who clears a position before the President signs is not written down. In practice the chair and co-chair review agendas drafted by two paid congressional-outreach coordinators, and the President opens town halls. The by-law paragraph has the Board establish the committee and appoint its chair and members; no appointment record was found.

Money

AFRP General Fund (D55, D65); the platform refuses to charge advocacy to ARFECF or ARFHSN (D65). It may also be the by-laws' 'Internships in Washington' Endowed Fund purpose; confirm. Two paid congressional-outreach coordinators sit on the General Fund's contract-labour line. A Washington internship programme (four interns a year, a partner to cover living costs) was voted a Federation contribution at the March 2025 Mid-Year with the funding source left open; ARFECF By-Law 6.3.1 names Internships in Washington as an Endowment purpose (Q-182).

Hand-offs

Fed by exchange-mission alumni. Feeds and is fed by the Day of Action. Advocacy is organised by district, so it relies on the consented member directory.

  • Clubs host meetings with representatives in their districts (outreach tracker, 2022–2023)
  • On the platform: no seat (the 2020 representative was not adopted); a district meeting is an event role; no line (D65); meetings hosted per club as the count (Club Experience Plan §7)

  • Constituent stories on a committee website: what consent, what retention, and is a story a member record? (Q-184)
