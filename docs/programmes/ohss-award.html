Outstanding High School Senior Award

An award recognising community service and leadership in graduating high-school seniors, created in 2013–14 as a technology scholarship under the Leadership Ramallah committee. The award was not retired and continues (D82, 3 Oct 2026, answering Q-163); the record's earlier "retired 31 Aug 2026 by Board resolution" (Program Architecture §6) is corrected. In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026): awards were made in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and the public site and the Federation's programmes flyer still offered it in October 2026.

Continues (D82): the 2025 cycle took applications on an online form, and afrp.org offered the award with a 31 May deadline in October 2026.

Who it's for

Graduating high-school seniors in good standing at their school, at the published minimum GPA, with demonstrated leadership and community service, whose families are Federation members in good standing (2014, 2019, 2020; afrp.org in 2026: applicant and family must be AFRP members in good standing). The pack: the form, an official transcript, an essay of at least 500 words on leadership and service, a school recommendation on letterhead and a verification letter from the organisation where the student volunteered. The form has no club field and no membership number.

The operating year

Applications due in May: postmarked 1 May (2014), 15 May (2019, 2020), 31 May (2023–2025; afrp.org, 2026). Posted to the office until 2020; an online form by 2025. Acceptance letters in July–August; the newsletter announces the number of recipients and the prize tiers each September. In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026) announcement was by newsletter, not at the Convention. The record's earlier retirement date, 31 August 2026, is corrected by D82: the award continues.

The five rungs

Not stated in the record for this programme. The five rungs are hear, show up, take part, give or serve, and lead (D25).

Who runs it

The Leadership Ramallah committee and its chair, with the programme coordinator (2013–2020): the chair requested the budgeted sum from the ARFECF Board in writing and signed the acceptance letters, and the 2020 pack is headed Leadership Ramallah. The register's earlier 'Scholarship Committee, on an award track' came from the prototype and is not evidenced (Q-164). Who scored, and on what sheet, is not written (Q-117).

Money

Tiered gift cards (laptops in the first year), bought by the office; budgeted in the Education Fund and released on the committee chair's written request to the ARFECF Board (2013–14); paid by the Educational Fund Treasurer (the Education Fund's report, 2017).

Hand-offs

Fed by Camp Ramallah; the 2019 description names Leadership Ramallah and Project Hope as programmes it feeds, and it sat before the Scholarship on the Education ladder. It continues (D82), so no gap opens between Camp and the Scholarship and Q-20 falls away.
