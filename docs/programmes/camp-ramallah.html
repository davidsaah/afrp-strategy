Camp Ramallah

A residential summer camp bringing young members from across the US together for Ramallah heritage, Palestinian history and culture, outdoors. For most people it is the first rung of the whole federation.

Who it's for

Campers aged 13 to 17 whose parents are members; capacity is in the sixties, set by the facility and the number of volunteers (camp committee, 2026). Counselors must be 19 or have finished a year of college, and must be Federation members in their own right (afrp.org). Counselors-in-training from 18. The 2025 annual report gives the range as 13 to 18 (Q-167). A small delegation of campers travelled from Ramallah in 2025 (Q-166). Counselors apply through My AFRP (afrp.org).

The operating year

December: preparation in the CRM. January–February: flyer, counselor applications and camper registration open. Until full or the end of April: registration, first come, first served. March–May: acceptance letters. May: parents' letter; travel details by form by the end of May. June: camp week (14–20 June in 2026). June–August: rebates to families, counselor reimbursements and the post-camp report.

Who runs it

A camp committee with a chair, supported by the Federation's programme director, appointed under By-Law 8.1.3 (President-appointed, one-year terms), whose members must meet 8.1.2. The committee decides acceptances; Federation staff administer registration, forms and reimbursements. What it escalates is not stated. In 2025 the volunteer staff were the committee (including a physician), support adults, counselors and a head counselor (annual report, 2026). In August 2026 the President's office set the committee four outcomes: more campers and counselors, a succession plan, a property plan and expanded offerings for families.

Money

ARFECF's Educational Fund (D55): a Camp Ramallah line in the approved budget, with camper fees as income. The registration fee carries a surcharge unless both parents are members; each family receives a rebate per child after camp, funded by donors; the Federation pays counselors' airfare; families pay campers' airfare. Several clubs add their own rebates, recorded on the club's statement (D57). The camp land account is ARFECF's (D55). Figures are not given here.

Hand-offs

Fed by household records (age). Feeds Project Hope at 18 and, via counselors, Leadership Ramallah at 21. The HS Senior Award is the next rung; it was not retired and continues (D82). The camp committee's proposed college-age conference (August 2026) would sit in it.

Open questions

  • Must a camper's parents be members? The application form (2022) requires member parents and charges a surcharge where one is not; afrp.org states membership for counselors and is silent for campers
  • May a club pay a first-year counselor's dues on their behalf (By-Law 8.12.4)?
  • The selection rule: the form says first come, first served until full; the Hub's per-club quota and four-per-family cap have no source. Which does the Camp committee mean?
  • Which state's camp regulation governs, and is AFRP ACA-accredited or does it want to be?
  • Holding entity: answered by D55 (ARFECF), pending Board confirmation. Whether Camp becomes its own tax-exempt entity is under discussion, not decided (camp committee, August 2026)
  • Screening (Q-86): no written screening or training policy is on file. Does the Board adopt a screening and training policy before the 2027 season, and does it reach counselors, committee members and the physician alike?
  • Is the Federation a mandated reporter under the camp state's law, and does the camp hold a written abuse policy? (Q-165)
  • Campers from Ramallah: who sponsors, who consents, which account pays travel, and what the record keeps (Q-166)
  • The upper age, 17 or 18, and whether a family programme is a camp session or a new programme (Q-167)
