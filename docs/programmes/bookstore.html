Heritage · programme

Bookstore

An online store for books, magazine back issues, clan books, merchandise and digital downloads, with member pricing for members in good standing (the design). In practice the Federation sells books through two online stores, one on afrp.org and one on the member portal, with seven and six titles, to US addresses only, with no member price, stock figures or digital goods; back issues, clan books, merchandise and downloads are not sold. The Federation's own overview sheet is an empty template, and no owner is recorded.

In service today
Two online stores: the afrp.org shop and the member portal's Digital Store, with seven and six titles, US addresses only, no member price; the CRM's event-guest records for a dictionary's pre-orders; a Cook Book chequebook.

Who it's for

Members and the public; the member price applies only when signed in. In practice neither store shows a member price, and both sell to US addresses only (Q-199).

The operating year

Not stated, except that the cook book is reprinted when stock runs out (2019–20).

The five rungs

Not stated in the record for this programme. The five rungs are hear, show up, take part, give or serve, and lead (D25).

Who runs it

Whoever holds the store:manage grant runs the catalogue and inventory. Who fulfils orders is an open question. No committee is listed (overview sheet, 2026). In practice the office takes online orders through the two stores and the Educational Fund Treasurer reports the Cook Book fund; nobody is recorded as the store's owner (Q-197).

Money

Sales are exchange revenue, never a gift; sales tax collected is a liability. The use-case note (§5.3) designs Michigan first with a nexus monitor; whether Michigan registration exists is Q-198. Payments are simulated until slice 5. In practice the Cook Book fund is a separate chequebook reported to the Board by the Educational Fund Treasurer: book sales and interest in; printing and, in 2025–26, a transfer to the Education Fund for the Federation's accounting and CRM systems out. The cook book, compiled by a Detroit women's union in 1976 and reprinted by the Federation, is also sold through at least one third-party museum store, and a trademark in its name is pending (Q-173). Whether Michigan registration has been made is open (Q-198).
