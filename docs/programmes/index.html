Branches and programmes

The programmes on the four branches, and the Convention where they meet. Each page says what the programme is, what it needs, and how far the platform has got.

Two ways of moving

People move in two directions, and they are kept apart (D39).

  • Along a branch, between programmes. How that works depends on the branch's shape. Education is a ladder by age, Leadership a ladder by capacity, Heritage a cluster with no "next", and Care a set of destinations.
  • Up the rungs inside one programme. Every programme uses the same five: hear, show up, take part, give or serve, lead (D25). Members never see a score, and moving down a rung is never a flag.

Pages marked Plan only have a register row and a written plan and little more. They say so, and where the record is silent they say that too.

What the research of 2 October 2026 changed

The second research round read each programme's own files and public pages against this register. Four entries read differently as a result. In each case the record keeps what was decided, shows what the sources found beside it, and leaves the choice to the people named.

  • The Leadership branch's entry rung. The record names Emerging Leaders as the way into the Leadership branch. No programme by that name appears in any of the Federation's documents, mailings, budgets or web pages. The one mention most likely refers to the Youth Summit, reported since 2023 as the Day of Action. Whether the entry rung is retired or pointed at another programme is for David and the Strategic Planning Committee (Q-178). The path from a high-school senior to a Board seat, through what the record already holds, is design note P25 (AFRP-Leadership-Pipeline.md §3).
  • Senior Living. The sources show the senior home is the Ramallah Foundation's own project: the Foundation owns it, built it and will run it. The Federation's part has been communications, gifts passed through and a budget grant voted at the Convention. That part is designed in P23 Part B; the capital-campaign design is marked as never operated by the Federation (P23 §9.7). Whether Senior Living is a Federation programme at all is for David and the Boards (Q-203).
  • The Relief Fund. It has run since 2020 from a separate bank account, with the Board voting emergency support and the Federation's representative in Ramallah distributing it. AFRP holds the fund (D83, 3 October 2026, David's decision for the design record); the books carry it inside ARFECF, which is a reconciliation item, and who approves a transfer between the Board's votes is still open (Q-109). P23 Part A designs the fund as run, with three paths for money out and who authorises each.
  • The bookstore. Two online stores already sell the Federation's books, one on afrp.org and one on the member portal, with no member price. The cook book's sales are the educational fund's income; for the other titles no entity is stated. Who owns the bookstore is for the Boards and David (Q-197; P24 §8).

The Outstanding High School Senior Award was not retired and continues (D82, 3 October 2026, answering Q-163); the record's earlier "retired 31 August 2026" is corrected. Which committee runs it is still open (Q-164).

Camp Ramallah

A residential summer camp bringing young members from across the US together for Ramallah heritage, Palestinian history and culture, outdoors. For most people it is the first rung of the whole federation.

Scholarship

Financial support for college students of Ramallah descent on two tracks, in the US and in Ramallah, each with a fixed annual amount: up to four years of undergraduate support, extended through graduation for medical students, with named scholarships alongside the general fund.

A two-week, hands-on programme in Palestine, run since 1997, that connects young adults to their ancestral homeland through volunteer service and meetings with local organisations, officials and youth. AFRPWorks (the Ramallah Jobs Initiative)

Connects Federation member employers with skilled professionals in Ramallah for gig, part-time and full-time work, so that income reaches Ramallah households. A prototype exists; a pilot sourced through a partner in Ramallah is planned.

Congressional Outreach

Year-round advocacy for Palestinian rights at every level of government and across party lines: members meet their own elected officials, and the committee briefs State Department and DHS officials. An issue becomes a statement, a member alert and meetings that are logged.

Day of Action

A yearly advocacy summit on Capitol Hill: young Palestinian-American adults meet their representatives and senators after training and advocacy-tools sessions with partner organisations. Its history runs back to the Federation's Youth Summit of 2015; in 2026 it was an open registration rather than an application.

Heritage · A cluster, used for life

The Ramallah Family Tree, the Preservation Project, Hathihe Ramallah and the Bookstore. There is no "next" here and no milestone invitations: people come and go all their lives, and nobody graduates from the archive to the magazine. The tree also holds every member's node, so the other branches draw on it too (D30, D53). More about Heritage

Ramallah Family Tree

The genealogy of the original Ramallah clans and their descendants: about 28,000 individuals, with more to add among those born since 1990 (Family Tree committee, 2026). It sits on the Heritage branch (D53). Every member still has a node on it, and a node is never a lineage claim (D30). A print edition is planned.

Built other
Ramallah Preservation Project

A digital archive of Ramallah's history, with a committee since 2017 (committee records; afrp.org gives 2020): photographs, documents, articles and oral histories with migrants and first-generation Americans, shown at Convention exhibits and, in plans, with a museum partner. A digitisation pilot with a scanning vendor is under way, funded by a foundation grant.

Medical Mission

Volunteer physicians, specialists and health professionals from the US and Palestine building long-term health capacity in Palestine. Active: prosthetics (under an agreement with a university and a rehabilitation centre), neurology capacity, urology and ultrasound-biopsy training, occupational therapy training. Past: a cardiovascular centre, a women's health centre, telemedicine, an emergency-medicine residency, PPE and oxygen, emergency supplies for Gaza and the West Bank, a radiology image system and hospital beds. The 2025–26 annual report adds service units at a medical complex funded by a major donor's project fund, a kitchen renovation there, prostate and eye screening with local partners, an occupational-therapy tuition programme for diploma-holders in Gaza, training with a Jordanian cancer centre, clinical rotations with a US health system, school laboratories in Ramallah, and a medical school as a prospective partner. The published Gaza report says appeal money bought supplies for a Ramallah hospital, medications and meals in Gaza, and funded a Ramallah dialysis clinic (Q-218).

Designed Flagship Mission
Women to Women

Direct financial help to women in Ramallah and their families for basic needs and self-improvement. Projects are proposed in writing from Ramallah and approved by a committee, which runs its own luncheon and co-funds Care projects.

Plan only run in practice since 2020 Committee
Senior Living Project

The Ramallah Foundation's new senior citizens' home in Ramallah, replacing a 1970s home run by a Ramallah women's union. Ground was broken in October 2023 and the cornerstone laid in June 2024; construction was reported complete in 2026 with an added floor, raising capacity to nearly 70, with furnishing under way, residents moving by the end of 2026, an executive director to be hired and a ribbon-cutting announced for the autumn. The Federation promotes it, has passed gifts to the Foundation and has voted it a budget line; whether it stays on the programme register is Q-203.

The Relief Fund

Monthly support to families in Ramallah since 2020, emergency appeals and the annual Ramallah charities, from a separate bank account titled in the Federation's name and reported at every Board meeting by the Educational Fund Treasurer. The Board votes emergency support; the Federation's representative in Ramallah distributes it on lists from local partners and reports counts and totals; recipients are told the Federation is the source. ARFECF's accountants book its gifts inside ARFECF; D83 names AFRP as the holding entity, and the books are a reconciliation item.

The Convention · The junction where the branches meet

Once a year every branch meets at the Convention: the governance floor, programme gatherings and the community. It belongs to no branch and is a rung on none. More about The Convention

The Annual Convention

The Federation's annual gathering, held since 1959 and drawing about 1,300 to 1,400 people: governance, the election and the Assembly floor, the banquet, symposiums, youth events and every programme's moment. It sits on no branch and is reachable from all of them. Hosts are set through 2030.
