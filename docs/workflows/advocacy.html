Advocacy and the Day of Action

An issue becomes a statement signed by the President; members are asked to act with a one-pager; meetings are supported and logged; once a year selected members go to Washington for the Day of Action. All of it is AFRP General Fund activity and is never charged to an affiliate (D65).

Steps

  1. Draft a statement or letter on an issueGovernment Affairs committee
  2. Approve and sign the statementPresident
  3. Alert members with a one-pager and ask them to call, write or meet their representatives; the audience is consent-filtered (S1)Government Affairs committee; platform
  4. Offer a committee member to join a member's meeting with an officeGovernment Affairs committee
  5. Log each contact and meeting against the officeMember lead; Government Affairs committee
  6. Run the Day of Action: open applications, select, train and reimburse travel (as in selection programmes)Programme director; President
  7. Report outcomes to the BoardGovernment Affairs committee
  8. Charge every cost to AFRP's General Fund; refuse any charge to ARFECF or ARFHSN (D65)Platform

Trigger, frequency, records, money

Trigger
An issue the Government Affairs committee takes up; the Day of Action's call
Frequency
On demand; the Day of Action yearly in November
Records
Statements with their signatory, member alerts, contacts and meetings by office, Day of Action participants, reports to the Board
Money
Day of Action travel reimbursements and advocacy costs, AFRP General Fund only

Rule against practice

  • The 2024 by-laws describe AFRP as a 501(c)(4) and its affiliates as 501(c)(3)s; a 2025 contract annex describes AFRP as a 501(c)(3) (the Federation's contract records; the Legal Advisor to confirm, D65).

Hands off to

  • advocacy → selection: Day of Action participants are chosen through the selection workflow
  • advocacy → comms: member alerts go to a consent-filtered audience (S1)
  • advocacy → reporting: contacts, meetings and Day of Action participation are reported to the Board
