Club remittance and the ledger

National collects once and remits club money on a periodic run. The ledger posts gross, closes daily as an idempotent batch, and books reversals as their own entries.

Built in the Hub
Partly built Daily close, receivables, reversals and the remittance statement are built; approval completes on a named officer (14 Sep 2026). The variance queue and the QuickBooks connection are not built.

Steps

  1. Attest each collection event: member, amount, date, channel, collectorClub treasurer
  2. Post the daily close by fund and kind; fees post as an expense, revenue stays grossPlatform; national treasurer
  3. Hold club dues as a liability to the club; post a receivable from the club for unremitted AFRP duesPlatform
  4. Produce the remittance run as a statement with carry-forward; it shows on the club's one statement (D57)Platform
  5. Approve the run by nameExecutive Committee officer
  6. Handle a reversal as a new entry; after remittance it becomes a receivable (S3)National treasurer
  7. Reconcile collections to remittance; a variance opens a queue item with an owner and a clockNational staff
  8. Settle each affiliate's 'due to' balance (ARFECF, ARFHSN, the Ramallah Foundation) to that affiliate — in practice (research, 2 Oct 2026) the books carry these balances and the cadence and approver are not written (Q-232)Executive Administrator (bookkeeper); General Fund Treasurer

Rule against practice

  • The 2024 by-laws require club money for AFRP projects to pass through headquarters; Mid-Year sponsorship cheques are made payable to the host club (the Federation's Mid-Year records, 2026).
  • D1 holds club-collected AFRP dues as agent and remits by run; no written remittance rule appears in the Federation's records since 2009, and the current member database computes a "due to local club" amount on event payments with no written rule behind it (Q-36).
  • In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026): since 2026 card payments run through one processing account per entity (AFRP, ARFECF, ARFHSN), replacing one AFRP account with transfers by hand; the registration system's pay links name the receiving entity's processor profile; QuickBooks Online holds AFRP's and ARFECF's books, fed from the registration system. AFRP's chart holds each affiliate purpose collected on the affiliate's behalf as a 'due to' liability — D1's agency treatment and D56's one door then remit, in the books for affiliates though not yet for club dues (Q-232).
  • In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026): the only club-share figure in any system is the registration system's per-payment 'due to local club' (the payment less the discount and the card cost); it is not a remittance or a settlement (Q-36, Q-127).
  • In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026): four roles answer to 'the CPA': the outside firm that reviews or audits and files, the General Fund Treasurer, a volunteer financial adviser and the bookkeeper. Which countersigns D1's treatment is Q-231.

Hands off to

  • ledger → reporting: the daily close feeds giving and fund views on the federation dashboard
  • ledger → club share: the remittance is one line on the club's single statement; the remittance rule stays D1 (D57)
  • ledger → voting: standing keys to the collection date, so a slow-remitting club cannot disenfranchise members (D1-D)
  • ledger → convention: delegate standing is verified against the club remittance ledger, not self-attested
  • ledger → affiliates: each affiliate purpose collected through AFRP is a 'due to' balance until settled (D56; Q-232)
