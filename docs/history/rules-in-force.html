How the record decides

Precedence, highest first: the operative by-law texts; the Decisions Register and the named design documents; the prototype and the narrative mockups, which evidence a story and never a rule; the code, which is evidence of what is and never of what should be (D41). Each open question carries one Q-number used in GitHub Discussions and in the committee's Google Doc (D43); the committee's Doc is canonical for the strategy text until the committee adopts the plan (D44). New site pages name roles only (D50). Every outside source goes through one intake, which proposes changes and never decides them; a new rule enters only as a decision in the register (D54).

Platform primitives and the build loop

The shapes the design notes draw again and again are built once, as the platform primitives of AFRP-Platform-Primitives.md (P29), before the slices that would otherwise each build their own: A2 (the refusal and the snapshot) and PR0 (the act and append-only record, the scoped parameter and authority row, the dated-role base, the record on file, the clock), then each remaining primitive in the first slice that needs it. Process tooling (PT1) generates the counts and records the registry now only checks, renders each round's findings from one file and generates journey stubs from the design notes. A slice that only builds a register or a form, and touches none of voting, payments, by-laws, authority, the tree, scholarships, the directory or core.decision, runs a lighter loop (build, one combined attack that includes its fix layer, the hygiene lints); every other slice keeps the full four steps (D99).

Who sees the living on the tree

Signed out, nothing about the living: a living person shows a name and a position and no dates (D28). A signed-in member sees the living in full inside their own branch: blood relatives sharing an ancestor no further back than a great-great-grandparent, which reaches third cousins, and their spouses; outside it a living person shows a name and a position only (D71, D76). Own branch runs along blood links only. At join and renewal a person who is not yet a member is shown no living person's details beyond what D28 allows a signed-out visitor (D98). The public build ships the deceased real and the living synthetic (D15). Using the tree to reach people is for members only, about their own branch (D36).

Proposing and approving tree changes

Members in current national standing may propose changes; no account exists for non-members to contribute (D70, confirming D13). Every life event queues for the committee (D14, D31). One committee approval applies an own-household life event or a non-structural correction; two approvals from different committee members apply a structural change; the committee may appoint clan stewards, who pre-review and recommend and never approve (D72). The structural kinds are a parent link added, changed or removed; two records merged; a branch moved; a person removed or marked removed; a clan assignment changed; a sex recorded differently; a link's grade raised; the committee may amend the list by a dated act (D81). The second holder of tree:moderate is a Family Tree Committee member seated by the committee's own act (D79). A sitting of work reaches the committee as one bundle (D21). A correction to a line a decision was made on is re-evaluated and flagged to the committee, never auto-revoked (D12).

The tree's record and its custody

The Hub becomes the record of the tree. During a parallel run of 30 to 60 days the Hub wins: the record keeper's edits arrive as GEDCOM imports through the diff preview and are applied as audited committee edits, conflicts held for a decision; at the end the external file is read-only and the Hub exports a GEDCOM backup every night under tree:moderate (D69). The licence and the account that hold the master file and its cloud copy are held in the Federation's name; the Family Tree Committee records, as dated acts, who may open the file and the export cadence; the AFRP Board records by minute the terms on which the Federation holds the compilation and the original author's work; the parallel run starts only after both are on file and cites them (D87).

Life events and deaths

Every life event queues for the Family Tree Committee (D14, D31). A household may submit its own life event to the Magazine; a Magazine correspondent or the editor may submit one on its behalf, and the correspondent's attestation that the family agreed, recorded with who entered it, when and from what, is enough for a wedding, a birth or a graduation to print; for a birth this is an exception to D33 (D89, as limited by D95). A death printed in the Magazine needs the approval of the named family contact: the person who reported the death cannot be that contact and no role overrides the hold; a correspondent may enter a death, which waits for the contact (D95).

The archive, drive documents and clan books

The Federation's shared drive is the archive of record; the platform holds its own registers and links to the drive for documents (D66). For now a document or media item held on the drive, such as a clan book or an item attached to a tree node, is not served to members: the platform lists it and gives its drive link to the seats of the committee that holds it only, gives no member a share link and stores no copy (D88). Once a book can be served inside the sign-in boundary, clan books are served to signed-in members in good standing within D71's limits with no approval step; a book carrying living people's details beyond a name and a position is not served by standing; until then access stays with the project's own process, which the Board records by minute with its test (D97).

Receipting, agency and giving

When a club collects AFRP dues it acts as AFRP's agent: the money is AFRP's from the first dollar, the receipt is AFRP's, and standing starts at collection (D1). Gifts to the Medical Mission and the Human Services Network funds are ARFHSN's, and the Federation's pages collect them as ARFHSN's agent (D56). Ticketed events are non-deductible entirely, and a contribution beyond the ticket is receipted separately by the receiving entity (D9). Surplus on a restricted appeal goes to the receiving entity's general fund only where that is disclosed on the ticket before payment, and never within ARFECF (D7). Raising in another entity's name runs from a purpose catalogue; a new purpose goes to that entity's board and blocks publication, and the authoriser is per purpose (D8). Contributors get four moments, then an annual statement (D10).
