Rules that did not bind this cycle

A credentials committee needs to know a rule existed and did nothing, as much as it needs to know one fired.

  • Maximum 8 seats — not reached. The largest club, Chicago, took 7. A club would need 38 eligible members to be capped; Chicago has 36.
  • Minimum 1 seat — structurally unreachable. With a divisor of 5 and a club floor of 6, the smallest apportioned club already computes a quotient of 1.20. The minimum cannot bind while the floor exceeds half the divisor.
  • Major fraction — fired once. Los Angeles only. All seven other clubs rounded down.
  • Club floor — fired twice. Birmingham and Portland excluded, 9 members removed from the house count.
Change a parameter and this table recomputes; it does not re‑read membership. Apportionment always runs against the frozen electorate, so a bylaw amendment mid‑convention changes seats without changing who is in the house.

  • “Chicago 36 → 7” is not defensible on the floor. “7.20, fraction .20 below .5, rounded down, cap of 8 not reached” is.
  • The HOUSE row states explicitly that 46.80 does not round to 45 — rounding is per club. That is the single most common challenge to an apportionment.

04

Ballot manager — and what creating one commits you to

Two mechanisms, one engine. Officer elections are secret with a one‑time receipt; bylaws and resolutions are recorded and attributed to each delegate by name. The choice is made once, at creation, and cannot be changed afterwards — so the creation panel states the consequence of each option in full, including the one nobody enjoys writing down: a lost receipt cannot be reissued.

What this screen decides

  • Secret or recorded, once and irreversibly. The two produce different tables, different receipts and different minutes.
  • Which threshold rule the engine will apply, including majority_of_seats, which uses the whole house — 14 of 30 loses even if it wins the room.

Rules enforced

  • Sealedballot_participation and sealed_vote are never joined. Double voting is impossible, turnout is known, and every voter can verify their own ballot.
  • Abstention handling is a convocation‑wide bylaw setting, so the alternative is visibly disabled rather than absent.
  • The electorate field is read‑only. There is no control anywhere in the product that repoints a ballot at live membership.

The cost, stated plainly

  • A lost receipt is unrecoverable. This is not a gap to fix later; it is the price of secrecy, and the UI says so at creation and again at the moment of voting.
  • This is not end‑to‑end‑verifiable voting. An administrator with database access could alter sealed rows; the mitigations are exact‑scope ballot:unseal, full audit, and certification against a result hash.
05

Election results and certification

The result screen has one job beyond arithmetic: to make the shape of the decision impossible to misread. The denominator is stated before the percentages. Abstentions are drawn on the same scale as the votes and then explicitly removed from the threshold calculation. Quorum is shown as a precondition, not a footnote. And the certify action is attributed to a named officer against a result hash that anyone can recompute.

06

Voter eligibility explorer

There is no isInGoodStanding(person) in this system — that signature is the bug, so it does not exist. Standing is always a fact about a person in a scope, at a date, and this screen shows both scopes side by side at the record date with the precise reason when one of them fails. It is the screen that resolves disputes, so it never says “ineligible” without saying why, and it never says why without citing the date the snapshot was taken.

Why this is a liability and not income

Booking club dues as revenue would overstate national’s income — and its Form 990 — by the entire club‑dues line. On this run alone that is $3,360 in a single month across five clubs. Across 18 clubs and a year it is a materially wrong return.

The caveat is real, and it is a CPA’s judgement rather than a schema’s. Under ASC 958 a recipient organisation may recognise revenue rather than a liability if it holds variance power or is financially interrelated with the beneficiary. A federation and its chartered clubs could plausibly meet that test. So treatment is set per club and defaults to the conservative agency; under revenue national books the dues as income and the payout as a grant to an affiliate. Both are implemented and tested, and the numbers move materially between them — which is exactly why it is an explicit setting rather than an assumption buried in a query.

And the third worked case — national staff. T. Haddad holds national_staff @ federation and may read every club roster in the federation. He may not export PII, post to the ledger, or unseal a ballot: all three are sensitive, all three require an exact‑scope grant, and breadth of read access confers none of them.
Every evaluation on this page is logged with subject, permission, scope, decision and matched grant — including the refusals, which are the ones people later argue about.

What this screen decides

  • Arguments. “Why can’t I see Chicago’s dues?” has a printable answer that names the grant, the scope and the rule.
  • Whether a refusal is a bug or the design. Support staff can tell the two apart without reading the source.

Rules enforced

  • DirectionalFederation satisfies club and program; club never satisfies another club; club and program are siblings.
  • Sensitivemember:pii:read, member:export, ballot:unseal, ledger:post and role:grant are never inherited — exact scope only.

Provenance

  • All four worked examples come from the access policy note: Detroit treasurer, Camp Ramallah chair, national staff, credentials committee. Implemented in src/access/policy.ts with 41 tests.

What is real and what is not. Every governance and ledger figure on these screens is verbatim output from the implemented engine against seeded data: the apportionment table and its 234‑member, 45‑seat house; the President election at 22–13 with 10 abstentions; the two‑thirds bylaw vote at 35 of 45; the daily close of $4,459.00 across four funds; and the August remittance run at $3,360.00 payable with $40.00 carried. People, clubs’ email addresses, gift identifiers, delegate names and dates of individual payments are fictional. Bylaw parameters used here are illustrative and tuned to the seed population — AFRP’s actual bylaws will differ, which is exactly why they are versioned configuration rather than code.

Two answers still outstanding, and both move numbers. Whether club dues are held as an agency liability or recognised as revenue under ASC 958 is a judgement for AFRP’s CPA, not for a schema; the platform defaults to the conservative treatment and records the answer per club. And the bylaws themselves — apportionment formula, quorum basis, thresholds, record‑date interval, proxy voting and tie‑breaking — are the blocker for the governance module. Proxy voting and tie‑breaking are the two most likely to be in the document and absent from the engine.
