Scholarship Alumni Recruiting — Desktop Screens

Eight annotated desktop views for the staff who will load forty years of scholarship history and then go and find the people in it. A recipient from 1998 is now about 48 — mid‑career, quite possibly with a child approaching college. AFRP paid for their education, which makes them the warmest list the federation owns. This is a recruiting project that happens to need historic data, not an archiving project with a recruiting side‑effect — so a name, a year and an amount is enough to start, and every screen below is built to reach someone rather than to complete a record. Built on AFRP Design System v2, inlined verbatim; each frame is a picture of a laptop screen.

Group A

Getting the history in

Three screens that turn a binder into a list of people. The bar is deliberately low: name and award year are the only required fields, and nothing loaded here is published or contacted until a human has looked at it.

01

Import wizard — upload, map, validate, preview

A spreadsheet surfaces from a filing cabinet and the whole programme depends on it going in without a fight. Only Name and Award year are required; everything else can stay blank and the row still loads. The screen’s real job is the preview at the bottom — before a single row is written, staff can see how many people this file will match to existing members, how many it will create, and how many it is going to hand back for review. Import is idempotent on (source, source_row), so running the same file twice is safe, and every row keeps its raw binder text beside the parsed value.

What this screen decides

  • Whether a forty‑year‑old binder can be loaded in an afternoon. Two required fields means it can; ten would mean it never gets loaded at all.
  • How much review work the import is about to create — 58 rows, not 412 — which is the number that decides whether match review is an afternoon or a project.

Rules made visible

  • Tier 1Name and award year required. Amount, scholarship, institution, field and graduation are all optional and 64 rows import with no amount at all.
  • IdempotentKeyed on (source, source_row). The strip states “already imported: 0 of 412” before the button is pressed, so a nervous second run is safe.
  • Raw keptThe row 3 panel shows binder text and parsed value together. A mapping mistake is fixed from the record, not from the filing cabinet.
  • Grouped21 people on 44 rows become 21 alumni and 44 awards. Never silently merged — A2 in the use cases.

Detail notes

  • Both mapping warnings are recoverable, not fatal: unparseable graduation years import blank; the rival “Class” column is left unmapped with its reasoning stated.
  • The four blocked rows are DOB conflicts, shown here rather than buried: those are fathers and sons, and getting them wrong is the single most damaging error the programme can make.
  • Figures are illustrative of a real binder shape — 412 rows, roughly 15 % needing review — not output from AFRP’s actual files.

What this screen decides

  • Whether a 1996 award belongs to the man who received it or to his brother at the same phone number. That is a judgement, and the screen is built to hand it to a person with the evidence laid out rather than to resolve it quietly.
  • Which imported rows become new people. 231 of 389 will, and that is a success, not a failure — a new historic_alumnus is still a person AFRP can now reach.

Rules made visible

  • WeightsEmail +0.96, fuzzy name up to +0.36 at a 0.80 gate, DOB +0.30, phone +0.30, postal +0.12 — stated in the black strip, not buried in a tooltip.
  • BlockingA conflicting DOB is −0.55 and takes row 44 to −0.07. The screen also shows the 0.48 it would have scored without the rule, because that is the number that explains why the rule exists.
  • No auto-pickRow 92 has three candidates; all three are listed with their scores and the reason each is weak. The system ranks and refuses to choose.

Detail notes

  • Transliteration counts are called out on the screen (17 of 58) because it is the single figure that tells AFRP whether exact matching would have worked. It would not have.
  • The blocked father/son case does not end in a dead record: it creates the 1940 alumnus and proposes a parent of link for the genealogy moderator, which is what later makes him traceable.
  • Every outcome on this queue is reversible. Frame 03 carries the unlink action and states exactly what it restores.

One person, assembled from three binder rows and twenty‑eight years. Group B

Finding people

The capability no commercial CRM has. Most organisations lose alumni to dead addresses and stop; AFRP has the family tree, and a 2003 address that bounces is not the end of the search.

What this screen decides

  • Whether the board funds the next phase. 24.8% scholar‑to‑member conversion against $18.40 per reconnection is the argument, and both numbers are on one line.
  • Where to spend the next hundred hours — 212 unlocated alumni with a family route open is a work queue with a cost attached, not a lament.

Rules made visible

  • Tier 1 onlyEvery headline figure derives from name, year, amount and scholarship name. Institution is on 47.7% of records and no number on this page depends on it.
  • Honest denominatorsAll percentages are of 863 recipients, never of “alumni we managed to find”. The 222 unlocated are stated on the page.
  • RetainedDeceased and suppressed alumni are counted in total awarded and in impact. Suppression removes contact, never history.

Detail notes

  • Awards grow from $500 in 1985 to $5,000 in 2026; the decade averages ($776 to $4,908) carry that without anyone having to say it.
  • The Salameh Award appears with zero disbursed because it is endowed and first awards in 2027. Omitting it would hide the clearest instance of the loop closing.
  • Seven links have been reversed to date. That figure is published rather than buried: a system that never reports a reversal is one where nobody is checking.

What is real and what is not. The rules on these screens are real design commitments: tier 1 as the loading standard; idempotent import on (source, source_row) with the raw original retained; the identity matcher at email +0.96, fuzzy name to +0.36 above a 0.80 gate, DOB +0.30, phone +0.30, postal +0.12 and a blocking −0.55 on a DOB conflict, with auto‑merge at 0.95 and human review from 0.60; always‑reversible links; consent applied at segment build; permanent suppression that closes the family‑tree route; and no donation ask in a first contact. The people, clubs, award amounts, campaign results and impact figures are fictional and internally consistent across the eight frames — 863 recipients, 1,142 awards, $2,847,500, 24.8% conversion — chosen to be plausible for a programme of AFRP’s age and size. They are not AFRP’s numbers, because AFRP does not have them yet. That is the problem this module exists to solve.

What is still open, and it changes the shape of the work. How far back the paperwork goes and in what form determines tier 1 volume. Roughly how many recipients there are in total decides whether match review is an afternoon or a project — at 15 % needing review, 800 recipients is a week and 8,000 is a hire. Whether any informal alumni contact list already exists could remove a third of the tracing queue on day one. Who works the match queue matters more than any of it: it needs someone who knows the families, because Khouri and Khoury is a judgement and not a data‑entry task. And two board questions sit behind frames 06 and 07 — whether a joining‑fee waiver for alumni is acceptable, and whether legacy status is ever a factor in a needs‑based award. It is modelled here as context only, deliberately.
