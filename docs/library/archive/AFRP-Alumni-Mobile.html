My AFRP — Scholarship Alumni

Fourteen annotated phone screens following one person. In 1996, 1997 and 1998 the Haddad Memorial Scholarship paid for Rania Mogannam's nursing degree. AFRP has not spoken to her since. Her address is twenty‑five years stale, so she was reached through a cousin in Detroit. These frames are what she sees, in order: the email, the claim, the verification, her own record, the program today, joining, giving back — and the door out. There is no donation ask anywhere in the first contact, and the four things she is offered appear in one fixed order that never changes: see the program → her club → join → give. Every colour, size and component comes from AFRP Design System v2, inlined here verbatim; this document adds only device frames and annotations on top of it.

How to read these screens

Each frame is a live 375 px-wide rendering built only from the .c-* classes and --* tokens of Design System v2. Touch targets are raised from the 44 px WCAG 2.2 AA floor to the 48 px --touch token throughout and every input sits at the 16 px floor, because a 1998 scholar is around 48 now and their parents are on this platform too. Frames extend past 812 px so a whole scrolling screen is visible at once rather than cropped at the fold.

The person these screens follow

Rania S. Mogannam, born 12 April 1978, Chicago. Awarded the Haddad Memorial Scholarship in 1996, 1997 and 1998 — $1,200 a year, $3,600 in total. Studied nursing at Wayne State University, graduated 1999. She is now a nurse practitioner in Chicago with a 17‑year‑old daughter, Layla. AFRP has had no contact with her since 1998. The address in the binder is twenty‑five years old and the email bounced; she was reached because her cousin Ghassan Mogannam is a current member of the Detroit Club and agreed to pass a message on. He was never given her contact details and she was never told which relative was asked — until she asked, on frame 02. Her institution is recorded wrongly in the 1996 binder, which is why she verifies with her date of birth and corrects the record on frame 06. All names, dates and figures are fictional; the organisation, the 18 clubs and the named scholarships’ behaviour are real.

The six rules these screens build to

Rule 1No ask in the first contactThe first message leads with her record — the award, the year, the named scholarship, the amount — and contains no donation request, no membership pitch and no price. Cold contact after twenty-five years is a first impression, not a follow-up. Frames 01–05.
Rule 2The four offers appear in one fixed orderSee the program today → connect with your club → join → give. Join and give are visually secondary until the first two have been offered. Frame 07 is that screen.
Rule 3Verification before any disclosureTwo facts — award year and institution, or date of birth. Until they match, nothing beyond her own name and award is on the page: no address, no relatives, no club roster. Frames 02, 03.
Rule 4Prior recipients are never charged a joining feeStated in the price, not in the small print, and stated as a reason rather than a discount: AFRP already invested in her. Frames 07, 09.
Rule 5A claimant never overwrites the recordA correction is a request with a reviewer and a state, not an edit. The 1996 value stays visible beside it until staff decide. Frame 06.
Rule 6“Stop” is immediate, permanent, and closes the family path tooOne acknowledgement, then silence, on every channel, with no cooling-off period — and no warm introduction through a cousin, ever again. Her award history is retained for program impact with contact suppressed. Frame 14.
Frames 01–03

Being found

Twenty-five years of silence, then a message. Everything in this group is written to be recognition rather than solicitation: her own record first, no price anywhere on the page, and an honest answer to the question she will certainly ask — how did you find me?

Frames 04–06

Recognition

She is through the gate. This is the part of the flow that has to earn everything after it: her own history laid out properly, the fund that paid for it given a face, the program she was part of shown as it is now — and a way to tell AFRP it got something wrong without being able to rewrite the record herself.

Frames 07–10

Choosing

Four things are offered and the order is fixed: see the program today, connect with your club, join, give. Two of them cost nothing and they are the two that come first. This group is where a recognition flow could quietly turn into a funnel, and the design work is mostly in refusing to let it.

Frames 11–14

Giving back, and the way out

Only now, after four screens that asked for nothing, does AFRP ask. Three ways to give something back — money, an endowment, or an evening a quarter — and then the screen that matters more than all of them: what happens when someone says no.

What these screens commit AFRP to

Five things on this page are promises rather than decoration. Each has a cost, and the cost is the reason it is worth writing down before build rather than discovering it during the first campaign.

CommitmentThe first contact carries no ask, everWhich means the campaign tool has to be able to refuse to send a first-contact template that contains a gift link or a price, rather than warn and continue. Frames 01–05.
CommitmentThe four offers are ordered in the product, not in the copyWhich means no staff member, template or A/B test can promote giving above the club, and the ordering has to live where campaigns cannot reach it. Frame 07.
CommitmentPrior recipients never pay a joining feeWhich is a board decision with a revenue line attached, and it needs to be taken before the first alumnus reaches frame 09 rather than after.
CommitmentA correction is a request with a reviewerWhich means somebody has to work that queue, forever, and the record has to store two values for the same field without either being “the” value. Frame 06.
CommitmentSuppression closes the family pathWhich means the tracing tool must read the suppression list of the target, not only of the relative it is about to write to — the one rule in this deck that is easy to get wrong and impossible to apologise for. Frame 14.

What is deliberately not here

No countdown timer, no “847 alumni have already joined”, no progress bar towards a target, no pre-ticked recurring gift, no dark-pattern decline link written in grey. Every one of them would raise the conversion rate on frame 11 and every one of them would cost more than it earned, because the asset being spent here is twenty-five years of goodwill that AFRP did not have to earn twice.

Still open

The joining-fee waiver, whether award history may ever appear in the member directory, the endowment minimum and draw rate, and whether legacy status is a factor in current scholarship decisions are all board decisions, not code. The values shown here — no fee, directory off by default, $25,000 minimum at a 4.3% draw, legacy as context only — are illustrative and tuned to the seed data. Decide them and they become configuration.
