Where the project actually stands

Three things are true at once, and the third is the reason for this packet.

The design is complete.Every element AFRP operates — membership, dues, clubs, the nineteen programs, the Convention, elections, the four institutions, the family tree and the money that moves between them — exists as a working, clickable design. Sixty screens. Nothing is a sketch.
It has been attacked, hard.Ten adversarial review rounds, 419 agents and 258 attacks, have tried to break it. Twenty-four breaks were confirmed and fixed. The last rounds found errors in our data and arithmetic, not just our screens — including a by-law citation that had been invented, and step-parents being counted as blood ancestors for 12% of the family tree.
And it cannot proceed.What blocks it is not engineering. It is six decisions only the Board can make, six governing documents that are cited as binding but that nobody has produced, and four operational facts AFRP has never published. Every one is listed here.

Nothing in this packet asks for money or approval to build. The build hold stays in place. It asks for decisions that are already AFRP’s to make, and that the design has been carefully written to wait for rather than guess at.

The ask

Six decisions

Each is stated as it would be minuted. The recommendation is the design team’s, and every one of them can be voted down without anything breaking — the platform is built to name the rule it is following, whichever way you decide.

1The agency treatment of club-collected dues

The motion. That the Board note, and the Federation’s accountant countersign, that when a club collects AFRP dues the club acts as AFRP’s agent — the money is AFRP’s from the first dollar, held in trust, never club revenue.

Why it mattersThis is the foundation of every ledger table. It also settles something members feel: standing begins at collection, not at remittance, so a slow-remitting club can no longer cost its own members their vote.
What the text saysBy-Law 5.2 requires host-collected dues to be remitted and says they do not count toward host proceeds — money that cannot be the collector’s proceeds was never the collector’s money. 11.1.5 and 8.12.4 point the same way.
If deferredThe first ledger tables cannot be specified. Every downstream money screen stays provisional.
Recommendation. Adopt, and obtain the countersignature. Decided in principle 20 Aug; the accountant’s signature is outstanding.
→ See the money model
2The directory grace period

The motion. That the Board set the number of days a lapsed member remains visible in the member directory.

Why it mattersThe parameter currently defaults to zero, which means a lapse is an instant disappearance — from the directory, from search, from the eyes of everyone who was looking for them. That is almost certainly not what anyone intends, and the platform will not invent a number.
What the text saysNo by-law addresses directory visibility. This is a policy choice, not an interpretation.
If deferredMembers vanish the day their dues lapse.
Recommendation. Ninety days, shown with a quiet “renewal pending” state rather than silently. It covers the ordinary late renewal without leaving non-members listed indefinitely.
→ See the directory
3Whether to advance the two By-Law 9.1.3 amendments

The motion. That the Board decide whether to put either or both of the prepared 9.1.3 amendments to the floor.

Why it matters9.1.3 lets a certified member extract their vote from their club’s delegation and never says when that stops, so delegate weights can move while a vote is being counted. Separately, members with no chapter club have no delegation to be counted in at all, and the text is silent on what becomes of their vote.
What the text saysBoth are 2/3 delegate-vote matters under 16.1.1 and 9.1.1(3) — which means 9.1.3’s weighted fractional voting applies to the amendments that fix 9.1.3. Worth saying to the floor rather than letting someone discover it mid-debate.
If deferredBoth gaps stay live. The platform enforces a cutoff at the moment voting opens anyway, because a vote counted under shifting weights is not a vote — but it does so on a rule no text supports.
Recommendation. Advance both. They fix different holes; adopting one leaves the other open. Note: this was previously recorded as decided. It was not — that was a misreading on our part, and it has been corrected in the register.
→ See the live floor vote
4Scheduling the digital-voting amendment

The motion. That the Board name the convention at which the By-Law 9.2 revision is put, and the body that drafts it.

Why it mattersThe Board has already approved full digital voting as the direction. Until 9.2 is amended, the adopted 2024 text still governs: mailed CPA ballot, sealed envelopes, a June 10 postmark, ties broken by a draw from a bag. The platform runs that process in full and labels it the transitional channel.
What the text saysAmendments are submitted 60 days before the General Assembly; the Constitutional Committee’s review distributes 45 days before.
If deferredThe approved direction stays unimplementable, and the gap between what the Board decided and what the by-laws say widens each year.
Recommendation. Put it with the 9.1.3 amendments as one package — three of the four touch Article IX, and a floor that adopts them piecemeal creates new gaps.
→ See digital voting
5Exhibitor and press credentials

The motion. That the Board confirm exhibitor and press credentials are non-attendee credentials, so the membership-mandatory rule stays absolute.

Why it mattersThis is current practice inferred from the by-laws rather than a written rule. Confirming it now costs nothing; discovering it is contested during a convention costs a great deal.
What the text saysThe by-laws require attendees to be members and do not contemplate a non-member on the floor.
If deferredA vendor or a journalist on the convention floor is an unresolved question at the moment it is asked.
Recommendation. Confirm.
→ See the convention
6Convention deficit responsibility

The motion. That the Board confirm deficit responsibility is read from the Convention Contract, and note where that contract is silent.

Why it mattersA host city that loses money needs to know before it bids who carries the loss. The platform reads the Contract and says plainly on the screen where the Contract does not answer rather than assigning the loss by assumption.
What the text saysBy-Law 10.1.6 refers to the Convention Contract Guidelines; 10.1.7 requires a final accounting within 120 days.
If deferredHost clubs bid without knowing their exposure.
Recommendation. Confirm the reading, and close the silence in the next revision of the Guidelines.
→ See the convention money

The gap

Six documents that are cited as binding, and that nobody has produced

Each of these is named in a governing text or in AFRP’s own material as controlling something. None of them is in any file we have been given. Where a document is missing, the platform enforces the by-law text alone and marks the other figures unsourced — visibly, on screen, rather than quietly adopting them.

DocumentWhat it controlsWhere it is cited
Scholarship Fund Policy Statement (2015)Sets the spend rule. The most valuable of the six — it settles the 4% floor against the 5% cap for both ARFECF funds, which decides how much can be awarded each year.cited in ARFECF 6.4.1
Endowment Fund Policy StatementSame instrument for the Endowment. Named as governing; never seen.cited in ARFECF 6.3.1
ARFECF determination letterThe exempt-status letter. Now load-bearing, because both the scholarship and the endowment receipt donors from ARFECF — and a donor taking a deduction needs the entity to hold the status.not on file
Board Rules & RegulationsThe authority for the dues ladder. Every dues figure the platform shows is currently taken on trust.cited in AFRP 8.x
A clean ARFECF 2013 by-laws copyOurs is an OCR of a marked-up scan — §11.2 still carries a literal “Replace with” redline. A clean copy would rule out other silent divergences.source quality
ARFHSN trustee rosterThe fourteen trustees, their metro seats and term expirations. The staggered-term machinery cannot compute who is up for election without it.ARFHSN Art. III §2

The smaller gap

Four facts AFRP has never published

These are not decisions in the governance sense. They are things the Federation knows and has simply never written down, and each one blocks a screen from being honest.

FactWhy it blocks a screen
What Camp Ramallah costsThe fee, what it covers, and whether club scholarships exist. A checkout screen cannot be truthful without it — and “ask the coordinator” is a barrier that falls hardest on the families the camp most wants.
Whether a camper must be a memberafrp.org states the requirement for counsellors and is silent for campers. No by-law addresses minor membership at all, so the platform flags the question rather than enforcing an unwritten rule.
Counsellor vettingBackground checks for adults supervising minors are described nowhere in AFRP’s material. This needs a policy before it can be a screen, and it is a liability question as much as a design one.
Medical Mission credential requirementsWhat is verified for a volunteer clinician, by whom, and to what standard. The platform treats a current licence as a hard gate; the standard behind it is unwritten.

What it will not do

Commitments the design makes to members

These are not features. They are refusals, and they were written in because the review rounds kept finding the same failure: a system that is helpful to the organisation and quietly unkind to a person.

A refusal names the rule, never the relativeWhen the platform declines something — a vote, a conflict, an application — it says which rule and which date. It never says “ask an administrator”, and it never explains a refusal by naming another member.
Nothing is chased that should not beAlmost every engagement signal in the platform does nothing on purpose. The reminders that exist are dated obligations — a document due, a licence expiring — never a nudge about why someone stopped coming.
Grief suppresses the machinery, invisiblyWhere a bereavement is on record, renewal chases and program invitations go quiet — and the suppression is invisible to staff, because a visible suppression leaks the death as loudly as the reminder would have.
A decline is recorded nowhere a committee can seeTurning down an invitation leaves no mark that anyone can act on, and no rung that fails to advance can be used to infer one.
A genealogy database never decides who is RamallahThe family tree shows the path and its sources. The Membership Committee decides. A database error must never be able to deny someone their membership.
The subject controls their own likenessAnyone may contribute a photograph; only the person in it decides who sees it. For a minor, the choice is re-asked of them directly at eighteen.

How this was prepared. Every figure was computed from the source data rather than remembered, and the design was reviewed adversarially in ten rounds before this packet was written.
