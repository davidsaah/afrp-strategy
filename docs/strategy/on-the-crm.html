AFRP Strategy

Building on the CRM

The Hub is built in four layers. Each rests on the one below, and the first is the member database.

Four layers

4 · Governance and reporting By-laws held as rules, elections and digital voting, committee records and reports, and outcome reporting for each branch.
3 · Programmes on the branches Nineteen programmes on Education, Leadership, Heritage and Care, each with the same five rungs, and the Convention where they meet.
2 · Shared services Events, communications, funds and the ledger, the family tree, the magazine and the directory. Every programme uses the same ones.
1 · The CRM One record for each person, their household, club memberships, standing, consent and every payment.

Why the order matters

A programme cannot invite last summer's campers to the next step unless the campers are on record with their households. A ballot cannot be trusted unless standing is computed from real payments. A donor cannot be shown the return on a gift unless the gift and the programme it paid for are in the same ledger. Every strategic goal depends on the first layer being in service, with real members in it. That is why the build started there.

What the CRM replaces

Today one member can be five to seven unlinked records: a Dynamics 365 contact, a record in each club's Breeze account, a Mailchimp subscriber, a payment processor's customer, and an entry in the family-tree group. That is tolerable for a mailing list. With digital voting approved by the Board, it becomes a question of election integrity, and that is the finding that started this platform.

What the last system taught

The Federation has put in a CRM before. It went live in 2023, getting members registered was hard, it ran over budget in 2024, and once its contractor left nobody could maintain it. The Hub is built on the opposite assumptions:

  • Plain and long-lived. The technology was chosen for "who maintains this in 2031", not for novelty.
  • The design record is public. Anyone who takes it over can read why every rule exists.
  • Nothing is guessed. Where the by-laws or the Board are silent, the platform refuses and names the question rather than inventing an answer.

What the record still does not say

Who maintains the Hub after launch, and what it costs each year, is written in no document. It is open question Q-15, and it belongs with the Board.
