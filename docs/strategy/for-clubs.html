AFRP Strategy

For club presidents

What the platform gives a chapter club, what it asks of one, and what is still the Council's to decide. Written for the Council of Chapter Club Presidents.

What a club gets

A club's officers get one screen for one club, as they have in Breeze, and then three things Breeze cannot give them.

  1. Each member's national standing beside their club standing. The roster shows both. A member who has lapsed with the Federation and not with the club, or the other way round, is plain to see, and a follow-up can be raised with a due date and an owner so that nobody falls through.
  2. One statement of what the club and the Federation owe each other. Dues remitted, camp rebates, travel shares, event reimbursements, the Convention or Mid-Year settlement, grants to the club, and the club's share of a Care grant: every amount on one page, per holding entity, with a dated close and a way to query a line (Decision 57).
  3. The club as one among all the clubs. The Federation sees each club as counts, never as names: members, groups, events, attendance, open follow-ups, campers and scholars and volunteers per club, the statement balance, and where the club is on its move from Breeze. That is what the Council sees too.

Beside those: groups and follow-ups, club events with door check-in and attendance, a release list instead of a printed code for children (Breeze prints a pick-up code on both tags, and no article read describes it being checked), the officers form filed once a year that seats every Federation list, a secretary's queue for the things that need a person (a join that names the club, a household that splits, a member who moves clubs), and the members' directory filtered to the club, which shows only members who chose to be shown while the roster shows all.

What a club gives up

The platform refuses some things on purpose, and the Council should hear the list before the first club moves rather than after.

  • No export of a list to Excel, to a mailing tool, or to a printed directory or labels. A consent a member gave the Federation cannot travel to a tool the Federation does not control. A club that keeps its own mailing tool keeps its own list by hand.
  • No free-text notes on a person. A follow-up is a task with one completion note; there is no place to write what you think of someone.
  • No deleting a person. Records end; they are not erased.
  • No texting until the Federation holds a provider and a registered brand. Then each club texts under its own name on the one account.
  • No club giving through the platform until that question is answered (Breeze Parity, question 3). Until then the club keeps its own processor and the platform records only what passes between club and Federation.

Whether the Board wants a consented print purpose for directories is an open question (Breeze Parity, question 7).

The officer year

The club's year on the platform runs from the officers form to the hand-over. The president files the form by 1 August (By-Law 3.2), started from last year's; the Council, the Board roster and the Membership Committee seat are read from it; the secretary keeps the roster; the treasurer runs the month and reviews each statement; the delegation is selected from certified members for the Convention; the president reports to the Council from the dashboard; and at year end the seats change on a dated form and the balance, the open follow-ups and the calendar carry to the new officers. The full account is in Running a club.

Moving from Breeze

One club at a time, with a named owner on each side. Read-only access through the club's Breeze account owner, who is the only person who holds the key (a club whose owner has moved on recovers ownership from Breeze first); an extract, with attendance taken first because Breeze's own export leaves it out; identity matching with every doubtful pair sent to a human; a parallel run of two to four weeks in which the club's officers verify their own roster on the platform's screens; a dated sign-off by the president and treasurer; then the final export goes to the drive and, only once it is verified there, the subscription is the club's to cancel, because Breeze queues an account for deletion the moment it is cancelled. Nothing is ever written to Breeze. A club that is not on Breeze follows the same path from a spreadsheet. The order of clubs: one pilot that uses Breeze for roster, groups and events and not for giving or texting; then any club whose Breeze is failing, as its president reports; clubs on no system in parallel; the rest once texting and giving are answered. The full account is in Moving a club from Breeze.

What is the Council's to decide, or to ask the Board

The platform refuses to guess where the by-laws and the Board are silent. These are the questions in the Council's lap, each with its number on the open questions page.

  • Who approves a club's affiliation and what a late officers form costs (Q-40).
  • Whether a member may belong to two clubs, and who decides a move (Q-41).
  • Whether the monthly meeting and its attendance expectation are a Board rule (Q-42).
  • Whether a club's own membership tiers are the club's to define (Q-43).
  • Who appoints a club's seats in a programme: the club or the committee (Q-45).
  • The statement's terms no committee has written: the remittance cadence and minimum, who bears the processing fee, the camp rebate, the dabke travel term, an ad-book share for the host club (Q-47).
  • Which club goes first, and who holds its Breeze key (Breeze Parity, question 4).
  • How many clubs there are. The record has carried seventeen, eighteen or nineteen (Breeze Parity, question 8); the Federation's public annual report for 2024–25 counts fourteen active chapters. Which figure is the Federation's, and what makes a club active (Q-229).
  • Whether the move to Breeze is a Federation programme or each club's own choice, and who pays (Q-230).
  • What a club sees of its members' contact details. Today's portal shows a club president names and contact details; the record shows the Federation counts and shows contact fields only where each member has agreed (Q-237).
  • Whether a club may have its own government-affairs seat or action committee, who appoints it and what it sees (Q-131).
  • Whether a club's direct giving to Ramallah projects must go through one channel, and how relief drives across the clubs are run (Q-228, Q-180).

What the research of 2 October 2026 found about clubs

The second research round read the Federation's files and public records about the clubs. What it found is how things are done today. It changes no rule; where it departs from the record, the question that settles it is named.

  • Clubs are their own organisations. Public tax filings show the chapter clubs as separate exempt organisations: large clubs as social clubs, one revived club as a charity, and at least one club with a charitable foundation of its own beside it. Whether the Federation records each club's legal form on its club register is Q-227. One club's 2026 by-laws draft admits members more widely than By-Law 4.1.1 does; what such a member is on the platform, and whether the club's rule conflicts with the Federation's, is Q-226.
  • Delegates. The record has a club's officers choose its delegation (club plan §6.4). The Legal Advisor described to the Board in May 2026 a community's certified members voting on their delegates. The by-laws say neither. The Convention design (P22 §6) therefore records each delegation as a dated act of the club, stating the method the club used, and computes the vote weights the same way whichever method it was. How a community's delegate vote is held and recorded is Q-37.
  • Relief drives and direct giving. Clubs raise money for Ramallah, some through the Federation and some directly. Money a club raises for the Federation's Relief Fund passes through headquarters (By-Law 11.1.5) and appears on the club's statement with the club's name on each gift, never as the club's own money. A drive across the clubs shows each club's total. A club's gift to its own project in Ramallah is outside the platform until the Board answers Q-228 (P23 §5; Q-180 for drives).
  • Gifts to the senior home. In 2026 clubs were asked to consider a club legacy gift to the Ramallah Foundation's senior home, recognised by signage there. A club gift made through the Federation is a line on the club's statement; a gift made straight to the Foundation is the club's and the Foundation's business (D92, 4 October 2026; P23 §9.4). Recognition at the home is the Foundation's to give.
  • Local action committees. Government Affairs launched local action committees inside the clubs in 2025–26. The platform seats none until Q-131 says who appoints them and to whom they report; a member's own meeting with an office may still be recorded with that member's consent (P25 §5.4).
  • Correspondents. Announcements reach the Magazine through a city correspondent as well as from families. The correspondent is the club's seat in the Magazine. An announcement the correspondent enters prints on the correspondent's recorded word that the family agreed (D89), and a family can still submit for itself (P24 §3.2).
  • Prospects. Clubs keep lists of possible members in their own spreadsheets and in Breeze. In the draft contact-record design (P21, not adopted; Q-70), a club would hold a prospect only once that person has acted on the club's invitation or has given their own details at the club's door; a prospect nobody referred is Q-72 (P21 §10). A club would never see another club's contacts.
  • An account held for a club. ARFHSN holds the balance of one club's closed branch account for that club. Whose money it is, and whether it belongs on the club's statement, is Q-216.
