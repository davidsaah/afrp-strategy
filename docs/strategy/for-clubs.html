AFRP Strategy

For club presidents

What the platform gives a chapter club, what it asks of one, and what is still the Council's to decide. Written for the Council of Chapter Club Presidents.

What a club gets

A club's officers get one screen for one club, as they have in Breeze, and then three things Breeze cannot give them.

  1. Each member's national standing beside their club standing. The roster shows both. A member who has lapsed with the Federation and not with the club, or the other way round, is plain to see, and a follow-up can be raised with a due date and an owner so that nobody falls through.
  2. One statement of what the club and the Federation owe each other. Dues remitted, camp rebates, travel shares, event reimbursements, the Convention or Mid-Year settlement, grants to the club, and the club's share of a Care grant: every amount on one page, per holding entity, with a dated close and a way to query a line (Decision 57).
  3. The club as one among all the clubs. The Federation sees each club as counts, never as names: members, groups, events, attendance, open follow-ups, campers and scholars and volunteers per club, the statement balance, and where the club is on its move from Breeze. That is what the Council sees too.

Beside those: groups and follow-ups, club events with door check-in and attendance, a release list instead of a printed code for children, the officers form filed once a year that seats every Federation list, and a secretary's queue for the things that need a person (a join that names the club, a household that splits, a member who moves clubs).

What a club gives up

The platform refuses some things on purpose, and the Council should hear the list before the first club moves rather than after.

  • No export of a list to Excel, to a mailing tool, or to a printed directory or labels. A consent a member gave the Federation cannot travel to a tool the Federation does not control. A club that keeps its own mailing tool keeps its own list by hand.
  • No free-text notes on a person. A follow-up is a task with one completion note; there is no place to write what you think of someone.
  • No deleting a person. Records end; they are not erased.
  • No texting until the Federation holds a provider and a registered brand. Then each club texts under its own name on the one account.
  • No club giving through the platform until that question is answered (Breeze Parity, question 3). Until then the club keeps its own processor and the platform records only what passes between club and Federation.

Whether the Board wants a consented print purpose for directories is an open question (Breeze Parity, question 7).

The officer year

The club's year on the platform runs from the officers form to the hand-over. The president files the form by 1 August (By-Law 3.2), started from last year's; the Council, the Board roster and the Membership Committee seat are read from it; the secretary keeps the roster; the treasurer runs the month and reviews each statement; the delegation is selected from certified members for the Convention; the president reports to the Council from the dashboard; and at year end the seats change on a dated form and the balance, the open follow-ups and the calendar carry to the new officers. The full account is in Running a club.

Moving from Breeze

One club at a time, with a named owner on each side. Read-only access; an extract; identity matching with every doubtful pair sent to a human; a parallel run of two to four weeks in which the club's officers verify their own roster on the platform's screens; a dated sign-off by the president and treasurer; then the final export goes to the drive and the subscription is the club's to cancel. Nothing is ever written to Breeze. A club that is not on Breeze follows the same path from a spreadsheet. The order of clubs: one pilot that uses Breeze for roster, groups and events and not for giving or texting; then any club whose Breeze is failing, as its president reports; clubs on no system in parallel; the rest once texting and giving are answered. The full account is in Moving a club from Breeze.

What is the Council's to decide, or to ask the Board

The platform refuses to guess where the by-laws and the Board are silent. These are the questions in the Council's lap, each with its number on the open questions page.

  • Who approves a club's affiliation and what a late officers form costs (Q-40).
  • Whether a member may belong to two clubs, and who decides a move (Q-41).
  • Whether the monthly meeting and its attendance expectation are a Board rule (Q-42).
  • Whether a club's own membership tiers are the club's to define (Q-43).
  • Who appoints a club's seats in a programme: the club or the committee (Q-45).
  • The statement's terms no committee has written: the remittance cadence and minimum, who bears the processing fee, the camp rebate, the dabke travel term, an ad-book share for the host club (Q-47).
  • Which club goes first, and who holds its Breeze key (Breeze Parity, question 4).
  • How many clubs there are: seventeen, eighteen or nineteen (Breeze Parity, question 8).

Everything on this page is built on the 2024 Constitution and By-Laws. If the 2026 restatement is adopted (Q-33), the affiliation and delegate rules change and this page changes with them.
