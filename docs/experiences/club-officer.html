Club officer

A president, secretary or other officer of one chapter club.

Lens
Club
What they need
  • Know who their people are and whether the club is healthy
  • Reach members lawfully
  • Run meetings, events and the officer year
  • Keep the roster true as people join, lapse, move house or move clubs
  • Run a local action committee's meetings with officials and report them (Q-131)
  • Report the club's direct gift to a Ramallah project so that it is counted beside the Federation's charities (Q-228)
  • Hold the club's legal details on the register: state entity, federal subsection, any sister foundation (Q-227)
  • Hold the club's own prospects, referrals and event guests as the club's roles, and nothing else of anyone (P21 §10)
  • Raise money for the Relief Fund as gifts to the fund's purpose with the club's attribution, through headquarters (By-Laws 11.1.4, 11.1.5; P23 §5); a club's own Ramallah project is Q-228
  • As the host club's coordinator, print badges and read sub-event rosters and counts by club at the desk without a guest-list export (P22 §8; Q-127)
What the platform does for them
  • Roster with national and club status side by side
  • The members' directory filtered to the club, which shows only consenting members; the roster shows all and never leaves the club lens
  • Groups, follow-ups and club events with check-in
  • The acting panel on any filtered set (mail it, group it, follow it up)
  • The secretary's queue of things that need a person (a join naming this club, a household split, a move between clubs, a reported death)
  • A lapse raises a follow-up only if the club switched that rule on, and names the rule
  • Federation context as counts
  • A club member admitted under the club's own wider rule shows club standing only; how is not yet written (Q-226)
  • The Program Director's summary of each club's priorities after the presidents' meeting (in practice, research 2 Oct 2026)
  • The club's own contact roles: a prospect in the secretary's queue once the person has acted on the club's invitation; referrals as a count until the person consents; the club's event guests under the organiser seat, with no export (P21 §10; Q-72)
  • A relief drive across the clubs is one catalogue purpose with an end date and a tally of attributions per club; it cannot publish until its receiving row has an entity (P23 §5; Q-180)
  • The host coordinator's scoped grant on the event: badges and QR codes produced by the platform from the registration (P22 §5.4, §8; P13 §2.4)

Rules that protect them

  • A club officer cannot read another club's roster
  • Bulk export refuses the officer as it refuses everyone
  • Club and programme scopes are siblings; neither implies the other
  • A follow-up is a task about a person, closed and never deleted; there is no free-text note on a person anywhere in the club lens except a follow-up's one completion note
  • Minutes and club documents stay on the drive, the archive of record (D66); the platform links to them
  • Today the president's view and the host coordinator's export show members' names and contact details; the record's counts-only and per-field-consent rules narrow both (Q-237, Q-127)
  • A club never sees another club's contacts, any programme's roles, or a contact's channels outside its own roles; a learner or subscriber is never named to a club (P21 §10)
  • A Breeze profile of a non-member enters as a staging row with no consent row (P21 §7.5)
  • A drive's gifts are the fund's, counted on the club's statement and never the club's money (P23 §5; D57)
