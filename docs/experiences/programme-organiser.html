Programme lens

Programme organiser

A programme chair, reviewer, editor or staff member whose scope crosses every club for one programme.

Lens
Programme
What they need
  • See who is in the programme across the federation
  • Run applications and selection
  • Report outcomes without naming people
  • Release a scholarship instalment only after the semester's documents are verified (Q-159)
  • Compile a one-paragraph summary of each Fellowship application for the committee (in practice, research 2 Oct 2026)
  • Answer any question about camp screening from a record of who applied, was interviewed and was screened; no written screening, training or reporting policy is on file (Q-86, Q-165)
  • Log each scanning scope against the vendor agreement and the grant before the grant report (Q-108)
  • Sponsor a member's Arabic-class seat from another programme's fund with its purpose posted (Q-168)
  • Magazine subscription manager: see every subscription payment, by card, cheque or at an event, in one ledger, and produce each issue's print list from it (P24 §5)
  • Magazine treasurer: file a per-issue cost line against subscriptions and obituary income (P24 §6; Q-174)
  • The Federation's representative in Ramallah: report each path's counts, amounts, dates and organisations, never a person (P23 §7; Q-246)
  • Host coordinator: print badges and read rosters and counts by club at the desk without exporting the guest list (P22 §8; Q-127)
  • Preservation: take an oral-history release that grants each place a recording may be shown, separately (P27 §6)
What the platform does for them
  • Programme plans on the record, every section sourced
  • The five-rung ladder per programme
  • Suppressed pipeline counts
  • A document-gap list per selected scholar before any cheque (in practice, research 2 Oct 2026)
  • A grant row with its period, spend to date and report date, so an unspent balance is seen before the period ends (Q-108)
  • A learner's, applicant's or subscriber's role read in the programme's own scope only; a send to a field ("everyone with a medical occupation") cannot be built (P21 §5, §8)
  • The subscriber ledger after one reconciliation act; the print list at freeze leaves out lapsed print subscribers and the hand-off refuses while the mailing house's agreement row is empty (P24 §5.2, §5.3; Q-66)
  • The obituary as two objects: the death approved by the named family contact and a placement order whose fee is held unposted (P24 §3.3; Q-193)
  • The beneficiary-organisation register and the representative's report: counts, amounts, dates and organisations; a returned cheque is a reversal on its own line (P23 §4.2, §7)
  • The verification rows and the release for each scholarship instalment, and the office's draft letters (P26 §2, §4)
  • Stock, reprints and consignment for the bookstore: a reprint refuses while the title's rights row is "not stated" (P24 §8.3; Q-173)
  • The archive's consent scopes: community archive, public web, print and research, each a separate row; a withdrawal is a new row (P27 §6.2)

Rules that protect them

  • A programme scope stops at everything else
  • Cohort names only with roster:read at that programme
  • No engagement score exists
  • Interview notes hold no free text about an applicant's views (Q-118)
  • An oral history needs a written release with separate permissions for the community archive, the public web, print and research (Q-189)
  • Mission images of sites, partners and patients wait on a consent rule (Q-219)
  • No field accepts a beneficiary person; a note carrying a name is replaced by a corrected entry, never edited (P23 §7; P11 rule 12)
  • The survivors in an obituary are the family's text and are never linked to person records (P24 §3.3)
  • A recording made on a spoken permission shows only to the Preservation committee's seats (P27 §6.3; Q-189)
