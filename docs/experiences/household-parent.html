Household parent or guardian

An adult acting for minors in a household: registering, applying to camp, declaring births and naming who may collect a child.

Lens
Member
What they need
  • Act for a child without guesswork
  • Apply to camp and register the family
  • Know what a partner or step-parent may do
  • Know, before applying, who will be with the child at camp and what screening ran on them
  • Send a birth or a graduation to the magazine once, through the city correspondent
  • Declare a life event for the household's minors and see my household's relatives as any member does (R6, D33, D35; P28 §5)
What the platform does for them
  • R6's four authority paths and enumerated powers
  • A release list writable only by a standing holder
  • Life-event declarations queued to the committee
  • The camp page states the screening the camp runs, once a policy is adopted (Q-86, Q-165)
  • A correspondent's entry for my household prints on the correspondent's recorded attestation that we agreed, and I can also submit for us myself (D89)

Rules that protect them

  • The more protective answer wins when holders disagree (D35)
  • A step-parent holds nothing until named (D34)
  • A child's announcement is withdrawable and re-asked at eighteen (D33)
  • Screening runs before contact with campers once the Board adopts a policy; the platform never screens (Q-86)
  • A minor on a programme's volunteer team (aged 16–17) has no rule outside camp (Q-191)
  • Campers travelling from Ramallah have no written consent or travel rule (Q-166)
  • A correspondent enters a household's life event on a recorded attestation that the family agreed (D89)
  • A minor is never a contact; a child at a club event is a line under the household's authority (P21 §3; R7)
  • No interest row, seat or own channel is recorded for a seventeen-year-old, and a minor narrator's archive consent is the household adult's, re-asked at eighteen (P25 §6; P27 §6; R6)
  • A step-parent's relatives never enter a child's own branch (D71; P28 §5)
