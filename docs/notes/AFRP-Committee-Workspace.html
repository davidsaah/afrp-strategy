The committee workspace and reporting

Every body the by-laws constitute as one object — its seats, its meetings, its decisions, its reports — and the Board packet assembled from them

Source: design/AFRP-Committee-Workspace.md in the public repository. The design record outranks this rendering only in the sense that the file is the record (D41); the page is generated from it.

Every body the by-laws constitute as one object — its seats, its meetings, its decisions, its reports — and the Board packet assembled from them

Design note P1 · 1 October 2026 · for the Hub's governance, reporting and authority modules and a new committees module

This is the note the master plan's blocked row "board workspace + committees (design note)" has waited for since the Hub refused to build a board workspace without one (plan/MASTER-PLAN.md §1a; AFRP-Delivery-Status.md, "Not built, and said so"). It rests on three sources of different rank: the 2024 Constitution and By-Laws (Article VIII and the clauses that compose the Board and the Councils); the 2020–21 Strategic Planning Committee Recommendations ("Federation 2.0", FED2), whose Part IV on administrative infrastructure holds the committee chair and member descriptions (§E), the chair guidelines with the thirty-day rule for minutes (§H) and the two reporting templates (§I), and which the minutes of 2021–22 record as approved at the 2021 Jacksonville General Assembly; and the Federation's working files read in September and October 2026 (INTAKE26, D54), which show how committees are seated, minuted and reported today. The crosswalk rows it answers are A3, B8, B9, B10, C4 and H1; it is also the "committee register" the directory note (P19 §4) defers to, and the body the club plan's officers form seats (club plan §4.1 step 2).

Precedence (D41). Where this note and a by-law text disagree, the text wins. Where it and the Decisions Register disagree, the register wins. FED2 is a recommendation adopted by a General Assembly vote whose minutes are not on file (crosswalk, FED2 row); every rule this note takes from it is labelled FED2 and is a Board-adopted guideline, not a by-law. The prototype's #/fed/dashboard and the 21 August Board packet (AFRP-Board-Packet.html, kept as a dated record under D49) evidence a story, never a rule. The Hub's governance and reporting modules show what is built and never what should be. Where the record is silent this note says so and raises a question; it picks no default.

Public-repo rule. No living person is named. No committee's budget or balance appears. Committees are named as the by-laws name them; the affiliates' committees as their by-laws and policies name them.

In the by-laws. The Constitution names fourteen standing committees (Const. Art. VIII §1) and By-Law 8.2.1 names fifteen, adding the Government Affairs Committee, which the unnumbered "Governmental Affairs Committee" paragraph under 8.15 establishes, with its chair and members appointed by the Board. The President, the Board or the General Assembly may create any committee (8.1.1, 8.15.1). Every committee member must have been a member in good standing during the year before appointment and remain so, with their club if any (8.1.2). Standing committee members serve one year and are appointed by the President, after consulting the Committee on Appointments if necessary, "except as otherwise specified"; the President appoints every chairperson except as otherwise specified (8.1.3). The exceptions are in §2.2 below. The President is an ex officio member of all committees (7.2.1). The Executive Director oversees "the day to day operations of all the A.F.R.P. appointed committees" (7.9.2). The Executive Assistant takes the minutes of Board and Executive Committee meetings and keeps the records (7.10.2); the Executive Secretary has custody of all files and records and takes those minutes in the Assistant's absence (7.4.1). The Board meets at least six times a year including the Mid-Year and the Convention and transacts business by "a majority of those present" (10.3.1); the two Councils meet at least twice a year on the same test (10.4.1, 10.5.1); the Board may be polled, and a member who does not reply within the time set abstains (6.2.4), passage being a simple majority of votes cast (7.9.10). Committee chairpersons or a representative attend the Mid-Year (10.2.1 D). Robert's Rules govern where the texts are silent (12.1). The by-laws state no quorum for any committee and no rule about committee minutes, reports or absences; those come from FED2.

In FED2 (Part IV, administrative infrastructure). §E gives job descriptions for the chair and a member of the Constitution, Finance and Selection Committees (and for the officers): monthly virtual meetings; attendance at the Mid-Year and the Convention required of a chair; the chair, "acting in concert with the Deputy President, in consultation with the President", to address a member who accrues more than two unexcused absences in the Federation year, July to July — the consequence being a conversation about fit, not a removal. The Board welcome letter in §C states the same two-absence test per calendar year for Board members, with the commitment "reevaluated". §H, the chair guidelines: chairs develop an annual plan with goals, timelines, outcomes, responsibilities and meeting frequency; set an agenda before every meeting, each item marked discussion or action with its presenter; approve meeting reports before distribution; report in writing and orally to the Board; relay Board decisions to the committee; the written report of a meeting carries date, time, place, chair, present and absent, every motion and its outcome, every decision with follow-up actions and deadlines, a brief summary without attributing comments, and the next meeting; "meeting reports should be prepared as soon as possible (and within 30 days) after the meeting". The staff (then the Executive Administrator and the Program Coordinator) set meetings, send reminders, help with agendas and minutes, orient new members, "provide access to the Federation database to review existing committee documents and … upload future documents", "remind chairs when quarterly reports are due" and "update the Federation database on committee members and their term limits". §I gives the two templates: the quarterly report (meeting dates, agendas, attendees; current projects; a quarterly income and expense statement of actual against budget — deposits, expenses, balance; challenges needing help; other updates) and the committees and programme report (members; mission; short-term and long-term goals; chair and co-chairs; current projects; the meetings of the past six months and the next; challenges; comments). The crosswalk cites this content as "FED2 §C"; it is Part IV §E, §H and §I of the revision of 3 May 2021, and the crosswalk's citation should be corrected.

Historically (the intakes, role-level). The Federation printed an annual Committee Directory listing every committee with chair, usually a co-chair, and members, and the Board members eligible to vote (the officer archive; 6.3.1 requires the Past Presidents' list to be printed in it); who maintained it is not recorded, and it continued to 2026 as a drive file. Appointments ran as a summer roster: the President appoints chairs, the Selection Committee vets from 2021–22, and ARFECF's President and Board approve a roster in July and August. Since August 2026 the Federation keeps a five-tab "overview" sheet per committee and programme — Overview (name, mission, active committee, key links, key outcomes), Budget for the year, Committee List (name, title, tenure, contact), Calendar of Events — on a shared drive with a folder per committee; in the sheets read, mission and outcomes were mostly blank and the budget tab empty or a single band. The Selection Committee's strategy meeting of September 2026 set that chairs get defined budgets, run their committees independently and report to the Board; that the programme director sends monthly reminders to chairs before the January Mid-Year; and that a calendar of mandatory Mid-Year and Convention business is being finalised. The Board's reporting rhythm, from a 2017 agenda to the 2024 General Assembly minutes, is reports at each Board meeting from the President, the Deputy President, each programme and committee chair, the four treasurers' and fund reports (General Fund, Education Fund, Endowment and investments, ARFECF) and ARFHSN; the office (the Executive Administrator in the 2020 job description) assembles and sends the Board package, digital and printed, with those reports, bank reconciliations and the agenda. FED2's quarterly report is not evidenced as ever practised: the archive's own list of unfinished 2021 items ends with "quarterly reporting", and the only quarterly reporting minuted is the 2021 decision to centralise the Medical Mission's and ARFHSN's financial reporting in the office with quarterly reports for donors and the CPA. Minutes are thin: the Federation's Meeting Minutes folder was empty when read; Board minutes exist for roughly half the meetings of 2020–2026 and committee minutes for a handful of committees; ARFHSN's six-monthly report to the AFRP Board (its by-laws, Art. III §7) is minuted as presented once, in 2023; the Investment Committee reports to the ARFECF Board at least yearly under its policy. The September 2026 Board minutes apply an attendance rule to Board members at large — losing voting status for missing three consecutive Mid-Years and three of the last five Conventions, with reinstatement by Board vote — a test By-Law 6.3.1 writes for the Council of Past Presidents only; the minutes record that its interpretation and enforcement are for the by-law review.

The shape of the problem. Three things exist and do not meet: the by-laws say who sits and who appoints; FED2 says how a committee should run and report; the drive holds what committees actually produced, unevenly. The platform's job is the register (who sits, by what authority, until when), the clocks (minutes in thirty days, the report when due, the term's end, the decision's owner) and the assembly (the report from what the workspace already knows; the Board packet from the reports). It is not the archive (D66) and it is not the minutes.

The by-laws know a chairperson and members (8.1.3); co-chairs exist in every Committee Directory and in the committee overview sheets, and no text creates them (Q-51). The authority on each seat is the clause that gives it, read from the body's rule, never typed free:

Where the text names the appointing authority, the seat is created only with that authority's dated act recorded (a President's appointment letter, a Board minute, an election result from the voting module, a Convention election, the officers form). Where the text is silent — a created committee under 8.1.1, a co-chair, the Young Leader Board seat — the seat is a register row naming who seated it and when, labelled "no appointing text", exactly as DIR2 does for the four designated Board seats. Article XVII makes the Selection Committee "responsible for screening, vetting and approving every individual recommended for a vacant Federation seat, including standing committee chairs, committee members"; the seat therefore carries a vetted by the Selection Committee on date, empty until the Selection workflow (P5, slice SC1) exists, and a seat without it is shown as such, not refused (Q-75).

A seat ends on its term's end date, computed from the authority's term rule (one year from appointment under 8.1.3; the staggered cohorts of 8.5.1 and 17.1.3; the Convention-to-Convention term of 7.1.2), or on a dated resignation or removal (13.1, a human act the platform records and never performs). The office is reminded ninety days before a standing committee's seats expire, in time for the summer roster — a clock the FED2 staff duties ask for ("update the Federation database on committee members and their term limits"; crosswalk B10). A term limit (17.1.3's six years; the 2015 policy's two three-year terms for the Scholarship Committee, with holdover counting, EC-OV-20) refuses a reappointment by name. The end of a seat is a vacancy, and the vacancy list is what the Selection Committee works from (17.1.4 "advertise and recruit"); this note creates the list, P5 the workflow.

2.3 Standing

Every AFRP committee member must have been in good standing in the year before appointment and remain so, with their club if any (8.1.2); every Board member must be in good standing of the Federation and their club (6.2.3); a committee member who loses standing is not automatically removed by any text — 13.2 removes an elected officer who loses standing and has the President replace them. The platform applies 8.1.2 as a refusal at appointment, by name, using the standing the CRM already computes (scoped, with D1's collection-date rule and D6's dormancy override); after appointment it shows a lapse beside the seat and raises a follow-up to the chair and the office, and removes nobody. For an elected officer it raises the same follow-up citing 13.2 to the President. Design choice, labelled: the platform never ends a seat for a lapse; it tells the person the text names. The Council of Past Presidents' active voting status (three of the preceding five Conventions attended and no three consecutive Mid-Years missed, 6.3.1) is computed from Convention and Mid-Year attendance once events hold it (B2) and offered to the President, who "each year shall compile a list"; the platform's list is a draft the President adopts, and reinstatement is the Board's two-thirds vote, recorded as a decision. The September 2026 practice of applying that test to Board members generally is recorded as rule-against-practice and not built (Q-53).

2.4 The majority test and quorum

The by-laws give the Board and the two Councils one test — "a majority of those present (in person or otherwise)" — and the annual meeting a quorum of twenty-five paid members (10.1.5). No committee has a quorum in any text. Robert's Rules govern where the texts are silent (12.1), and under them a committee's quorum is a majority of its members unless its creating body set another; whether 12.1 reaches committee meetings is a reading the Legal Advisor should give (Q-52). Until then a body's majority-test row is filled only where a text fills it; a committee's row is empty, the workspace records who attended and shows "no quorum rule on file", and a decision the committee records carries its vote count without the platform saying whether the meeting was competent. Nothing is computed from a rule nobody wrote (Breeze Parity §4: nothing automatic without a named rule).

2.5 The vote property and the directory

Whether a seat votes on its body is a property of the seat with the clause cited (6.2.2 for the Board; 7.1.1 for the two non-voting employees; 10.6.1 for the Mid-Year; the Chairman's tie-break at ARFHSN, HS-OV-6), never the person's franchise (P19 §4; David, 1 October 2026). The leadership directory (DIR2) reads its committee seats from this register and retires the office's interim register the day CW1 is live. The Committee Directory on the drive is the archive of record for every year before the platform and the evidence for the first load, which the office types from it and never imports (P19 §4; D66).

2.6 Age mix

Crosswalk B12 ("young people on every committee") is a by-law matter; the register can report each body's age mix as bands under the suppression floor (S7, R43), computed from the household record's dates and shown only as counts — design choice, offered to the Strategic Plan Committee for the plan's outcome measures (D42), not switched on by this note.

3. The workspace

One page per body, under its entity's lens, read by its seats, its staff liaison, the President as ex officio member of every AFRP committee (7.2.1), the Executive Director for AFRP's appointed committees (7.9.2), and the Deputy President for the bodies he or she chairs (6.4.1, 7.3.3) (the Executive Director's read is by 7.9.2 as a default; P16 Q-56 asks whether the office map should assign it per body). A member of one committee reads nothing of another (Four-Lens §6: a grant is scoped). Six objects live on it.

3.1 The annual plan

FED2 §H: a plan with goals, timelines, outcomes, responsibilities and meeting frequency. The plan is not typed here twice: for a programme committee it is the programme's plan on the register (master plan slice P1's eight headings, which a committee revises with attribution), and for every body it is the current cycle of the planning survey (design note P2), from which the mission, the short-term and long-term goals and the budget estimate are read. The workspace shows the plan's state (not started, submitted, confirmed) and links to it.

3.2 Meetings

A meeting is an event scoped to the body (B2 as built: one event row, one scope), with the body's seats as the eligible set, attendance taken on the day, in person or by video, and the Mid-Year and Convention sessions of the Board and the Councils cross-listed from the Convention's and Mid-Year's events. The agenda is a list of items, each with a title, a presenter, a flag discussion or action (FED2 §H), a link to the paper it concerns on the drive or to the decision item it will settle (§3.4), and a time; the staff liaison or the chair prepares it, and the chair approves it before it goes to the seats through the seat channel. Standing items a body's rule imposes (the Board's six meetings including the Mid-Year and the Convention, 10.3.1; the Councils' two, 10.4.1, 10.5.1; the Mid-Year's purpose, "to review A.F.R.P. activities, and to formulate proposals for the forthcoming Convention", 10.2.1; the calendar of mandatory Mid-Year and Convention business the Selection Committee is finalising, once filed) are seeded on the year's calendar as the office enters them, never invented.

3.3 Minutes

Minutes are a document; documents live on the drive (D66). The meeting record on the platform holds the structured facts FED2 §H lists — date, time, place or link, chair, present and absent, each motion with its outcome, each decision with its follow-up and deadline, the next meeting — as attendance rows, decision items and follow-ups, and holds one link to the written minutes in the body's drive folder. It holds no narrative. The thirty-day rule (FED2 §H: "prepared as soon as possible (and within 30 days)") is a clock: the meeting opens a minutes due follow-up to the chair and the staff liaison on the day, due thirty days later; filing the link closes it; the thirty-first day shows it overdue on the body's page and on the Federation lens (§5). The chair's approval of the report before distribution (FED2 §H) is a dated act on the link. For the Board and the Executive Committee the taker is the Executive Assistant, or the Executive Secretary in her or his absence (7.10.2, 7.4.1), and the follow-up goes to that seat.

Design choice, labelled: no free text about a person exists anywhere on the workspace — not in an agenda item, a decision, a follow-up or an attendance note — for the reason the club plan gives (Breeze Parity §4 r2): the committee keeps tasks and decisions, not opinions. A Membership Committee discussion of an applicant, a Selection Committee discussion of a candidate, a Scholarship Fund Committee discussion of a student are minuted on the drive under that body's own confidentiality, and the platform records only the decision in the terms the engine already uses (a membership class granted, a slate approved, an award made).

3.4 The decisions queue

A decision item is the unit of a body's business: a question with an owner (the body), a clock, the rule it concerns, where it came from, and an outcome. It comes from three places. From the platform, by R40: wherever the engine finds the by-laws silent or admitting two readings it names the committee — the Membership Committee and the Legal Advisor for 4.1.1's readings, the Membership Committee for a minor's club attachment (R4), the Camp committee and the Legal Advisor for a guardian's authority, the Constitution Committee for every drafting item the record raises (Q-40's 8.4.1-against-6.4.1, D3's Scholarship Committee amendment, the four-amendment package) — and today those questions are shown on screens and land nowhere; they now land here, one item each, on the named body's queue, carrying their Q-number (D43). From the record: an open question on the site's questions page whose owner is this body (the governance agent's numbering; D43's one number in both places). From the body itself: an item a seat raises, or an action item the chair records after a meeting. Outcomes: decided (the decision in one line, the vote count if taken, the minute link, the date — and, where the decision is one the register waits on, the governance agent writes the D-number from it, not the platform); referred to the Board (the item moves to the Board's queue and onto the next packet, §4.3); referred to another body; deferred with a date. A body sees its own queue; the Federation lens sees every open item's age and owner as a count and a list of items, never the content of a confidential one beyond its title (§5). The seven committees' absence of a design note (JF-073) was itself a refusal the engine named; this queue is where such refusals go once a body exists to receive them.

3.5 Follow-ups and attendance

Follow-ups are the club plan's object (a task about a person or a thing, with an owner and a date, closed and never deleted; Breeze Parity §4 r2): minutes due, report due, a seat expiring, a lapse beside a seat, an action item from a meeting assigned to a seat. Attendance is per meeting per seat — present, excused, unexcused — as the chair or the staff liaison marks it; the platform never infers "unexcused". The FED2 review is a rule-named follow-up: on a seat's third unexcused absence in the Federation year (July to July, FED2 §E) the platform opens a follow-up to the chair and the Deputy President, titled with the rule ("FED2 §E: more than two unexcused absences — review the member's commitment"), copied to the President as consultee. It is a prompt to a conversation the guideline describes; it ends nothing and changes no seat. For a Board member the §C letter states the same test per calendar year with the commitment "reevaluated"; the two periods disagree inside FED2, the by-laws hold neither, and the September 2026 Board applied a third rule (§2.3); the platform counts absences against the Federation year for committees and raises the Board case only when a Board says which rule it applies (Q-53). The Council of Past Presidents' 6.3.1 test is the one attendance rule a by-law holds, and §2.3 says how it is offered.

3.6 Money, documents and contact

Money. FED2 §I's quarterly report wants actual against budget; the September 2026 meeting wants "defined budgets" for chairs. A body therefore has a budget line on the funds ledger, in its entity's books: for a programme committee the programme's typed budget line (Program Architecture §3; crosswalk H5; D55 for the entity), for the Government Affairs Committee AFRP's General Fund (D65), for a fund committee its fund (6.3.4, 6.4.4), for other committees a General Fund line the Finance Committee proposes and the General Assembly adopts (8.7.1). Deposits, expenses and the balance against the line are read from the ledger; nothing on a report is typed. Which committees hold lines of their own, at what amounts, is the budget's to say each year (Q-55); the platform shows a committee with no line as "no budget line this year" and its report's money section empty and labelled.

Documents. The body's drive folder (D66): the register links it; the staff liaison maintains it; the FED2 duty to "provide access to the Federation database to review existing committee documents and … upload future documents" is met by the folder and by the registers the platform already holds, not by a document store in the platform. Papers for a meeting are agenda-item links.

Contact. The body's seat channel (P19 §4) — the committee's functional mailbox where one exists (the intake found programme committees with their own) or the office — is how a body is reached and how it reaches its seats; a seat's personal details are never in the workspace's payload (R27). A body writing to members at large goes through the comms desk under the member's consent and is not this note's.

3.7 The Board and the Executive Committee as bodies

The Board is an instance of §2–§3 with three particulars and no new machinery. Its seats are DIR2's 6.2.2 roster. Its meetings are the six of 10.3.1, with the Mid-Year as the Board convened under 10.2.1 and its attendance list (the Executive Committee, the Past Presidents, every chair or representative, a host-city committee member, the two employees, the Foundation's President and the Magazine's representative). Its polls (6.2.4, 7.9.10) run on the voting module's fixed-denominator poll as the rulesets already define it — non-reply is abstention and the deadline is stated at issuance (HS-OV-2's reasoning, applied to AFRP's own text), a poll called by the Executive Assistant or a designated Executive Committee member, or on request of five Board members — and the workspace only places the poll on the Board's queue and records its result as a decision; the vote-count rules, EC-OV-11's and HS-OV-1's denominators, and the Board's two-thirds-of-all votes (6.2.1 for Rules and Regulations; 6.3.1 for reinstatement) are the rulesets' and are not restated here. The Executive Committee is the same with 7.1.1's seats and the quarterly evaluation meetings of 7.9.6 and 7.10.5 as standing items. 4. Reporting

4.1 The quarterly committee report (FED2 §I)

Generated from the workspace, signed by the chair, not written. Sections and sources: the quarter's meetings with dates, agendas and attendees (§3.2, §3.5); current projects (the plan's goals, §3.1, and the decision items decided or open in the quarter, §3.4); the income and expense statement — deposits, expenses, balance, actual against budget — from the ledger (§3.6); challenges needing the Federation's help, and other updates, the two fields the chair types. The report is due on the quarter's end plus a parameter the office sets; the staff liaison's reminder (FED2 §H) is a follow-up to the chair opened on the quarter's last day; filing freezes the report as a dated record with its figures as at that day (the funnel snapshot's pattern, reporting step 5). Which quarters — the fiscal year's (June to May, 8.7.2), the calendar year's, or FED2's Federation year (July to July) — no text says, and practice shows reports at Board meetings rather than quarterly; the period is a parameter with no value until the Deputy President or the Executive Director sets it (Q-54), and until then the report runs on demand for any range.

4.2 The committees and programme report (FED2 §I)

The six-monthly form: members (the seats, §2.2); mission, short-term and long-term goals (the current survey cycle, P2); chair and co-chairs; current projects; the meetings of the past six months and the next (§3.2); challenges and comments (typed). It is the report chairs bring to the Mid-Year (10.2.1 D) and the Convention (the General Assembly agenda of 2024 lists reports from every committee and fund), so it is due twice a year on those dates; for a programme committee it carries the programme's counts for the period — the five rungs as the register defines them (D25) and, in year one, the D67 baseline counts — appended from the reporting module and never typed.

5. What the Federation lens sees

A committees page in the Federation registry, beside the clubs page (club plan §10.1), one row per body: seats filled and vacant, with the next expiry; the chair's seat and its vetting date; the last meeting and the next; whether the last meeting's minutes are filed (and days overdue if not); the last report filed and the next due; the budget line's balance against the line (money, not member data); open decision items and the oldest's age; the body's entity and lens. Three views: the Executive Director's and the President's (every AFRP body, every column; the President ex officio, 7.2.1); each chair's own row in full and the others as the page shows them; the Council of Chapter Club Presidents' view of the Membership Committee and its own row. The affiliates' bodies appear on their entity's page, and the AFRP page shows of them only the reports they owe the AFRP Board and whether filed. The vacancy list on this page is the Selection Committee's intake (17.1.4), and the expiring-seats list is the summer roster's. The page carries no personal contact detail and no minute's content; it is the Federation's view of whether its bodies are seated, meeting, minuted and reporting — which is what crosswalk A3's "core group that meets regularly" and B8–B10 asked for, made visible.

What the member lens sees of a committee: its seats on the leadership directory (DIR2), its seat channel, and — if the Strategic Plan Committee wants it — its mission and goals from the survey as a public line (P2). A member sees no queue, no minutes link and no attendance.

6. Rules

  1. A body exists on the register only with its constituting clause, or as a created committee with its creating act recorded (8.1.1, 8.15.1).
  2. A seat is created only with its appointing authority's dated act, as the body's text names that authority (8.1.3 and the exceptions in §2.2); where no text names one, the seat is labelled "no appointing text" with who seated it (P19 §4, DIR2).
  3. Appointment to an AFRP committee refuses, by name, a person not in good standing in the prior year or not in good standing of their club (8.1.2); a Board seat refuses on 6.2.3. A lapse after appointment is shown and raised, never acted on (design choice; 13.1 and 13.2 are human acts).
  4. A term ends on the date the authority's rule gives; a term limit refuses reappointment by name (17.1.3; the 2015 policy with EC-OV-20).
  5. The Selection Committee's approval of a seat is a dated field (17.1.1), empty until SC1 (P5) and shown as such.
  6. A body's majority test is the clause that gives it (10.3.1, 10.4.1, 10.5.1) or empty; the platform computes no quorum from a rule no text holds (Q-52; Breeze Parity §4).
  7. Whether a seat votes is a property of the seat with its citation, never the person's franchise (P19 §4).
  8. Minutes are a drive link (D66) with a thirty-day clock (FED2 §H); the platform holds attendance, motions as decisions and follow-ups, and no narrative; no free text about a person exists on the workspace (design choice, Breeze Parity §4 r2).
  9. Every question the engine routes under R40 lands as a decision item on the named body's queue with its Q-number (D43), and nowhere else.
  10. The third unexcused absence in the Federation year raises a rule-named review follow-up (FED2 §E) and changes no seat; the Past Presidents' 6.3.1 status is a draft list for the President and the Board's two-thirds vote.
  11. A report's money is the ledger's; a report's meetings are the workspace's; only challenges and comments are typed (FED2 §I).
  12. The Board packet is frozen as a dated record and filed on the drive, which is the archive (D66, D49).
  13. ARFHSN's six-monthly report is the one thing of ARFHSN's the AFRP Board's packet carries (D63; R39).
  14. A body lives under its entity's lens; the federation scope reads an affiliate body's reports and nothing of its store (R39, Four-Lens §6).
  15. Nothing automatic without a named rule; every refusal names its rule.
