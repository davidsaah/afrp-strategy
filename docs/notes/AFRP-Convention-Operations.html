3. The award

3.1 The rotation and the forfeiture reading

What the text says and does not. 10.1.1 gives a rotation "between these three regions", a three-year bar on returning to a region, and one forfeiture sentence: "If a region elects not to host a Convention during its turn in the rotation, it will forfeit the right until the next rotation." It does not say who determines whose turn it is, what happens when a region fields no bid at all or only bids that fail the fifty-for-five test, or what "until the next rotation" means when two regions forfeit in the same cycle. The 2026 Assembly acted on the Legal Advisor's reading that a region with no qualified bid has, in effect, elected not to host, so the turn passed to the next region. That is a reasonable reading and it is a reading: "elects not to host" can also be read as requiring a region's clubs to decline, which a region with no qualifying club cannot do. Under R40 the platform holds both and decides neither (Q-240).

What P8 already builds. HB1 shows each bid's region under 10.1.1 and the three-year check; HB2 shows forfeiture and leaves the Assembly to decide what follows. This note adds the object HB2 needs to show it honestly:

  • The rotation turn. For each Convention year a row on the events register: the region whose turn it is (as a register row the office sets from the text, never computed from a club's ARFECF region; P8 §2), the last three host regions, the bids received for that year with their eligibility results from HB1, and a turn state: bids qualified, no qualified bid, declined by the region (a dated statement from the region's clubs, if any is made), forfeited (only by the Assembly's dated act), passed to region X (only by the same act). The platform never moves a turn by itself.
  • The reading cited. When the Assembly acts on a reading of 10.1.1 — as in 2026 — the act records the reading's source (the Legal Advisor's written opinion, a drive link, D66) beside the clause, labelled reading, never rule. A later year's turn row shows that an earlier reading exists and does not apply it automatically.
  • The vote. 9.1.1(2) requires delegate voting for "the Convention city, where a contest exists"; where one qualified bid stands, 9.1.2 allows voice or raised hands. HB2's floor motion therefore takes its mode from the count of qualified bids: two or more → a weighted delegate vote on the floor as built; one → a voice or hands vote recorded with its outcome; none → no motion, the turn state no qualified bid and whatever the Assembly decides on the record. Only Regular Members in good standing vote (10.1.1), so the floor's roll is the certified roll (§6).
  • What the Assembly may not do on the floor. The 2026 advice that the Assembly could not vote a different city than a qualified bid is the Legal Advisor's; the platform records it as the reading attached to that year's act. Placing a city "in the rotation without a competing bid" (the 2029 path) is either an amendment of 10.1.1 under 16.1.1 or something else; that is Q-223, and until it is answered the platform offers only the text's path: a bid in writing by the Mid-Year, tested under HB1, decided by the Assembly.
  • The hosts through 2030. The recorded hosts are award records with their source (the Assembly's minutes, where on file) and the turn state the office can attest. Whether the schedule fits 10.1.1's three-year bar is Q-89; the rows show what the text would say and change nothing.

3.2 A fourth hosting model: the Federation as host, abroad

P13 §7 offers three templates (host city, hybrid, Federation as central host) and the Mid-Year. The 2026 checklist describes a fourth: a Convention outside the United States, hosted by the Federation itself if need be, gated by a requirements checklist with stop conditions. It is not adopted (status "hold / feasibility only"; Q-223), so the platform offers it as a template with every gate's threshold blank, exactly as P13 offers the other three, and adopts none of its content.

The template's rows (on the agreement row of P13 §2, class CONV68, with the host recorded as the Federation and, where a local body takes part, that body as a party under P11 §3):

  • Gate rows. One row per gate group — Board, legal and financial approval; security assessment; counsel; insurance (event, evacuation, cancellation, per-attendee); cancellable contracts; government liaison and advisory monitoring; entry and attendee requirements; transport and access; venue, security and medical readiness; final readiness — each with an owner seat, evidence required (a drive link and a filed date, D66), a state (not started, in progress, met, not met) and the date last reviewed. P13 §2.8's evidence pattern, extended.
  • The advisory threshold. A typed field holding the government travel-advisory level at or above which the Convention may not go ahead, and the current level with its date, entered by the owner seat from the State Department's published advisory (four levels, from "exercise normal caution" to "do not travel"; Sources). The checklist says the threshold is one "no Board vote may waive". Whether the Board adopts that — a self-binding rule the platform would then enforce by refusing a GO while the current level is at or above the threshold — is part of Q-223. Until it is adopted the field is blank and the platform shows the level beside every decision and refuses nothing.
  • The review clocks. Readiness reviews at the intervals the checklist sets, as dated items on the owner's queue; an overdue review is shown to the Board, never assumed passed.
  • The decision. A dated act with one of six outcomes — GO, GO WITH CONDITIONS (each condition a row with an owner and a clock), HOLD, RELOCATE, POSTPONE, CANCEL — the authorising body or seat, and the next review date. The written stop authority the checklist asks the Board to give is a delegation row: which seat may HOLD or CANCEL between Board meetings, by the Board's dated act; blank means only the Board's own act decides.
  • What the decision does downstream. RELOCATE opens a new agreement version (P13 §2.9) for the new place; POSTPONE moves the event dates and re-runs D7's disclosure gate and the refund terms; CANCEL closes registration and opens the refund run under the refund terms published before opening (§5.3). Dues paid with registration are membership dues for the year (5.1, 5.2's "separate from, and in addition to"); the platform records them as dues and the cancellation's refund run does not touch them. Whether a member may ask for dues back when the Convention is cancelled is not written (Q-244, which this note extends to cover it); registration refunds follow the published terms; a deficit reads from the agreement (Q-129). What happens to the Annual Meeting — the elections, the budget, the amendments — when the Convention it is "held during" (10.1.1) does not happen is not in the text (Q-244); the platform shows the meeting as not held and holds every process that waits on it.
  • Publication gates for a Convention abroad. Registration may not open until the refund terms are published on the ticket page (the checklist; alongside D7's gate) and the written risk disclosure is attached to the registration, acknowledged by each adult registrant as a dated consent row, and by the person acting for a minor under R6. These are the checklist's own terms; they apply only to an event started from this template, and only once Q-223 adopts them. Until then the template carries them as rows labelled checklist and the platform shows them unmet.
  • The hedge. A large Mid-Year in a Convention city in the same year is an ordinary Mid-Year under P8 and 10.2.1 (which limits the Mid-Year to "the USA"); the platform needs nothing new for it.

Whether only an affiliated club may bid and whether the Federation may host with no bidding club is Q-91; which models a bid may propose is Q-94; this template joins them under Q-223.

3.3 The Mid-Year

P8 owns the Mid-Year's bids, its decision and its meeting; P13 its agreement. The 2020–21 guideline, now on file, has the Executive Committee review applications received by February of the year before and announce the selection at the general meeting, club presidents present their case at the previous Mid-Year, and a sharp fee rise is approved in writing by the Executive Committee; 2026–27 practice had the Board choose after quotes, the Federation sign the hotel contract and the Executive Committee set the fee (Q-92, which the research round extended). Two points belong to operations and are built in this note's slices: the Mid-Year's membership gate (§5.2, Q-225) and the fee approval — a fee that the guideline would call a sharp rise is a decision item on the Executive Committee's queue (CW4) with its written approval as a dated act, labelled guideline, offered and not required until Q-92 is answered. The Mid-Year's money that does not reach the General Fund (its revenue line is at zero; a separate Mid-Year account was reported by the Federation's treasurer in earlier years) is Q-92's sponsorship-and-account part, carried.

4. The host agreement

P13 is the design; HB3 and HA1–HA6 are its slices. This note adds nothing to the agreement's terms except three items the 2026 close showed, each of which P13's own rules already govern:

  1. Netted items. The 2026 statement netted a sum the host owed the Federation against the release and charged a Federation-hosted dinner to the host's side, neither with a schedule row. P13 §2.3: "what no one may do is settle an item that has no row". The platform keeps that rule. A netted item is entered on the close (§9) as its own line, each way, naming the schedule row it rests on; a netted item with no row is listed and held, naming Q-124, and the rest of the statement settles (P13 §4's query pattern). An agreement version that adds the row (P13 §2.9), or the Executive Committee's dated act under the dispute term (P13 §5), releases it as a new line naming the first. Nothing else releases it.
  2. Processing fees. The registration system's "due to local club" figure deducts the card cost per payment; the record's statement never nets fees into revenue (QuickBooks spec §3.1) and P13 §4 has no fee line. Which side bears the card cost on Convention money is a term kind the agreement may state (processing fees: Federation / host / shared as stated / not stated), read by the statement as a reimbursable line under §2.3 when stated and printed as not stated otherwise. 5. Registration and the desk

5.1 The product set, and the unified registration

The worksheet (P13 §2.4) already lets a host define a base registration "with or without banquet" and sub-events whose inclusion is mandatory, optional or mandatory until full. The 2027 decision — a unified registration including the banquet, decided by the Board in May 2026 — is that worksheet with the banquet sub-event mandatory. The platform builds it so, with three consequences it states on the event:

Which rule applies is the host's and the office's choice on the worksheet; the platform offers both and picks neither.
  • Banquet-only tickets (the guideline: at the host's discretion, for the Mid-Year) are a separate product only where the worksheet defines one.
  • Who set the fee. A ticketed event is non-deductible entirely and a gift beyond the ticket is receipted separately by its entity (D9). 7.4 What the tax law asks, and the record has not answered

Corporate sponsorship of an exempt organisation is excluded from unrelated business income only as a qualified sponsorship payment: a payment for which the sponsor receives no "substantial return benefit" beyond the use or acknowledgement of its name or logo; advertising — a message that promotes the sponsor with comparative language, prices, endorsements or inducements — is a substantial return benefit (IRC 513(i) and its regulations; IRS guidance in Sources). A paid page in the ad book and a booth are exchanges; a lanyard bearing a logo may be an acknowledgement; a package with entries, a table and an advertisement mixes all three. Separately, a 501(c)(4) above a gross-receipts threshold must state in each fundraising solicitation that contributions to it are not deductible as charitable contributions (IRC 6113). Whether the Federation's Convention sponsorship packages, ad-book pages and booths are treated as sponsorship or advertising, which part of a package is a gift receipted by an affiliate, and whether the Federation's Convention solicitations must carry the 6113 statement, are for the outside accountants and the Legal Advisor (Q-242). Until answered, the platform carries a kind on each sponsorship product (acknowledgement only, advertising, mixed, not stated) entered by the office, prints the kind on the statement, and claims nothing.
