A club's bid as an object, the decision that accepts it, the host agreement generated from it, and the settlement that closes it

Design note P8 · 1 October 2026 · for the Hub's events, clubs, ledger, voting and governance modules and the agreements register

Since 4 October 2026 (D99): the shared shapes this note draws — the cycle — are built once as the platform primitives of AFRP-Platform-Primitives.md (P29 §6); this note's rules are carried as their settings.

What it rests on. Crosswalk rows G1 ("Mid-Year guidelines: host bids ~13 months ahead, schedule, room block, fees"; FED2 §B) and G3 (the Convention MOU's terms), and the proposed notes P8 and P13. By-Law 10.1.1 (Convention locations chosen two years ahead; bids in writing by the Mid-Year; the bidding city's membership test; the three regions and the rotation), 10.1.6 (the Convention Contract Guidelines), 10.1.7 (the Convention Liaison and the 120-day accounting), and the whole of Article X for the Mid-Year (10.2.1, 10.3.1, 10.4.1, 10.5.1, 10.6.1). D59: the host agreement is the rule for every Convention, and "the same shape serves the Mid-Year". D57: the settlement is a line on the club's statement. The convention workflow (workflows.yaml), the club plan §5.2 (settlement line types) and §7.1 (the Convention row), the club journeys, and the Federation's working files on Conventions and Mid-Years read in September and October 2026 (INTAKE26, D54), at role level only.

Precedence (D41). Where this note and a by-law text disagree, the text wins. Where it and the Decisions Register disagree, the register wins. The 2022 host agreement template and the 2021 Mid-Year guidelines are the Federation's own documents, one an approved template and one a recommendation whose adopting vote has no minutes on file (crosswalk, FED2 row); every rule taken from them is labelled as such and is not a by-law. The prototype's #/convention evidences a story and never a rule. The Hub's events and voting modules show what is built and never what should be. Where the record is silent this note says so and raises a question; it picks no default.

Public-repo rule. No living person is named. No figure from an agreement, a budget or a sponsorship form appears; the by-laws' own numbers are cited as the text gives them. Clubs and host cities are named only where the record already names them (the regions in 10.1.1; the hosts through 2030 on the programme register).

1. What the record already holds

In the text. By-Law 10.1.1: the Annual Meeting is held during the Convention; "Convention locations are selected two (2) years in advance, by the general membership meeting during the Annual Convention"; only Regular Members in good standing vote; "any city bidding for a Convention must have at least 50 paid members for at least 5 consecutive years"; "a bid for a Convention must be submitted in writing by the Mid-Year Meeting to be considered by the General Assembly"; the Local Chapters are divided into three geographical regions, each listed by city; "each year the Convention location will rotate between these three regions"; "a Convention cannot return to a region within a three year period"; a region that elects not to host in its turn forfeits until the next rotation. 10.1.4: the Magazine's notice at least sixty days before. 10.1.5: twenty-five paid members are a quorum of the Annual Meeting. 10.1.6: "the rights, responsibilities and obligations of the host city and the A.F.R.P. are derived from the Convention Contract Guidelines". 10.1.7: the President appoints a Convention Liaison from the Council of Past Presidents to oversee pre-Convention planning and post-Convention accounting; the final accounting to the office within 120 days. 10.2.1: "the Board of Directors shall convene at a Mid-Year meeting to be held in Detroit, Michigan, or elsewhere in the USA as approved by the Executive Committee, in order to review A.F.R.P. activities, and to formulate proposals for the forthcoming Convention", attended by the Executive Committee, the Council of Past Presidents, every committee chair or a representative, "a Committee member from the forthcoming host Convention city", the two employees, the Foundation's President and the Magazine's representative. 10.3.1, 10.4.1, 10.5.1: the Board, the Past Presidents and the Club Presidents each meet at least so many times a year "inclusive of the Mid-Year and Convention". 10.6.1: "whether in the Mid-Year Meeting or in the regular or special meetings of the Board of Directors, only members of the Board shall have the right to vote". 6.5.2: a Member-at-Large candidate submits their name "during the Mid-Year Meeting". 6.3.1: a Past President stays an active voting member by attending three of the last five Conventions and not missing three consecutive Mid-Years. 5.2: dues and fees collected by the host are remitted and never count as the host's proceeds; the Treasurer gives the host the paid-members list thirty days before. 11.1.1: the Executive Committee, including the Legal Advisor, must approve the final language of any contract before execution. 3.1 and 3.2: a club affiliates with the Federation's approval and files its officers by 1 August. Nothing in the text says who may bid for a Mid-Year, by when, or by what vote it is chosen; nothing names a Mid-Year agreement; nothing says what "paid members" of a city means where the city has a club and members outside it.

Decided. D59: each Convention runs under a host agreement signed before it, recording the Federation's share, the holdback, the settlement date and each side's obligations; the platform records the terms on the event and produces the settlement statement; a change to the share goes from the Convention Innovation Committee to the Board, never the floor; the same shape serves the Mid-Year. D57: the settlement is a line on the club's statement. D1 consequence G: Convention money is Federation money at the first dollar. D4: digital voting is the direction; the 2024 text is the law until 9.2 is amended. D5 (reopened): when delegate weights fix. D66: the drive is the archive; the signed agreement is a drive link.

Designed. The convention workflow: nine steps from registration to settlement, with "record the host agreement" as a step (convention committee; host club president) and the close reading deficit responsibility from that year's agreement. The club plan §5.2: the Convention settlement line (AFRP → host club after the share, the holdback and any breach costs, raised by the event's close under the agreement, "never later than By-Law 10.1.7's 120 days"), the Mid-Year settlement "the same shape", the ad-book or sponsorship share (proposed, not decided; Q-47). The club plan §3.1: the host-bid form is on the club president's list and §4.1 step 6 names it. The club plan §7.1 Convention row: host committee seats are roles on the Federation's event. The agreements register (workflow partner-agreements; crosswalk F14) with the host agreement on it (convention workflow hand-off). Crosswalk G3: the Convention element and "class CONV68"; "a settlement statement is not in the record" — now the club plan §5.

Built. Events with one scope, registration, household registration, sub-events (the CRM worksheet's shape), the door and attendance (B2); the voting floor with weighted delegation and the S4 freeze; the funds ledger with the convention class. The Convention element itself (registration desk, credentialing, room block, on-site run, money) is "its own project" on the master plan's blocked row and is not built. No bid object, no host agreement row, no settlement statement exists.

Historically (the intakes, role-level). The approved host agreement template (2022 text): a host "elected two years in advance" by delegate vote at the Convention; "bids must request the MOU at the Mid-Year meeting two calendar years before"; signed at once on approval by the host's representative and the President or the Liaison, with the Deputy President also signing; the host's hotel choice approved by the Federation's convention committee (the Liaison and the Executive Committee) and the hotel contract by the Legal Advisor, copies of signed contracts to the Federation within days; registration only through the Federation's portal, with the host's event coordinator access and a worksheet before opening; the host represented by its club president, convention chair and co-chair; registration fees set by the Board at the Mid-Year; a fixed share, a per-registrant Relief Fund amount and dues as the Federation's; a holdback released within sixty days with an accounting; a convention bank account, duplicate receipts, the final accounting to the Executive Committee then the Finance Committee; the Federation's determination binding in a dispute; non-compliance "may count against future bids". No signed host agreement was found for any year; the 2024 General Assembly set the Federation's take by motion because none was signed. The Mid-Year guidelines (FED2, 2021): a club applies by email to the office by February, about thirteen months ahead; candidates are presented at the Mid-Year; the Executive Committee approves and the choice is announced at the Convention's General Assembly; the host provides rooms for the Board, the General Assembly, the Past Presidents and Leadership Ramallah, blocks a minimum of hotel rooms and a banquet hall, runs registration online with Federation staff, and agrees fees with the Federation's leadership, any sharp increase approved in writing by the Executive Committee. No written Mid-Year agreement was found for any year. In practice (the Mid-Year minutes 2023–2026 and the presidents' meetings): the Mid-Year host is a smaller club, chosen — the observer's note says — by the Board; the Federation runs the Board and General Assembly sessions and the registration system, the club runs the hotel contract, the social programme, the banquet and local sponsorship, with sponsorship cheques payable to the club and a separate Mid-Year account reported in the Federation's financial report; the Mid-Year is moving from March to January; members have proposed cancelling or shortening it; the committee has floated the Federation itself hosting in rotating cities. The presidents' meeting of August 2026 recorded the hosts through 2030, that one club was ruled ineligible for the 2028 vote "under rotation by-laws", and a proposal to place that club in the rotation for 2029 without competing bids under risk criteria the Board would set. The Convention Innovation Committee (2025–2026) named three hosting models — a host city; a hybrid of a smaller club with the Federation; the Federation as central host with local clubs soliciting sponsors for a percentage — discussed testing the hybrid on a small club, a central fund fed by clubs and the Federation to support small-city conventions, a convention handbook, and a formal bidding process; it noted that most of the smaller clubs have never hosted. The 2025 host wrote a lessons letter to future hosts naming the committee heads a host needs (hospitality, gift bags, transport, security, banquet, set-up, entertainment, young adults) and asking for a playbook. By-law texts on delegates, quorum and rotation "are not in these folders" (the intake's own note).

The shape of the problem. The by-laws say when a Convention bid is due and who votes; a template says what the host signs; a guideline says how a Mid-Year is applied for; and none of the three is a form anyone fills in on the platform or a record anyone can find afterwards. The settlement the club plan designed has nothing upstream of it. This note puts the bid, the decision and the agreement in one line so that the settlement has a source, and leaves every term where the record leaves it: in the agreement the Federation signs.

2. The bid

The object. A bid is a row on the Federation's events register, opened by a club president from the club lens (club plan §4.1 step 6), for one named meeting: the Convention of year N or the Mid-Year of year N. It carries: the bidding club; the city it proposes; the proposed dates and venue (a hotel or centre, as a name, with the room block the club expects to secure and the meeting rooms the meeting needs); a fees proposal (what the club proposes the base registration and banquet should be, to be set by the Board or agreed with the leadership — the bid proposes, it does not set); the host committee seats the club names (president, convention or Mid-Year chair, co-chair — the template's three — and any committee heads it already has); the club's statement of the money it expects to raise locally (sponsorship and ad book as kinds, no figure required); a link to the club's five-year plan on the drive if it has one (the club SOP; D66); the date the bid was submitted in writing; the hosting model the bid proposes where the Board allows more than one (§5). Each field is the club's statement; the office marks the bid received with a date. The written bid is the drive document; the platform row is the register entry pointing at it (D66).

Eligibility, shown before the bid is opened. Three tests, each from a text, each shown with its citation and refusing by name where the text refuses:

  1. Affiliated and in good standing for the year — the club's standing pill from the officers form (3.1, 3.2; C1). A club not affiliated cannot bid: 10.1.1 speaks of a city and 10.1.6 of a host city, but a Convention is "a function of the A.F.R.P." run under its guidelines, and only a Chapter Club complying with Article III sits on the Council (6.4.1); design choice, resting on 3.1 and 10.1.6, raised as Q-91 for the Board to confirm. A club in the Q-40 state (filed, approval pending) is shown as such and the bid is held, not refused.
  2. The membership test of 10.1.1 — "at least 50 paid members for at least 5 consecutive years" for a Convention bid. The platform computes it from the remittance ledger's collection dates (D1 consequence D: standing starts at collection) for every year it holds, and shows the count per year; for years before the platform's data begins the office attests the figure with a source and a date, and the bid says which years are computed and which attested. What "paid members" of a city means — members of the bidding club, or members whose address is in the city, including those in no club — is not in the text; the platform shows both counts and decides nothing (Q-90). The text sets no membership test for a Mid-Year bid; the platform shows the same counts for information and refuses nothing.
  3. The rotation of 10.1.1 — for a Convention bid, the region of the bidding city as 10.1.1 lists it, the regions of the last three Conventions, and whether the three-year rule would be met. 10.1.1 lists cities, not clubs: a club in a city the text does not list is shown as "in no region under 10.1.1" and the platform decides nothing about it (Q-89). The region for 10.1.1 is a register row the office sets from the text and is not the ARFECF region of the club register (club plan §6.8), which is a different scheme from a different by-law; the register carries both (the club plan §6.1 gains the row). The text sets no rotation for the Mid-Year; 10.2.1 names a default city and "elsewhere in the USA as approved by the Executive Committee".

The window. For a Convention: a bid in writing "by the Mid-Year Meeting" of year N−2 (10.1.1); the template says the bid requests the agreement at that Mid-Year. The platform opens the bid window for Convention N on the day Convention N−3 closes (design choice; the text gives only the close) and closes it on the Mid-Year N−2 date, shown on the club's calendar; a bid after the window is recorded as late and shown as such to whoever decides, because the text says "to be considered" and does not say refused. For a Mid-Year: FED2's guideline — an application by February, about thirteen months ahead, presented at that year's Mid-Year, approved by the Executive Committee, announced at the Convention. The platform carries the February date and the thirteen months as parameters labelled FED2, not as rule; the window closes nothing and shows the guideline's date against the bid's date.

Who sees a bid. The bidding club's president and officers; the office; the Executive Committee and the Convention Liaison; the Board; the Council of Chapter Club Presidents as the list of bids for the meeting (because the presidents are the Board, 6.2.2). A competing club sees that a bid exists for the meeting and the city, not its contents, until the decision is announced (design choice; the record is silent).

3. The decision

The Convention. 10.1.1: the location is selected "by the general membership meeting during the Annual Convention" two years ahead, and only Regular Members in good standing vote. The General Assembly votes through the delegate mechanism of 9.1.3 and 9.1.4 (weighted delegations; a certified member may extract their own vote), under the Annual Meeting's quorum (10.1.5), on the floor the voting module already runs (D4; the S4 freeze; D5 reopened on when weights fix). The platform puts the eligible bids on the floor as one motion per meeting — "the Convention of year N is awarded to the bid of [club], [city]" — with the eligibility tests of §2 shown beside each bid, and records the result against the bid: accepted, not accepted, withdrawn. The template's "elected … by delegate vote" and 10.1.1's "general membership meeting" are the same act. A region with no eligible bid in its turn is shown as forfeiting under 10.1.1's last sentence; the platform shows it and the Assembly decides what follows. Since 2 October 2026: the Convention operations note gives HB2 a rotation turn row for each Convention year (P22 §3.1, CV1). The row records the region in turn, the last three host regions, the bids with their HB1 results, and a turn state that only the Assembly's dated act moves. The 2026 Assembly acted on a reading that a region with no qualified bid has elected not to host. That reading is recorded beside the clause as a reading, not a rule (Q-240). The award motion takes its mode from the count of qualified bids: a delegate vote when the award is contested (9.1.1(2)), voice or hands when one bid stands (9.1.2), and no motion when there is none. The delegation that votes is the club's dated act with its method stated (P22 §6; Q-37). Placing a city such as Ramallah (2029) in the rotation without a competing bid is Q-223. P22 §3.2 offers it as a fourth hosting model, a template with every gate blank.

The Mid-Year. 10.2.1 gives the Executive Committee the approval of the Mid-Year's place when it is not the default city. FED2's guideline agrees: the Executive Committee approves and the Convention's General Assembly hears the announcement. The observer's note on practice says the Board chooses, and the 2026 committee floated the Federation hosting in rotating cities. The platform records the Mid-Year decision as a dated act of the Executive Committee (a minute number, a poll on the voting module's fixed-denominator polls — 6.2.4 and 7.9.10 as the Committee Workspace note reads them), with the Board's confirmation as a second dated act where the Board takes one, and shows the announcement as a General Assembly item. It does not decide which body is right (Q-92). Until that is answered the decision row names the body that made it, as every seat with "no appointing text" does. In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026), the guideline now on file (2020–21) has the Executive Committee review applications received by February of the year before. Club presidents present their case at the previous Mid-Year, and a sharp fee rise is approved in writing by the Executive Committee. In 2026–27 the Board chose after quotes, the Federation signed the hotel contract, and the Executive Committee set the fee; see Q-92. Since 2 October 2026: P22 §3.3 carries the fee approval as an Executive Committee decision item labelled guideline, offered and not required until Q-92 is answered. It also gives each Mid-Year a membership-gate parameter with its source, without which the event cannot publish (P22 §5.2; Q-225).

After the decision. The accepted bid's club gains the host role on the meeting's event row (club plan §7.1: host committee seats are roles on the Federation's event), the Convention Liaison seat is created for the President to fill from the Council of Past Presidents (10.1.7; CW1), a "Committee member from the forthcoming host Convention city" is expected at the next Mid-Year (10.2.1) and is a seat on that meeting's attendance list, and the host agreement is generated (§4). A bid not accepted is kept as a record with its result; the template's "non-compliance may count against future bids" is a fact the Executive Committee may record against a past agreement (§4) and the platform shows beside a new bid, never a refusal.

4. The host agreement, generated from the bid

The rule. D59: every Convention runs under a host agreement signed by the Federation and the host club before the Convention, recording the Federation's share, the holdback, the settlement date and each side's obligations; the platform records the terms on the event and produces the settlement statement; the same shape serves the Mid-Year. 10.1.6: the host's and the Federation's rights and obligations derive from the Convention Contract Guidelines — a document the intakes did not find (Q-93). 11.1.1: the Executive Committee, including the Legal Advisor, approves the final language before execution.

The object. An agreement row on the agreements register (workflow partner-agreements; crosswalk F14), of class CONV68 for a Convention and the same class for a Mid-Year, created from the accepted bid: the parties (the Federation; the host club, represented by the seats the bid named); the meeting; the Federation's share (an amount or a formula, as the signed text says); the per-registrant amounts the agreement sends to a fund (the template's Relief Fund amount — the fund's holding entity is not stated, crosswalk F15; since 3 Oct 2026 it is AFRP, D83); the holdback and the release period; the settlement date; the final-accounting date (never later than 120 days after a Convention, 10.1.7); the obligations of each side as a list of named cost lines with the side that bears each (the template's lists: meeting rooms, insurance, security, the keynote to a ceiling, the symposium split, the youth programme, the employees' rooms, and the rest); the registration rule (only through the Federation's portal; the host's coordinator access; the worksheet before opening); the hotel approval (the Federation's convention committee; the Legal Advisor on the contract); the ad-book and solicitation rules (one book per family; no solicitation outside the host's community; no causes the Federation has not approved — 11.1.4 by analogy); the dispute rule (the Executive Committee final); the approval of final language (11.1.1, a dated act); the signatures (the host's representative; the President or the Liaison; the Deputy President where the template has them) as dated acts; and the drive link to the signed text (D66). Every money term is a field on the row, typed from the signed agreement by the office, so that the settlement reads the agreement and never a template or a resolution: the 2024 case — a take set by floor motion because nothing was signed — is exactly what D59 forbids and what this row makes visible when empty.

Terms the agreement may express and the record has not decided. The Convention Innovation Committee's three models are term sets, not rules: who runs registration and the programme, who raises sponsors and ads, and what share of each flows which way. A host-city agreement has the host run the meeting and raise locally; a hybrid has the Federation plan with the club raising; a central-host agreement has the Federation host with clubs soliciting sponsors for a percentage. The platform offers each as a template the Executive Committee may start an agreement from, with every amount blank, because D59 puts a change to the share on the Convention Innovation Committee's path to the Board and nowhere else. Which models the Board allows a bid to propose, and whether the Federation may host with no bidding club (10.1.1 assumes a bidding city; 10.2.1 names a default city for the Mid-Year), is Q-94. The central fund for small-city conventions is a fund on the funds ledger if the Board creates one, with its holding entity and its rule to be stated (D55's pattern); it is not designed here.

The Mid-Year's agreement. D59 makes the same shape serve; the record holds no Mid-Year agreement for any year and no Mid-Year guideline on money beyond fees agreed with the leadership. In practice the host club keeps registration through the Federation's portal, signs the hotel contract, takes sponsorship cheques payable to itself and sees a Mid-Year account reported by the Federation's treasurer. Under D1's four treatments, platform-collected registration is Federation money and off-platform sponsorship is the club's; an agreement would say whether any share moves either way. The platform refuses to produce a Mid-Year settlement with no agreement row — it shows "no agreement on record for this meeting" and the club's statement carries no Mid-Year line until one exists (club plan §5.2; a line type the record holds, raised by the event's close under the agreement). Whether every Mid-Year must have a signed agreement, or only one where money moves between the Federation and the host, is Q-92.

5. The settlement

Designed in the club plan §5.2 and §5.3; nothing is added here, only connected. Since 2 October 2026: the close is designed as dated acts by seats in P22 §9 (CV6), on P13 §4's statement; see P13 §4's dated paragraph. The event's close (convention workflow, steps 8 and 9) reads the agreement row: gross under the convention class on the ledger; the Federation's share and the per-registrant fund amounts kept (D1-G); dues and fees collected by the host remitted and never counted as proceeds (5.2); the holdback held to the release period; the breach costs, if any, as a dated line with the Executive Committee's decision number; the balance released as the Convention settlement or Mid-Year settlement line on the host club's statement, with the agreement as its evidence link; the host's final accounting as a drive link on the agreement row with its filed date against 10.1.7's 120 days; the Liaison's report to the Board as a committee-workspace document (CW2). Deficit responsibility reads from the agreement and the screen says where it is silent (the register's board-confirmation item 2). The ad-book share, if the Board ever adopts one, is a term on the agreement and a line type the statement already has (Q-47).

6. The Mid-Year as a meeting

Because the Mid-Year's own by-law is about who attends and who votes, three things the record holds elsewhere connect here. The Mid-Year is the Board convened (10.2.1) and counts toward the Board's six meetings (10.3.1), the Past Presidents' two (10.4.1) and the Club Presidents' two (10.5.1): it is one Federation event with the by-law's attendance list as its invited roster (the Committee Workspace note §4 already reads it so) and attendance recorded per seat, which is what 6.3.1's "three consecutive Mid-Years" test for a Past President's active status needs — a count the platform can show and a status only the President's list sets. Only Board members vote at it (10.6.1); the minutes call the sessions a General Assembly and record motions and reports accepted — the platform labels any Mid-Year vote as a Board vote under 10.6.1 and raises the naming (Q-92, second part). Member-at-Large candidacies are filed "during the Mid-Year Meeting" (6.5.2): a dated candidacy row on the election the voting module runs, open through the Mid-Year's dates. A Leadership Ramallah summit beside the Mid-Year, RBPN's slot, the dabke troupe's travel (Q-47) and the welcome session for new members (P3, LT3) are sub-events on the same event row, each with its own scope and money.

7. Rules

  1. A Convention bid is in writing by the Mid-Year two years ahead and the location is chosen by the general membership at the Convention two years ahead; only Regular Members in good standing vote (10.1.1), by the delegate mechanism (9.1.3, 9.1.4) under the Annual Meeting's quorum (10.1.5).
  2. A bidding city must have at least fifty paid members for at least five consecutive years (10.1.1); the platform computes from collection dates where it can (D1-D) and shows attested years as attested; what "paid members of a city" counts is open (Q-90).
  3. The Convention rotates between the three regions of 10.1.1 and cannot return to a region within three years; a region that declines forfeits its turn (10.1.1). A city not listed in 10.1.1 is in no region under the text (Q-89).
  4. A club bids only if affiliated and in good standing (3.1, 3.2, 6.4.1, 10.1.6 — design choice, Q-91).
  5. The Mid-Year is held in the default city or elsewhere in the USA as approved by the Executive Committee (10.2.1); FED2's thirteen-month application and February date are parameters labelled FED2, not rules.
  6. Every Convention runs under a host agreement signed before it with the share, the holdback, the settlement date and the obligations (D59); the same shape serves the Mid-Year (D59); the Executive Committee including the Legal Advisor approves the final language (11.1.1); the agreement's terms are typed from the signed text and the settlement reads only them (design choice under D59).
  7. A change to the share goes from the Convention Innovation Committee to the Board, never the floor (D59); the three hosting models are agreement templates with blank amounts, not rules (design choice).
  8. The Liaison is a Past President appointed by the President; the final accounting reaches the office within 120 days (10.1.7).
  9. Host-collected dues and fees are remitted and never count as proceeds (5.2); Convention money is Federation money at the first dollar (D1-G); the settlement is a line on the host club's statement (D57; club plan §5.2).
  10. Only Board members vote at the Mid-Year (10.6.1); Member-at-Large names are filed during it (6.5.2); attendance at it counts for the Board, the Councils and 6.3.1.
  11. The signed agreement and the written bid live on the drive; the platform holds the rows and links (D66).
  12. Every refusal names its rule; where the record is silent the screen says so.
