1. What the record already holds

In the text. The by-laws constitute none of the four selection programmes and do not name the Arabic Program. They reach them in four places. 8.1.2–8.1.3: committee members are members in good standing of the Federation and their club and serve one year by the President's appointment, the chair appointed by the President "except as otherwise specified" — so every programme committee below is a body on P1's register with those seats unless its own text says otherwise. The paragraph under 8.10: the Young Leader Chairman and four members, appointed by the President with the Board's approval, "shall be alumnus of the A.F.R.P. Project Hope or Leadership Ramallah Programs" — the one place a by-law reads a programme's alumni record. 8.13.1 and 8.14.1: the Young Adult and Youth Program Committees run on rules the Board establishes. 10.2.1: the Mid-Year's attendance list, which does not name Leadership Ramallah. 4.3.1: good standing is the Federation's dues and the club's for the current year. 4.1.1 and 4.2.1: who may be a member — which matters because two of the four programmes admit people who are not.

Decided. D55: Project Hope, the Exchange Mission and the Arabic Program are ARFECF's (the Educational Fund); the Day of Action is AFRP's (the General Fund); Leadership Ramallah is not named and its entity is "for the Boards to confirm" (its register entry; the club plan §7.2). D57: a club's travel share is a line on the club's statement, each programme setting its own terms. D61: past participants of Project Hope, Leadership Ramallah and the Day of Action enter as alumni records (name, year, club); applications, essays, résumés, consents and passports never enter, past or future; future cycles apply in the platform; the Exchange Mission is not named. D65: the Day of Action is charged to AFRP only. D66: the drive is the archive. D67: counts that exist today are the first-year baseline (participants per programme per club, learners by track). D36: the family tree reaches people only for members, about their own branch.

Designed. Built. Historically (the intakes, role-level). The Exchange Mission has the one formal procedure (2020, approved by its chair and ratified by the Federation President; revised yearly by the coordinator): recruitment by every committee member; an online-presence review of candidates; an application of a form, a one-page essay and two recommendation letters; a four-member vetting subcommittee interviewing by video with the coordinator; the full committee voting, one vote each including the coordinator, ties to the coordinator and the chair; a signed acceptance or rejection letter from the coordinator; consent and release, passport and photo ID to the coordinator on acceptance; a booklet and two briefings before the trip; alumni follow-up; the number of delegations fixed at least six months ahead by the chair; clubs encouraged to host a yearly dinner with an alumnus speaker and not otherwise involved. No October delegation is evident since 2023; the committee has moved to a Palestine Fellowship (terms published yearly; open to members of the community aged 21 and over; a proposal, a budget, a narrative and a CV; three professional references for finalists; quarterly and final reports; the award at the committee's discretion), "reverse missions" and a symposium at the Convention. The Fellowship's selection panel, scoring and payment are not written. Project Hope's 2019 procedure is a draft partly pasted from the Exchange Mission's: ages 18 to 29, students or community-active, Arab and non-Arab, a passport valid six months beyond the trip, airfare paid by the delegate through Federation staff, a journal, advocacy afterwards; a selection subcommittee of at least two committee members, the coordinator and one alumnus, at least three present at every interview, a selection guide that is a stub; recruitment seven months ahead through clubs, alumni, RBPN and the magazine; a second form after selection; alumni "may attend only once and are discouraged" (2019 minutes); raising the minimum age to 19 was discussed in 2020 and not adopted. The programme has run no trip since 2023 and plans a revival from 2027 with an alumni call. Leadership Ramallah's records hold written cost rules (2026: the Federation pays the hotel, part of travel to a cap, a lunch, a reduced banquet and a prize budget; the club covers about half of travel and the Federation's travel share applies only where the club does not; participants pay registration, shirts and other meals; volunteers on like terms), acceptance and denial letter templates (2024: dues current by a stated date or the place is lost; receipts to the office; registration for the Mid-Year through the portal), itineraries, evaluation forms and applicant sheets with résumés; eligibility 21 and over "with occasional flexibility"; the co-chairs and the programme director review and score; "the selection protocol exists" and is not on file. The Day of Action's records hold a yearly calendar (applications from 1 September; review and a first training in October; a second in November; the event in mid-November), application questions (state, student, employed, interest, involvement, experience with elected officials, views on the issues, speakers wanted), participant and reimbursement sheets, a hotel room-block contract reviewed by counsel, and a CRM event form holding the answers as form responses on a contact; no eligibility, scoring or selector is written; afrp.org says first-time attendees aged 21 to 35 who are members have registration, hotel and part of travel covered. The Arabic Program runs three terms a year on two tracks (Traditional, six levels; Conversational, four), twelve weekly sessions on video with teachers from a partner college in Bethlehem that invoices per enrolled seat plus books; a 24-hour priority window for past and waitlisted learners before general registration; registration and payment through the Federation's current portal with a contact ID per learner; a disclaimer accepted by form each term; placement by self-assessment on request and an informal call, with an adaptive test wanted; rosters in spreadsheets; discount codes; recordings planned on the Federation's file storage; an anonymous end-of-term survey; certificates as files; volunteer conversation partners to be recruited through club presidents; a surplus whose location is an open action; the committee's August 2026 decisions (non-members at a higher rate; the adaptive test; the volunteers; a promotion line; 1099 contracts for US instructors; one shared drive). The landing page for the current term still says members only, which the committee's decision and D60 supersede. No written agreement with the teaching partner was found.

The shape of the problem. Four programmes choose people the same way and none of the four has a form, a roster or a decision anyone can find a year later; two admit people who are not members; one has written money rules with a club in them; one has no written rule at all; and the by-laws read one of their alumni lists for a committee seat. The Arabic Program is not a selection — anyone eligible who pays is in — but it is a cycle with a door, a placement, a partner and a close, and D60 has already opened its door to people the platform does not otherwise know. This note designs one engine for the four, and the term as its own cycle, so that every call, decision and share has a record, and leaves every rule the committees have not written where they left it.

2. The selection engine

2.1 The programme cycle, and the call

A cycle is one programme's one run: Project Hope's trip of a year, Leadership Ramallah's summit at a Mid-Year, an Exchange Mission delegation or a Fellowship year, the Day of Action of a November. It is opened by the programme's committee (a body on P1's register with its seats under 8.1.3, or as its own text seats it — the Exchange Mission's chair by the committee's choice ratified by the President, practice 2020) and carries:

  • the programme and the holding entity, read from the programme register (D55); a cycle whose entity is not stated (Leadership Ramallah today) opens, takes applications and decides, and refuses to raise any money line until a Board names the entity (the club plan §7.2's rule; P18-J07);
  • the deciding body and, where the programme has one, the vetting sub-body (the Exchange Mission's four-member subcommittee; Project Hope's selection subcommittee of at least two members, the coordinator and an alumnus) as bodies on CW1 whose seats the committee fills by a dated act, with "practice" as the appointing text;
  • the places (a number the committee sets, held on the cycle and never in this note), the dates (call opens, call closes, decision, acceptance deadline, standing deadline where the programme has one, the event's dates), and the event the cycle runs into (§2.7);
  • the eligibility rows that apply (§2.3), each with its source;
  • the money terms that apply (§2.6): the cost rule rows, the club share term, the holding entity's fund line;
  • the form version (§2.2), the retention parameter (§2.8), the letters that will go (§2.5), and the drive folder (D66);
  • its state: draft, open, closed to applications, deciding, decided, running, closed.

The call is the cycle published: places, eligibility, dates, the costs covered and any club share, through the communications desk as a programme-invitation-class message to the segment the committee names (the workflow's step 1; the consent filter of S1 and R27; the alumni segment of §2.9 where the committee invites alumni to recruit or to return). The recruitment channels the procedures name — the magazine, the clubs, RBPN, social media, alumni — are the message's audiences, and a club's outreach contact (the club plan §3.4; C4) sees the call and the count of applicants from their club, never an application. The Exchange Mission's rule that the number of delegations is fixed at least six months before the first trip is a check the cycle shows against its own dates (practice 2020), refusing nothing.

2.2 The application as a door

An application is one person's dated submission to one cycle. It follows P5 §3.3's shape and differs in one thing: the applicant need not be a member. It holds:

  • the applicant, as a member record where the programme's eligibility row requires membership (Leadership Ramallah; the Day of Action's covered first-timers) and otherwise as a member record or a contact record — name, verified channel, consent — on D60's precedent, as the Family Referral note (P4) already uses it. The Exchange Mission's delegates (journalists, educators, clergy, political leaders) are mostly not members; Project Hope's procedure welcomes "Arabs and non-Arabs" and is silent on membership (Q-116). A contact who applies is never a member and is never counted as one; if they later join, the join resolves the contact to the member through the identity service with the applicant's own confirmation, nothing automatic (slice 9's rule: nothing links itself). Since 2 October 2026: the contact record note holds such an applicant as an applicant role on one person record with dated roles (P21 §2, §7.2; CR5). That design is DRAFT under Q-70, so this paragraph stands until Q-70 is answered;
  • the structured fields the eligibility rows read: date of birth (held on the member record already; asked of a contact and held on the application only), the club (read from the member's scopes; asked of a contact), standing on the day (read, never typed), prior participation (read from the alumni records of §2.9; attested by the office for years the records do not hold), a passport-validity attestation where the programme asks one (a yes-and-expiry-month the applicant states; never the passport, D61), residence state where the programme asks it;
  • the programme's questions, as a versioned form the committee owns: the Day of Action's questions on interest, involvement and experience with elected officials; the Exchange Mission's form; Leadership Ramallah's application; Project Hope's. A form version is register data; a question's answer is the applicant's own text under the applicant's control, the only free text in the cycle, shown to the deciding body and to the applicant and to nobody else. The Day of Action's questions on the applicant's political views are answers the applicant chose to give on a form the committee wrote; the platform holds them on the application under the retention clock, shows them to the deciding body only, and never copies them to the member record, a segment, a count or the directory (design choice; the S7 and R27 principle). A committee that wants to drop a question drops it from the next version;
  • the applicant's statement (the essay; the Fellowship's narrative) as a field, and documents (a résumé; recommendation letters; the Fellowship's work plan, budget and partner letters) uploaded to the application and held under the retention clock — not copied by the platform to the drive — a reading of D61: its "never enter" is read as governing past cycles' documents and its "future cycles apply in the platform" as admitting what the applicant types or uploads at the door under the retention clock; if David reads "never enter" as reaching future cycles, the statement stays a field and the uploads become an on-file flag like the passport's. Raised with Q-118. Since 4 Oct 2026: D94 adopts this reading for now, pending the ARFECF Board. Design choice, labelled: P5 keeps the Selection Committee's profile documents on the drive because the committee collects them; here the applicant supplies them at a door, and a door that sends a stranger's documents into the Federation's archive would make the archive what D66 says it is not. The committee's own copy on the drive, if it keeps one, is under the committee's own deletion rule (D61's second paragraph);
  • what never enters: passports, photo ID, consent and release forms, medical forms, travel forms (D61 for Project Hope, Leadership Ramallah and the Day of Action; extended to the Exchange Mission by this note as a design choice until D61 names it, Q-118; since 4 Oct 2026 extended by D94, for now). The coordinator collects them outside and the platform holds an on-file flag per document kind with the date and the recorder (the camp's pattern under D61); the flag is a condition of travel (§2.7), and a flag with no date refuses the roster;
  • how it arrived: self; a club's outreach; an alumnus's referral (the applicant names it; nothing is inferred);
  • the applicant's consent to the deciding body reading their record for the eligibility check, as a dated consent row (R27);
  • states: received, late (shown to the committee with the date; refused only where the committee's rule says refused — the Exchange Mission's "rolling basis until deadline" and the Day of Action's calendar say nothing about late ones, so nothing is refused: Q-117), withdrawn, checked, interviewed, decided (accepted, waitlisted with rank, declined), offered, accepted by the applicant, declined by the applicant, lapsed (the acceptance deadline passed), travelled or attended, closed.

Who sees it. The applicant: their own application, its state and the letters sent to them, never a ranking or anyone else's. The deciding body's seats and the vetting sub-body's: the applications to their cycle. The programme coordinator or director (a staff seat on the body): the same, and the on-file flags. The club's outreach contact or travel coordinator: a count from their club, and, once accepted, the accepted participants from their club with the share due (the club plan §3.4), never an application. The Federation lens: counts per cycle under the floor (S7). The Executive Director's 7.9.2 reach to appointed committees' operations: clocks and counts, not applications (P5 §3.3's reading). Nobody else.

Past cycles. No application from before the platform is imported (D61). A past participant is an alumni record (§2.9) and nothing more.

2.4 The committee's review

The vetting. Where the programme has a vetting sub-body (the Exchange Mission; Project Hope), the sub-body's recommendation per application is a decision item on its queue (CW2 §3.4) with the outcome recommend, do not recommend or no recommendation, dated, with the seats present. Where the programme has none (Leadership Ramallah's co-chairs and director; the Day of Action's director and committee), the deciding body reviews directly.

The interview. A meeting of the sub-body or body (CW2) scoped to the cycle, with the applicant as an invitee and attendance taken — which is how the Exchange Mission's "at least two subcommittee members plus the coordinator" and Project Hope's "at least three present" are checked: the platform shows the count present against the rule (practice) and refuses nothing. The platform holds no notes, no scores and no opinion from the interview (P1 §3.3; P5 §3.5); those stay in the committee's drive folder under its own confidentiality (D66).

Scores. Leadership Ramallah's records say the co-chairs and the director "review and score"; the protocol is not on file. The scholarship engine (slice 4) holds scores because its specification defines the rubric and the median rule. Design choice, labelled: the platform holds scores for a programme only where the committee has adopted a written rubric as a register row (the criteria, the scale, the aggregation), dated; until then the ranking is the committee's dated outcome ("ranked by the committee on ") and no figure is held (Q-117). A rubric the committee adopts later applies to the next cycle, not the one in progress. In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026): the Fellowship's selection is a committee discussion over a one-paragraph summary of each applicant, with no scoring. What the committee keeps from it is part of Q-118's wording; see also Q-119.

The vote and the tie-break. The decision is a decision item on the deciding body's queue: per application, the outcome and the tally where a vote was taken (the Exchange Mission's one vote each, the coordinator included — practice 2020), the seats present, the date, the minute link. A tie is recorded as broken by the seats the committee's rule names (the Exchange Mission: the coordinator and the chair — practice 2020; the others: not written, so the body records who broke it and the platform names no one). Individual votes are not held per member (design choice, P1 §3.3's reading: the platform records the decision, not the deliberation); a committee that wants a recorded roll-call vote takes it on the voting module's committee polls (CW4's pattern) and the platform links the result.

Conflicts. The scholarship engine detects conflicts by degree over the family tree (slice 4). Nothing in the four procedures states a conflict rule; the platform shows a deciding seat's household relation to an applicant where the tree holds one, as information to the chair, and refuses nothing (design choice; Q-117 asks whether a rule is wanted).

2.5 The decision, the letters and the applicant's acceptance

The decision as a dated act. When the body's decision item closes, each application moves to accepted, waitlisted with a rank, or declined, dated, with the body named. The decision is frozen (R43's pattern); a later change is a new act naming the first. The acceptance letter and the denial letter go out on the act, as rows on P3's template register (LT-P), each with: the class programme invitation for the acceptance and operational for the denial and the waitlist; the signatory seat the programme's practice names (the Exchange Mission: the programme coordinator, 2020; Leadership Ramallah: the co-chairs and the programme director, 2024; Project Hope and the Day of Action: not written — the chair and the director by analogy, wording open); the fields merged (the cycle's dates, the costs covered as the cost rule rows state them, the conditions below, the acceptance deadline, how to accept); the consent honoured (a member must receive an operational letter under the club plan §9.2's reading; a contact receives on the consent they gave at the door); the entity it goes out under (D55). The wording of every letter is open until the committee adopts a text (P3's rule).

The applicant's acceptance. An offer is accepted or declined by the applicant by the acceptance deadline, as a dated act on their own application. Accepting records the applicant's acknowledgement of the conditions the cycle states: the costs they bear as the cost rule rows state them (airfare for Project Hope; registration, shirts and meals for Leadership Ramallah; travel pending reimbursement for the Day of Action); the on-file documents they will supply to the coordinator outside the platform; the programme's expectations where written (Project Hope's journal and advocacy; the Fellowship's reports). A declined or lapsed place moves to the next ranked waitlisted applicant by the coordinator's dated act, not automatically (design choice; the workflow's step 5 "goes to the next ranked applicant" is honoured as a prompt to the coordinator, so that a committee may re-decide rather than be bound by a rank it set weeks earlier).

The standing deadline (Leadership Ramallah). The acceptance letters say an accepted participant must be current by a stated date or lose the place (practice 2024). The platform shows the participant's two standings (4.3.1) beside the place from acceptance to the deadline, sends a reminder (an operational letter, LT-P) before it, and on the deadline opens a follow-up to the coordinator naming the rule; the loss of the place is the coordinator's dated act (design choice: the platform ends nothing on its own, as P1 §3.5 and the club plan hold). A participant whose dues are collected by their club has standing from the collection date (D1 consequence D).

2.6 Money: the club's share, the Federation's share and reimbursement

Every amount in this section is a register row the programme committee or its entity's Board fills with a date and an authority; this note holds none.

The cost rule rows. Each cycle carries the written cost rule as rows: what the Federation pays (Leadership Ramallah: the hotel, part of travel to a cap, a lunch, a reduced banquet, a prize budget — the 2026 cost rules; the Day of Action: the hotel block and part of first-timers' travel — afrp.org and the reimbursement files; Project Hope: in-country costs — the 2019 procedure; the Exchange Mission: in-country costs, the trip leader's own flight — 2020), what the participant pays, what the club is expected to cover. A programme whose rule is not written (the Day of Action's amounts; Project Hope's share) has empty rows, and an empty row posts nothing and says so.

The club share line (D57). On the applicant's acceptance the platform raises the programme's line on the participant's club's statement (the club plan §5.2): Leadership Ramallah travel share (club → participant, recorded against the club, entity ARFECF once confirmed); Project Hope and exchange-mission travel share where the programme writes one; Day of Action travel (AFRP → first-time attendee, recorded against the club for the count, no club money). The line carries the term row, the participant (held, shown to the club's travel coordinator by name because the participant is an adult who accepted a place whose terms name the club — design choice; shown to nobody else on the statement), and the evidence link. Leadership Ramallah's rule that the Federation's travel share applies only where the club does not cover the remainder, and that a club that cannot "contacts the Federation" (2026), is modelled as: the club's treasurer confirms the line or marks it not covered with a reason by a date the cycle sets; a line marked not covered raises the Federation's share row for that participant; a line unconfirmed on the date opens a follow-up to the programme coordinator and the club treasurer, and the platform computes nothing until one of them acts. The 2026 minutes say the club co-funding model may be reconsidered; until a committee changes the rule the rows stand as written.

Reimbursement. A participant's receipts become a reimbursement row against the cycle: the claimed amount, the receipt as an attachment under the retention clock, the mailing address held on the payment and never on the directory (R27), the rule row it falls under, the approver (the finance administrator's seat in practice; the entity's treasurer by D55), the payment with the funds ledger's fee rule (R20) from the holding entity's programme line (R38): ARFECF's Educational Fund for Project Hope and the Exchange Mission (D55), AFRP's General Fund for the Day of Action (D65; the platform refuses any Day of Action charge to ARFECF or ARFHSN by name), and nothing for Leadership Ramallah until a Board names its entity — the row is held, shown as "entity not stated (D55)", and the participant and the coordinator both see why. The Leadership Ramallah banquet, paid or reimbursed at a reduced rate the Mid-Year host sets (2026), is a term of the Mid-Year's host agreement (P13 §2; Q-92) and reads from it.

The Fellowship's award. The terms set a grant to a fellow or team with reports due; the decision mechanism and the payment (tranches or one sum) are not written. The award is a grant on P11's grants register in shape (an approval act with a ceiling, payments, reports as delivery), paid from ARFECF (D55), with the private fund's grant received on P11's agreements register as grant received (CG2, kind proposed in P11 §3.3); nothing of it posts until the committee writes the decision and payment rule (Q-119).

2.7 The event the cycle runs into

The trip, the summit, the delegation or the Day of Action is an event on the events module (built; B2 for the door and attendance), scoped to the programme, with the accepted participants as its roster: Project Hope's trip; the Exchange Mission's delegation and its two briefings as sub-events; the Day of Action's two trainings and the three days in Washington as sub-events, with the hotel room-block contract on P11's agreements register (kind supplier, signed for AFRP under 11.1.1's approval, as the Day of Action's register entry already lists it); Leadership Ramallah's summit as a sub-event of the Mid-Year (P8 §6 already places it there) so that the participant's Mid-Year registration (the acceptance letters' condition, 2024) is the Mid-Year event's own registration and the summit's roster is read from the cycle. The roster is refused to a participant whose on-file flags are incomplete (§2.2) or, for Leadership Ramallah, whose standing lapsed past the deadline without the coordinator's act — the refusal names the flag or the rule. Attendance is taken (B2) and feeds the alumni record (§2.9) and the counts (D67). The trip leader (Project Hope: a committee-appointed three-year role, 2019) and the Exchange Mission's coordinator are seats on the body (CW1) with the event's organiser role; the post-trip report is a drive document linked from the event (D66), with the structured facts the committee's quarterly report needs (CW3).

The Mid-Year pairing (F4). Leadership Ramallah "held with the Mid-Year" is the 2019 plan's wish and the committee's practice since; 10.2.1's attendance list does not name it, FED2's Mid-Year guidelines ask the host for a room for it (P8 §1), and the cost rules name the host's reduced banquet price. The pairing is therefore three things on the platform: the sub-event (above); two standing obligation rows on the Mid-Year host agreement (a room for the summit; the reduced banquet) that the agreement may carry (P13 §2.3, HA1); and a question — whether the summit is always at the Mid-Year, and whether those two obligations are standing terms of every Mid-Year agreement or negotiated each year (Q-92). Nothing else of the pairing is modelled, and the note says so.

2.8 The close, and the retention clock

When the event closes, the cycle closes: each participant who attended becomes an alumni record (§2.9); each application's structured rows (dates, state, outcome, the eligibility check's result, the letters sent) are kept as the record of the committee's decision; the applicant's statement, the form answers, the uploaded documents and the receipt attachments are deleted on the retention clock — a parameter per programme with no value until the committee and its entity's Board set one (Q-76; P5 §3.3's Q-76 is the same question for the Selection Committee) — and until the parameter is set nothing is deleted and the cycle's page says "retention not set: nothing deleted". The on-file flags are cleared at the close because the documents they attest were never in the platform; the committee's deletion of them from the drive is the committee's rule, outside the platform (D61). A declined applicant's rows are kept under the same clock as an accepted one's (design choice: the record of a decision includes whom it was not made for; the committee may set a shorter clock for declined applications as a second parameter).

2.9 Alumni, after the cycle

The alumni record is D61's object: name (the person, as a member record or a contact record), programme, year, club at the time, and — added by this note for the role the by-laws give it — the cycle it came from where the platform ran it, and attended as the fact (D61's camper record carries attended; a selected participant who did not travel is not an alumnus). Past participants enter through the alumni workflow (import a list; resolve through the match queue, slice 9; review by hand; nothing links itself), extending the queue from scholarship recipients to the four programmes (the workflow's could_share; D61). Who reviews the queue is the workflow's own open item (Alumni use cases §6 q4), carried.

What reads it. The eligibility rows (Project Hope's one-trip fact; the Day of Action's first-timer fact; §2.3). The Young Leader Committee's seats: P1's table reads the paragraph under 8.10 ("alumnus of the A.F.R.P. Project Hope or Leadership Ramallah Programs") and CW1's seat check for that body reads the alumni record as its evidence — met where a record exists, undetermined where none does, with the office's attestation as the human finding (never a refusal from an absent record, since the records are incomplete until loaded). The Day of Action's and Leadership Ramallah's hand-offs (alumni as facilitators, as Congressional Outreach recruits, as RBPN's path): segments. The counts (D67): participants per programme per club per year. Since 2 October 2026: P25 §4 seats the Young Leader Committee as the text reads: five seats, each with the President's appointment and the Board's approval, and this alumni check on each. In practice the committee has more members, listed by club city, and two co-chairs who are also Leadership Ramallah's. Whether the two committees are one body or two is Q-175, and the composition is Q-179 (YL2).

Contact. An alumnus who is a member is reached under their own consents (R27, S1). An alumnus who is a contact record from a cycle the platform ran is reached on the consent given at the door, for that programme. An alumnus who is a resolved historical record and not a member has no consent row until they claim the record (the alumni workflow's step 5) or give one; the platform sends nothing to them, and the "reconnection campaign" of the workflow reaches only those with a row (S1; R27; slice 9's do-not-contact closes every path). The Exchange Mission's wish for an alumni newsletter (F5) is a segment of its alumni with a consent row, sent through the communications desk as a programme-invitation-class message by the committee's chair or coordinator; the record is silent on whether alumni who are not members and have not claimed a record may be written to, and on whether a newsletter is marketing or programme invitation — the platform treats it as programme invitation and sends to consent rows only (design choice, Q-120). The committee's "alumni database" (F5) is this record and nothing more: no notes, no history beyond programme-year-club-attended, no profession unless the alumnus lists it themselves on their member record (P19 §3).

The committee's reunion call (Project Hope, December 2026). A segment of Project Hope alumni with consent, invited to an event. The alumni list the committee holds today is the first import; the match queue resolves it; nobody is written to before resolution and consent.

3. The Arabic term

The Arabic Program is a cycle with a door but not a selection: whoever is eligible and pays has a place until the class is full. It is designed as its own object because its door admits non-members (D60), its partner is paid per seat, and its placement is a form that is not an application.

3.1 The term

A term is one of the three a year (a fall term from September; a spring term from January; a short summer term), opened by the Arabic committee (a body on CW1; a standing committee joined through the committee interest form, P7 §6) with: the tracks and levels as register data the committee maintains (Traditional, read-write-speak, with its levels; Conversational, dialect without the alphabet, with its levels; each level's entry criteria as the class descriptions state them — practice); the classes, each with a track, a level, a weekly slot, the number of sessions and the weeks skipped, a teacher as a role filled by the partner (the partner's teachers are not members and are not named on the platform beyond the partner's own roster, design choice under D50's roles-only rule for the public site and R27 for the platform), a cap, the partner's minimum below which the class is cancelled (practice: the committee's cap on classes is "capped by the teaching partner's minimums", August 2026), and a waitlist; the rates — the member rate and the public rate (D60) — as register rows with the committee's dated act, figures never in this note; the priority window for past learners and waitlisted prospects before general registration (practice: 24 hours in 2026), as two dates on the term; the disclaimer version the term uses; the partner agreement the term runs under (§3.5); the drive folder for the term's catalogue and certificates (D66).

3.2 Registration and payment: the member rate, the public rate and the contact

The door. A learner registers for one class of one term. A member (national standing current — design choice: the member rate tests the Federation's standing alone, not 4.3.1's conjunctive test, because the programme is the Federation's and not a club's and the committee's decision speaks of "AFRP members"; Q-123 asks the committee to confirm) pays the member rate. A non-member pays the public rate and the platform creates or reuses a contact record — name, verified channel (an email or mobile verified by code, as the join wizard does), consent per channel for the programme's messages — "and not a membership" (D60). The contact record carries the learner's track and level history so that the priority window and a prerequisite can read them; it carries no standing, no directory row, no vote, no club. A contact who later joins is resolved to the member on the join with the person's own confirmation (slice 9's rule), and their class history moves with them.

The payment. D60 names this a new caller of the one payment door: the payments module takes a purchase from a person who is not a member, receipted by the holding entity (ARFECF, D55; the receipt as LT-R7 in the entity's name, with no deductibility language because a class fee is a purchase, D9's shape), posted to the programme's class under the Educational Fund's income line (R38), gross with the fee as an expense (R20). What the record does not say — whether AFRP collects and forwards, as the 2024 invoice addressed to the Federation suggests, or ARFECF collects as the budget says, and where last year's surplus sits — is the arabic-term workflow's open item and is Q-121; until a Board answers, the receipt names ARFECF and the posting shows "collection path to confirm". Live money waits on slice 5 as every purchase does.

Discount codes, cancellations, refunds. The rosters show a discount-code mechanism. On the platform a discount is a rate row (who may use it, how much, which term, who authorised it, dated) and never a free entry at checkout; who may create one is not written (Q-123). A learner's cancellation, a class cancelled under the partner's minimum, and the refund that follows are not written anywhere; the platform refunds through the payments module's reversal (R24, S3) only on a rule row the committee sets, and until then a cancellation opens a follow-up to the coordinator and refunds nothing automatically (Q-123).

The disclaimer. The programme's disclaimer (an educational programme; discussion reflects participants, not the Federation; no guarantee of fluency — practice) is accepted before the first session as a dated acknowledgement row on the registration, versioned with the text, in place of the yearly form; a registration without it is shown to the coordinator as incomplete and the learner is reminded (an operational message, LT-P); the platform refuses nothing on it because the committee's practice refuses nothing (design choice).

The welcome. The class welcome (dates, the video link, the book, the placement step) is an LT-P row of class operational, signed by the committee chair, wording open, sent on the registration's completion under the member's consents or the contact's. Since 2 October 2026: the non-member learner is the learner role of the contact record note (P21 §2, §3; D60), the one role CR1 switches on first. The contact's consent is read by that role only (P21 §8).

3.3 Placement: a form that is not an application

A learner is placed by level. Today: self-assessment on request against a level's entry criteria, then an informal call; the committee wants an adaptive test (August 2026). On the platform a placement is a form the learner completes for a track — the self-assessment's items as a versioned form the committee owns, with the entry criteria per level shown — and a placement act by the teacher or the coordinator (a dated row: the level placed, the recorder), the learner seeing both. It is not an application: it has no decision body, no ranking, no acceptance, and no competition for a place. A learner who completed a prior level with the programme (a class history row with completed) meets the next level's prerequisite without a test (practice: "prior AFRP level or equivalent"); "equivalent" is the placement act. The adaptive test, when the committee has one, is the same form with branching, scored by a rubric the committee adopts as register data; the platform holds the form's result as the recommendation and the placement act as the decision, so a teacher may still overrule (design choice). The form's answers are deleted on the term's retention clock (§3.6); the placement act is kept as class history.

3.4 The volunteers: conversation partners (P7)

The committee's volunteer conversation partners are a roster under P7 §3: the term conversation partner, scope the Arabic Program, opted into by members on their own record (VI1), with availability as yes or no for a slot; the committee's chair or coordinator owns the roster; each club's volunteers' lead (the club plan §3.4; C4) reads it filtered to their own club, which is how "club presidents are asked for volunteers" lands without a club seeing another club's people. The ask goes through the communications desk as a programme-invitation-class message (VI2). The pairing of a volunteer with a class slot is an assignment (volunteer, class, slot, weeks) the coordinator records; the learner sees their partner's name and the slot and reaches them through the broker under the volunteer's contact consent (R27), never a personal number. Learners are adults, so no screening row of P7 §5 applies; a term the committee ever opens to minors would change that and is not designed here. Volunteers and learners per club are the club's count (the club plan §7.1; D67); no money flows through clubs for this programme (the committee's minutes, August 2026).

3.5 The teaching partner, the invoice and the close

The partner agreement. The partner college is an agreement on P11's register (CG2, kind teaching partner): parties (the college; ARFECF as the entity that carries the obligation, D55), term, notice, obligations as the practice shows them (bilingual teachers of the Palestinian dialect on video; the textbook; an invoice per enrolled seat plus books after the term starts; the Federation's side: rosters before the term, fees collected, the invoice paid), the signatory row per entity (empty until Q-107 is answered) and the drive link. No written agreement was found: the row is created in state draft with "no signed text on file" and the committee is asked. The 1099 contracts the committee approved for US-based instructors are agreements of kind supplier on the same register, signed for the entity that pays (not stated; Q-121).

The per-seat payable. When the partner invoices, the coordinator records the invoice against the term as a payable: the seats (which the platform counts from the rosters on the invoice date and shows beside the partner's figure — a difference is a query, not an edit), books and shipping as lines, paid from the holding entity's programme expense line (D55, R38) by the entity's signatories. The term's page shows income against the payable, so the surplus or shortfall the committee asks about is a figure on the term and not a question for the finance administrator.

Certificates and the survey. A certificate per learner per completed class is a document the platform produces (an LT-P row of class operational, signed by the committee chair, wording open) and a completed row on the learner's class history; the file is kept on the drive (D66). The end-of-term survey is anonymous on an outside form today; it stays outside until the platform offers an anonymous survey, and the term records the link and the response count. Enrolment by track and level per term, and learners and volunteers per club, are the committee's D67 counts and the report to the Board (CW3).

3.6 Recordings, the contact's retention and the term's clock

Recordings. The committee plans to store class recordings on the Federation's file storage. The record holds no rule for them — not in D61, not in the disclaimer, not in any committee minute — and a recording is personal data of every learner whose voice or face is in it and of the teacher. The platform does not store recordings (they are outside, D66's pattern) and holds, per class, whether sessions are recorded and the link; what it must hold before a class is marked recorded is a per-learner, per-term consent row to be recorded and to the recording's audience (the class only; later learners of the same level; the public), withdrawable, with a learner who declines shown to the coordinator so the teacher can act (design choice, from D22's per-item consent and R27); the teacher's and the partner's rights are the agreement's (§3.5). Who may view recordings afterwards and for how long they are kept is Q-122, and the platform marks no class recorded until it is answered.

The non-member contact's retention. How long a non-member's contact record is kept after a term is the workflow's open item. Design choice until decided: the contact is kept while the person has a class history the priority window reads, and is deleted on the same clock as the alumni-contact question (Q-70), which also asks whether a non-member learner may be told about the next term — the consent they gave at the door answers it for the programme's own messages and for nothing else (D60: consent is part of the record). Since 2 October 2026: that sentence is a design choice, and Q-70 is its gate, not its answer. Q-70 still asks whether a non-member learner may be told about the next term. The contact record note builds it that way: the door asks for no programme-invitation consent, and news of the next term is refused until Q-70 is answered (P21 §8). The learner role's retention row is P21 §9's, with no value.

The term's clock. The placement form's answers and the disclaimer's text version are kept for the term and deleted on a retention parameter with no value (the same shape as §2.8); the class history (track, level, completed, year) is kept as the learner's own record, which they see on their member or contact record.

4. Rules

Every rule below cites a by-law, a decision, a register row or is labelled practice (with its committee and year) or design choice.

  1. A programme's holding entity is the fund that budgets it (D55); a cycle whose entity is not stated opens and decides but raises no money line and reimburses nothing (the club plan §7.2; D55).
  2. The Day of Action is AFRP General Fund activity and no cost of it is charged to ARFECF or ARFHSN (D65).
  3. Future cycles apply in the platform; no application, essay, résumé, consent or passport from a past cycle enters; past participants enter as alumni records of name, year and club (D61). Passports, photo ID, consent and release, medical and travel forms never enter for any cycle: D61 for Project Hope, Leadership Ramallah and the Day of Action; design choice for the Exchange Mission until D61 names it (Q-118).
  4. A club's travel share is a line on the club's statement under the programme's own term (D57; the club plan §5.2); the Day of Action's travel is AFRP's and counts against the club only (D57; D65).
  5. A member is in good standing when the Federation's dues and the club's are paid for the year (4.3.1); Leadership Ramallah's standing test is that clause; its deadline and the loss of the place are practice (2024), and the loss is a human act.
  6. Committee members are members in good standing of the Federation and their club (8.1.2), appointed for a year by the President unless a text says otherwise (8.1.3); a programme committee, its vetting sub-body and its coordinator are seats on P1's register.
  7. The Young Leader Committee's chair and members are alumni of Project Hope or Leadership Ramallah (the paragraph under 8.10); the alumni record is the evidence CW1's seat check reads, met, undetermined or attested, never refusing on an absent record.
  8. The selection procedures are the committees' practice: the Exchange Mission's vetting subcommittee, interview, one-vote-each decision and tie-break by the coordinator and chair (2020); Project Hope's subcommittee and three-present rule (2019, draft); Leadership Ramallah's review by co-chairs and director (2026; protocol not on file). The platform shows each against its rule and refuses nothing by them.
  9. The platform holds no interview notes, scores or opinions about an applicant (P1 §3.3; P5 §3.5); scores only under a committee's adopted rubric as a register row (design choice); the decision is a dated act of the body (CW2 §3.4), frozen (R43's pattern).
  10. A non-member may apply where the programme's eligibility row does not require membership, as a contact record on D60's precedent (design choice; D60 is written for the Arabic term and its use for an applicant is a reading, raised as one question for governance with P4's Q-70); a non-member buys an Arabic class at the public rate as a contact record and not a membership; members pay the member rate (D60).
  11. Eligibility rows carry their source and grade; a silent row is undetermined and the committee decides; a minor is refused by every programme's own text (the age rows; R7's shape for a missing birth date).
  12. The one-trip rule (F3) is not in the rules register: shown as a fact, never enforced (crosswalk F3; Q-116).
  13. Reimbursements and the partner's invoice post gross with the fee as an expense (R20) to the holding entity's programme line (R38); the mailing address on a payment is never a directory field (R27).
  14. Every letter is a row on the template register with its class, trigger, signatory seat and consent; its wording is open until adopted (P3 §2).
  15. Nothing is sent to anyone without a consent row (S1; R27); a resolved historical alumnus who is not a member has none until they claim the record or give one (the alumni workflow; slice 9).
  16. The applicant's statement, form answers and documents are deleted on a retention parameter with no value until set; nothing is deleted before (design choice under D61's principle; Q-76).
  17. A roster is computed from interests and owned by the seat that owns the scope (P7 §3); a club's lead reads only their club's volunteers (Four-Lens §6; C4).
  18. A recording is marked only with per-learner consent to be recorded and to its audience (design choice, D22's pattern) and only once the data rule is set (Q-122); recordings themselves stay outside the platform (D66's pattern).
  19. Documents live on the drive; the platform holds the rows and the links (D66). Every refusal names its rule; where the record is silent the screen says so (R40).
