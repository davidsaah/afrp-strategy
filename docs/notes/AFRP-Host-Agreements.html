The shape of the problem. D59 made the signed agreement the rule and P8 made the row that holds it. What is missing is what the row is: which terms it must carry, in what form, with what the platform may compute from them and what it may not; what the host's obligations look like as rows a treasurer can settle against; where the banquet's rules and the speakers' agreements sit; what the settlement statement prints; and what happens when the two sides disagree. Every one of those exists today only inside a template nobody has signed and a set of letters and minutes that disagree with it. This note turns the template into a schedule the agreement may take, without adopting a single term of it.

2. The agreement as an object

The agreement is P8 §4's row on the agreements register (CG2), class CONV68, generated when a bid is accepted (HB2) and otherwise empty. This section defines what it holds. Every money term is typed from the signed text by the office, with the drive link to the page it comes from; a term the signed text does not state is not stated and the platform computes nothing from it. The three hosting models (§7) pre-fill the kinds of terms and never their amounts.

2.1 Parties and seats

  • The Federation, represented by the President or the Convention Liaison, with the Deputy President where the signed text has them (template); the signatory row for AFRP on the register (P11 §3.1; Q-107, carried) names the role and the authority under 11.1.1.
  • The host club, represented by its president, its convention or Mid-Year chair and co-chair (template): three event roles on the Federation's event (the club plan §7.1), filled from the bid's named seats (P8 §2) and changeable by the club's dated act; the club's standing pill (C1) shown beside them for the whole term.
  • The Convention Liaison: a seat on CW1 filled by the President's dated act from the Council of Past Presidents (10.1.7), created on the bid's acceptance (P8 §3), with the duties the text gives it (oversee and report on planning and accounting) as the follow-ups it owns here (§4).
  • The Federation Convention Committee — the Liaison and the Executive Committee (template) — as a body on CW1 of the kind "created, no appointing text", whose acts (the hotel approval, the worksheet approval) are decision items on its queue (CW2 §3.4). Whether it is a body the Board recognises is Q-130.
  • The Digital Infrastructure Committee, which builds the registration page from the worksheet in practice, as a body on CW1 if it exists on P1's register; otherwise the office.
  • The Legal Advisor: the seat whose review of the hotel contract and approval of the agreement's final language (11.1.1) are dated acts on the row.

2.3 The schedule of obligations

Each side's obligations are rows on the agreement: a named item, the side that bears it, a ceiling where the text sets one, the rule above the ceiling (the keynote: the Board's prior approval, then a Federation cost — template), whether evidence is required on file (an insurance certificate; a security contract; a hotel contract copy) with the drive link and the date filed (D66), and whether the item produces a settlement line (a reimbursable cost one side pays for the other). The template's lists seed the rows of the host-city model (§7) with the side the template gives; the ambiguous rows of the template's summary table (Federation-hosted events' costs; audio-visual for them) seed with side: not stated in the template and refuse to settle until the signed text says (Q-124). The schedule is the agreement's, so a host and the Federation may strike or add rows; what no one may do is settle an item that has no row. Two rows the Mid-Year's guideline and Leadership Ramallah's cost rules ask of a Mid-Year host — a room for the summit; a reduced banquet price for its participants and volunteers — are offered as rows of the Mid-Year template and are not standing terms until a body says so (P12 Q-92). The Preservation Project's exhibit room (the club plan §7.1) is a row of the same kind.

2.4 The registration terms

  • Only through the Federation's portal; anything collected outside it is a breach (template): on the platform this is the events module's single registration path (built), with the host's registrations posted to the convention class under the Federation's host field (R23; D1 G). A breach is a fact the Executive Committee records (§5), not something the platform detects.
  • The registration data is the Federation's (template; the crosswalk G3; D1 G) and the member's record is the member's (R27): the host's event-coordinator role is a scoped grant on the event (Four-Lens §6) that reads event transactions, guest counts, sub-event rosters and seating for the event and reads no member record — not standing, not consents, not addresses beyond what the registrant gave for the event. Whether the host's coordinator sees seating, which the 2025 host was refused, is a term the agreement states (Q-127); the template says all records the host creates are the Federation's and are handed over on request, and the platform makes that automatic by holding them nowhere else. Since 2 October 2026: this grant stands (D41). In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026), the registration system's coordinator role gives the host more than this grant does: every guest's contact details with an export, full seating control, the waitlist, and a report by payer with breakdowns by gender, age and local club. See Q-127, which asks whether the Board adopts the narrower grant. P22 §8 builds this grant. It reads "event transactions" as lines by product, with no payer contact field and no age or gender breakdown, and it lists what the host gets instead: badges printed by the platform, rosters for each sub-event, and counts under the floor. If this note meant the payer to be visible, P22 §8 is stricter than it; the two readings are recorded, not resolved (Q-127).
  • The worksheet (the Digital Infrastructure Committee's practice) is the event builder's form for the host: name, dates, cap, base products, close date, hotel link, questions, and sub-events with date, cap, price, seating chart, reserved tables, form, waitlist and the inclusion rule (mandatory, optional, mandatory until full). The committee's "hold registration until all is supplied" is a gate on publication, alongside D7's gate (surplus and shortfall rules on the ticket page for any money-taking event).
  • Who may register: members, membership mandatory (the register's standing rule); the Federation may restrict to the community as the Constitution defines it and may grant special permission (template): a credential class per registrant — member, household member, guest with the fee or donation of 5.2, exhibitor and press as non-attendee credentials (the register's board-confirmation item 1), special permission by the President's dated act.
  • Deadlines and refunds: early-bird to a date, cancellation penalty, no refund within a stated period, a hard refund deadline the host sets, no exceptions after the seating deadline including Board members (template; the CIC's 2026 rule): products' date rules on the event, applied by the payments module's reversal rules (R24; S3) and refused by name after the deadline.
  • The thirty-day list (5.2): the General Treasurer's list of members whose dues are paid, produced for the host thirty days before as an office document of names and standing only — the one list the host receives, because the by-law gives it, and it is not a directory (P19 §4's pattern for the office's roster).

2.5 The banquet programme and the head table

The template fixes the banquet programme — the listed speakers in order with their minutes, speeches to the office a week ahead, a normal and a never-exceed length, installation by the outgoing President, no awards without the President's approval — and lists the head table and reserved tables by seat. On the platform this is a schedule the agreement carries (a template of rows: speaker seat, minutes, order; the lengths as two parameters; the approval acts), with the reserved tables as seats on the banquet sub-event named by seat (past Presidents, the Foundation's President, club presidents, the Board at large, the sitting President's family tables, the incoming President's) and filled by the office from CW1 and the officers form (C1), so that "Board members must register to hold a reserved table" (the CIC's rule) is a check the platform can show: a reserved seat with no registration against it by the seating deadline is listed to the office and to the Liaison, and released only by the office's dated act. The 2025 host's requests — names for the Federation's front seating by a date, fewer seats held, tables kept empty for late donors — are parameters of the schedule the agreement may set, not rules. The banquet is one sub-event with several floor plans (the CIC's 2026 decision; the events module's seating chart); a banquet seat is inventory (R26). Awards announced at the banquet are the programmes' (the OHSS award is retired on the register; any other needs the President's approval as a dated act — template).

2.6 Speaker agreements as sub-agreements

A keynote or a symposium speaker is a sub-agreement on CG2 (kind speaker), linked to the host agreement: the speaker by role (never named on the public site, D50), the engagement (the address's length, the session), the payer (the Federation, the host, or a programme committee's funds — the Exchange Mission decided in 2026 to pay a symposium slot's honoraria and travel from its funds), the terms (a stipend, travel to a cap, rooms, forfeiture for non-adherence — the 2026 keynote agreement, figures on the register only), the obligations schedule's row it falls under and the ceiling there, and the signatory row for the paying entity. The platform shows a variance where the sub-agreement's payer or amount departs from the host agreement's row (the 2026 case: the Federation paying where the template has the host paying to a ceiling) and corrects nothing — the variance is a fact for the settlement and for the Executive Committee. Who invites the keynote (the President with the Board's approval, from the host's three proposals, the President deciding disputes — template) and who chooses symposium speakers (the template: the host proposes and the Federation's committee decides jointly; the Federation's recent practice: itself alone) are terms of the agreement, and the conflict between the template and practice is Q-125; the platform records the inviting act by whichever seat the agreement names and refuses nothing. The 2025 host's request for a deadline on the Federation's keynote choice is a date on the row.

2.7 The ad book, sponsorship and solicitation

  • The ad book: one per family and to every advertiser at a stated size or more (template) — a fulfilment rule on the event's ad-book product, shown to the host.
  • No solicitation of ad-book donations outside the host's community (template) — a term the agreement states; the platform's giving pages and the host's own channels are outside its sight, so the rule is recorded and not policed.
  • No solicitation at the Convention for causes the Federation has not approved (template; the shape of 11.1.4) — the catalogue of purposes (D8) is the list of approved causes on the platform; an appeal at the Convention through the platform must name a catalogue purpose or the entity's Board's dated act, and anything else is refused by name; off-platform solicitation is the Executive Committee's to act on.
  • Sponsorship packages: products on the event or on a programme's event inside it (the Endowment's golf outing processed through ARFECF in 2026), each naming the entity that receipts it (D9: the ticket part non-deductible; a gift beyond it receipted by the entity) and the programme its money is allocated to (the partner-agreements workflow's sponsorship step); which entity receipts a package sold through the Federation's portal where the package benefits an affiliate's fund, and whether sponsorship paid to a Mid-Year host club by cheque is the club's money (D1 treatment 3, by channel), is Q-92, joining P8's Q-92.
  • A share of the ad book or sponsorship to the host club is a term kind the agreement may carry (the 2024 Federation-led Convention returned one; the CIC has discussed one) and a line type the statement already has (the club plan §5.2; Q-47, carried); without a term the line does not arise.

2.8 Insurance, security, the hotel and the contracts

Obligation rows with evidence required: the host's liability insurance naming the Federation as additional insured (minimums as register figures); the professional security contract with a copy to the Federation; the hotel contract — the host's choice approved by the Federation Convention Committee (a decision item, §2.1) and the contract read by the Legal Advisor (a dated act) before the host signs; copies of every signed contract within the stated days (a clock per row); the host never signs for the Federation (template; 11.1.1 for the Federation's own instruments). Each row shows filed on with the drive link or not on file, and the Liaison's planning report (10.1.7) lists the rows not on file. 2.9 Approval, signature and versions

The agreement's final language is approved by the Executive Committee including the Legal Advisor (11.1.1), a dated decision item on the Executive Committee's queue (CW4) linked from the row; the signatures by seat (§2.1) are dated acts; the row's status moves draft → approved → signed → in force → closed (P11 §3's states, with approved before signed for the 11.1.1 act and closed on the settlement's close; renewal due, lapsed and superseded apply as P11 gives them). A signed agreement is frozen (R43's pattern): an amendment is a new version with its own 11.1.1 approval and signatures, the earlier version kept; a floor motion is never a version (D59; P8-J09). An agreement for a Mid-Year is the same object with the Mid-Year template (§7) and the Executive Committee's decision in place of the General Assembly's (P8 §3); whether every Mid-Year needs one is P8's Q-92, carried, and without one the settlement is refused (P8 §4).

4. The close and the settlement statement

The close is the convention workflow's steps 8 and 9 and the club plan §5.3's run, with the agreement as the only source of terms. The settlement statement the platform produces is a dated document with these lines, each with the agreement field it reads:

  1. Gross receipts on the platform under the convention class, by product: registration (by class), banquet seats, sub-events, ad book and sponsorship sold through the portal, store items (R23; D1 G).
  2. Less dues and the 5.2 fees and donations collected with registration — shown, remitted to the General Treasurer, and excluded from the gross (5.2: never proceeds; P8-J11).
  3. Less the per-registration fund amounts (§2.2), posted to the named fund or to the liability of "the entity to be named".
  4. Less the Federation's share as the agreement expresses it (fixed, percentage, hybrid or formula), with the computation shown where the kind allows one and refused where an input is not a ledger figure.
  5. Reimbursable obligations each way under the schedule (§2.3): an item one side paid for the other, with its evidence link and its ceiling check; a row with side: not stated is listed and not settled.
  6. Speaker sub-agreements paid, by payer, with any variance shown (§2.6).
  7. Breach costs, if any, as a dated line with the Executive Committee's decision number (§5), and the breach-retention rule applied only by that act.
  8. The holdback: held during the period, released on this statement.
  9. The balance, as the Convention settlement or Mid-Year settlement line on the host club's statement (C3; the club plan §5.2), with the agreement as its evidence link; a balance the host owes is the same line the other way.
  10. Deficit responsibility read from the agreement's field; where not stated the statement prints "deficit responsibility: not stated in the agreement" and assigns nothing (board-confirmation item 2).

Two clocks. The release clock (the settlement period, template: sixty days) is the Federation's duty: the Federation's treasurer produces the statement and the accounting to the host of registration data, dues collected and the fund amount per attendee (template), and the release posts. The accounting clock (10.1.7: 120 days) is the host's: the final accounting of the host's own income and expenses — the dedicated account's records, the duplicate receipts (template) — is a document on the drive linked from the row with its filed date, received by the Executive Committee and then the Finance Committee for audit as two dated acts (template), and the Federation's choice of its own auditor a third. The two clocks are independent in the template, so the release may precede the final accounting; whether the Board wants the release to wait, and what the Finance Committee's audit step requires of the platform's own figures, is Q-128. The Liaison's post-Convention report (10.1.7) is a document on the committee workspace (CW2; P8 §5) that lists both clocks' states.

Immutability and queries. A closed statement is immutable; a correction is a new line on the next run naming the one it corrects (the club plan §5.3); a query by the host's treasurer holds the queried line and settles the rest (§5.3), and escalates as §5 says. The Mid-Year's statement is the same object; with no agreement row it is refused, naming D59 (P8-J08).

What the platform cannot see. Off-platform money — sponsorship cheques to a Mid-Year host, the host's own vendors — is in the host's final accounting and nowhere in the statement, unless the agreement makes a share of it a term (§2.7). The statement says so in a standing footnote, so that nobody reads the platform's gross as the Convention's.

Since 2 October 2026 (P22 §4, §9; CV6). The Convention operations note turns the close into dated acts by seats:

  • the office's draft statement;
  • a reconciliation meeting at which each correction is a line naming its basis;
  • the host's confirm-or-query;
  • approvals by the seats the Board sets (the approver set is a parameter, Q-221);
  • payment by the General Treasurer, with both signature texts shown (11.1.2; 7.5.1–7.5.2; Q-241) and the channel recorded (Q-222);
  • the item in the Board packet.

In practice (research, 2 Oct 2026), the 2026 statement netted items each way that had no schedule row. 5. Disputes, breach and the record against future bids

  • The dispute term. The template makes the Federation's determination binding and the Executive Committee the final decision-maker. That is a term the agreement states, labelled template on the row, never a by-law; an agreement that names another forum (the register's witness and dispute-forum fields, P11 §3) is honoured as it reads.
  • The path. A disagreement about a line is first a query on the settlement (the club plan §5.3): the host's treasurer or the Federation's raises it, the line is held, an item opens with an owner and a clock. Unresolved, it becomes a decision item on the Executive Committee's queue (CW4) under the dispute term, with the Legal Advisor as consultee; the outcome is a dated act that releases or adjusts the line as a new line naming the first.
  • Breach. A breach (registration taken outside the portal; a contract signed in the Federation's name; a required evidence row never filed; solicitation for an unapproved cause) is a fact the Executive Committee records against the agreement as a dated act with its ground; the breach-cost line and the breach-retention rule (§2.2) follow only from that act. The platform detects no breach; it shows the evidence rows not on file and the clocks overrun.
  • Against future bids. "Non-compliance may count against future bids" (template) is recorded as a fact on the agreement by the Executive Committee's act and shown beside that club's next bid to whoever decides (P8 §3); it refuses nothing.

6. The archive and the history row

The signed agreement and its versions, the hotel and security contracts, the insurance certificate, the final accounting, the Liaison's reports and the host's lessons letter are drive documents linked from the row (D66); the platform keeps the rows, the clocks and the links. For each Convention and Mid-Year the platform keeps a history row — the counts the Convention Innovation Committee's data template wants: registrations by class and product, attendance by sub-event, delegates credentialed, the settlement's balance each way, the survey's response count and overall score as the committee enters it, the hosting model, the host club — as counts and never names (S7), which is the archive's index and the first-year baseline for the junction (D67). The convention playbook the committee wants is a drive document the agreement template links to, not platform content; the survey's raw responses stay outside and restricted (the committee's own rule). The template's clause that all records the host creates are the Federation's is satisfied by the platform holding them and by the host's event roles ending on the row's close (the club plan §4.1's hand-over pattern), with the host club's statement keeping its lines.

7. The three hosting models as templates

The Convention Innovation Committee named three models: a host city (the club runs the Convention and raises locally; the 2022 template's shape); a hybrid (the Federation plans with a smaller club raising; the 2024 Federation-led Convention's shape, which the committee has proposed to test on a small club); the Federation as central host with local clubs soliciting sponsors for a percentage. Each is a template on the agreements register from which the Executive Committee may start an agreement: it pre-fills the kinds of the money terms (§2.2 — the host-city template a fixed or hybrid share; the central-host template a percentage to clubs on sponsorship), the sides on the obligation rows (§2.3), the registration and seating terms (§2.4), the banquet schedule (§2.5) and the ad-book rules (§2.7), with every amount, percentage, ceiling and period blank. Choosing a template is no decision (P8's rule 7); the share's kind and value for future Conventions go from the committee to the Board (D59), and which models a bid may propose, whether a central fund for small-city Conventions is created and whether the Federation may host with no bidding club are P8's Q-94, carried. A fourth template, the Mid-Year, carries FED2's guideline obligations (rooms for the Board, the General Assembly, the Past Presidents and Leadership Ramallah; a room block; a banquet hall; registration with Federation staff; fees agreed with the leadership and sharp increases approved in writing by the Executive Committee) as rows labelled FED2, and the practice's split (the host's hotel contract, social programme, banquet and sponsorship; the Federation's sessions and registration) as sides, all blank until signed, under Q-92 (P8, P12 and this note).

8. Rules

  1. The Convention is a function of the Federation; the host's and the Federation's rights and obligations derive from the Convention Contract Guidelines (10.1.6), which are not on file (P8 Q-93); every Convention runs under a host agreement signed before it recording the share, the holdback, the settlement date and each side's obligations, the platform records the terms on the event and produces the settlement statement, a change to the share goes from the Convention Innovation Committee to the Board and never happens on the floor, and the same shape serves the Mid-Year (D59).
  2. The Executive Committee including the Legal Advisor approves the final language before execution (11.1.1); the agreement is frozen on signature and amended only by a new version with the same approval (design choice, R43's pattern).
  3. Dues and the 5.2 fees and donations collected with registration are separate from and in addition to the registration fee, are remitted to the General Treasurer, and never constitute or offset the host's proceeds (5.2); the General Treasurer gives the host the paid-members list thirty days before (5.2).
  4. The President appoints the Convention Liaison from the Council of Past Presidents; the final accounting reaches the office no later than 120 days after the Convention (10.1.7); a longer typed period is refused by name.
  5. Convention money is Federation money at the first dollar (D1 G); platform-collected receipts are Federation-held and settled under the agreement, off-platform receipts are the collector's (D1's treatments); event money routes by the host field (R23); a banquet seat is inventory (R26); a ticketed event is non-deductible and a gift beyond it is receipted separately by its entity (D9).
  6. Every money term is typed from the signed text with its drive page; the settlement reads the agreement's fields and nothing else; a term not stated computes nothing and the screen says so (design choice under D59; R40).
  7. The settlement is a line on the host club's statement (D57; the club plan §5.2); a query holds one line; a closed statement is immutable (the club plan §5.3).
  8. The registration data is the Federation's (template; D1 G); the member's record is the member's (R27); the host's coordinator role is a scoped grant on the event reading no member record (Four-Lens §6; design choice).
  9. A money-taking event cannot publish without its surplus and shortfall rules on the ticket page (D7); an appeal at the Convention names a catalogue purpose or an entity Board's act (D8; the template's unapproved-causes rule; 11.1.4's shape).
  10. Exhibitor and press are non-attendee credentials; deficit responsibility reads from the agreement and the screen says where it is silent (the register's board-confirmation items).
  11. The banquet programme, the head table, the ad-book rules, the hotel approval, the insurance and security requirements, the keynote's invitation and the dispute term are the template's terms, carried as schedules the agreement may take and labelled template; none is a rule until signed.
  12. A speaker agreement is a sub-agreement with its own payer and signatory row; a variance from the host agreement's schedule is shown and never corrected (design choice).
  13. A breach is recorded only by the Executive Committee's dated act; the platform detects none (design choice); "may count against future bids" is a fact shown beside a bid, never a refusal (template; P8 §3).
  14. Documents live on the drive and the platform holds rows, clocks and links (D66); the history row holds counts and never names (S7; D67).
  15. The three hosting models and the Mid-Year are templates with blank amounts; choosing one is no decision (P8 rule 7; D59).
  16. Every refusal names its rule; where the record is silent the screen says so (R40).
