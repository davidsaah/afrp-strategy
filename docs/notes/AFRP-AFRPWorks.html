Design note P17 · 1 October 2026 · for the Hub's programs, network (the job board), member, comms, ledger and committees (CW1) modules, and the agreements register of P11 (AFRP-Care-Grants-and-Partners.md §3)

What it rests on. Crosswalk row A8 (the September 2026 refresh priorities: "Ramallah Works" among them) and the crosswalk's P17 row ("A programme page only (D64); its owning committee and fee model wait on the working group"). Decision 64 in full: "AFRPWorks, the Ramallah Jobs Initiative, is a programme on the Leadership branch beside the RBPN job board, held by AFRP", adopted by David on 1 October 2026, settling Q-39 and Q-12 and leaving "its owning committee and fee model, which the working group names". The programme register's afrp-works entry (site/data/programmes.yaml), read in full: what the working group's minutes of September 2026 say the initiative is (member employers in the United States matched with skilled professionals in Ramallah for gig, part-time and full-time work, so that income reaches Ramallah households; a prototype outside the Hub with intake forms for employers and candidates and a searchable candidate repository, built by a volunteer developer; a pilot sourced through a partner organisation in Ramallah for one to two months from an autumn 2026 soft launch, widened if supply or matches stay low; gigs on direct contracts and full-time roles through a local employer of record; employer profiles private; first openings from member-owned businesses before candidates are invited; sector focus in legal assistance, accounting, information technology, design and medical records; no fee, sponsor, budget line, account or signed agreement recorded; data classes "not stated; no D61 rule names this programme"). The rbpn entry's own lines on the boundary ("Where does the RBPN job board end and AFRPWorks begin?"). The Network Plan (AFRP-Camp-Directory-Network-Plan.md) findings 3 and 4: LinkedIn offers no API that would do any of this and a share link is the whole list; on a job board the salary range is AFRP's own legal obligation as third-party publisher, with state thresholds keyed to where the work is performed, so the range is a required structured field universally with benefits and a closing date, and open-ended ranges are prohibited in two states. The job board as built in slice 10 (AFRP-Delivery-Status.md): the hybrid of the Network Plan — a public posting page with complete markup, applying at sign-in; every posting with its salary range, benefits, pay period, closing date and apply path, the laws named when one is missing; an expired posting gone with its markup; the RBPN desk verifying membership and ownership of a listing and refusing under a named line of the exclusion policy. The directory formats note (P19 §3): the professional services directory is the RBPN listing, members only, the place of business as listing data. D60 (a non-member's contact record: name, verified channel, consent; never a membership) and D61 (applicant data and documents never enter the platform; past participants as alumni records only) as the two principles for people who are not members. D55 (the holding entity is the fund that budgets the programme), D65 (the Federation's own activity is the General Fund's), D66 (the drive is the archive of record), D67 (the year-one baseline). P4 (the contact record as the Family Referral note uses it) and P12 §2 (the applicant as a contact record on D60's precedent). The intakes of September–October 2026 (D54; role-level only): the Federation's AFRPWorks folder (a member announcement of September 2026 describing two account types — candidates in Ramallah with a profile, skills, experience and availability, and employers who browse "verified candidate profiles" and "connect directly" — and the working group's minutes of the same month, with its open items: portal or managed service; who verifies candidates; who holds the legal and tax exposure when a US employer hires an individual; no pricing, fee or sponsor; which committee owns it; candidate data privacy), and the Board's minutes of September 2026 reporting the pilot as "intended to become part of RBPN". Outside sources at the end, used only to say what the Legal Advisor must be asked.

Precedence (D41). D64 is the only decision. Then the programme register entry and the Network Plan, whose job-board findings slice 10 built. Then the working group's own minutes and announcement — practice, read through the intake, and in two places disagreeing with each other (§1). The prototype outside the Hub is the working group's and is evidence of what it wants, never of a rule. The Hub's job board is what is; this note says what it would carry for a Ramallah posting and what it must not.

What this note is, mostly. Questions. D64 placed the programme and named what it did not settle; the working group's minutes are a month old and most of what the platform needs from them is undecided there too. The note gives each object the smallest shape the record supports so that, when the working group answers, a build session has one thing to build; it does not pick a fee, an owner, a verifier or a retention period, and where the record is silent it says so.

1. What the record already holds

Decided. D64: the branch (Leadership), the neighbour (the RBPN job board), the holder (AFRP). By D55's logic the programme's money, if any, is AFRP's General Fund (the register's money: "Any fee to employers would be AFRP's"); by D65's logic it is Federation activity and not the affiliates'. Nothing else.

Built. The RBPN job board (slice 10): a posting object with employer, title, description, salary range (required, structured, with the state laws named when it is missing), benefits, pay period, closing date and an apply path; a public posting page with schema.org markup; applying at sign-in by a member; an expired posting returned as gone; the employer's counts; the RBPN desk's verification of a listing's membership and ownership and its refusal under the exclusion policy; the promoted business listing and the free profession listing on the professional services directory (P19 §3). The Hub has no candidate object that is not a member, no introduction object, no programme page for AFRPWorks beyond the register entry's rendering, and no journey for any of this (the register entry's journeys row is zero).

Practice (the intakes, role-level). A working group of the Federation — the President, a volunteer developer, member-business owners — met in September 2026 and named the initiative; a member announcement followed. The prototype: intake forms for employers and candidates and a searchable repository of candidate profiles. The pilot: candidates sourced in Ramallah through a community organisation's existing database, with that organisation's leadership running local outreach; the Federation running intake and matching; one to two months; widened if thin. Employment structures the group described, as the member employers' own arrangements and not the Federation's: project or gig work on a direct contract; full-time roles through a local management company or HR agency in Ramallah so that the US firm is a client, not a foreign employer. The group's promotion channel is the village-society conventions; no club role is written. The Board heard in September 2026 that the pilot was targeted for the start of November and "intended to become part of RBPN". The group's own open items are listed above and are carried into §9.

Where the practice disagrees with itself. The announcement says employers "browse verified candidate profiles" and "connect directly"; the minutes say employer profiles stay private from public browsing and the group runs intake and matching. "Verified" is not defined anywhere. The announcement describes candidate accounts on a web platform; the minutes describe the partner's database as the source. The register entry records the first disagreement; this note designs for the minutes (brokered, not browsed, §3.4) because the minutes are the group's decision and the announcement is its marketing, and raises it (Q-155).

2. The programme page

AFRPWorks is a programme on the register, Leadership branch, order 3, shape other, status Plan only, rendered as every programme is from programmes.yaml's eight headings and the intake's added keys. The page says what the register says and nothing more: what it is in the working group's words; who it serves (member employers; professionals in Ramallah); the operating year (a soft launch, a pilot, a review); the five rungs as the entry gives them with lead "not stated"; the organiser ("a working group of the Federation; the owning committee is not yet named, D64"); money ("not stated"); the hand-offs (fed by the RBPN job board and the Convention's business gatherings; feeds RBPN); and what is missing, which is this note's §9 by number. Its sources list D64, the crosswalk, this note and the drive folder (D66). The page carries no employer's name, no candidate, no count below the floor, and no claim about the partner organisation beyond "a partner organisation in Ramallah" until an agreement is on P11's register (§6).

The page is the one deliverable the crosswalk's P17 row says is ready. Everything from §3 on is gated.

3. The objects, on the job board that exists

The pilot needs five things the Hub does not have and one it does. The design principle, labelled: nothing new where the job board already has it, and nothing about a person in Ramallah beyond what D60 allows a non-member.

3.1 The employer

A member, or a member's business from the professional services directory listing (P19 §3; slice 10's verified ownership). The record says the first openings come from member-owned businesses; the announcement's "thousands of businesses" is a hope, not an eligibility rule. The platform's employer is therefore the job board's existing poster: a member in good standing whose business listing the RBPN desk has verified for membership and ownership. Whether a non-member employer, or a member's employer that the member does not own, may post for Ramallah is not written (Q-154); until answered the board's existing rule holds (a member posts; the desk verifies ownership of a business listing). The minutes' rule that employer profiles stay private from public browsing is honoured by the existing shape: the professional services directory is members-only, and a posting page shows the employer as the posting names it, which the employer chooses at posting (design choice, carried from slice 10).

3.2 The posting

The job board's existing object with one addition: a work location of Ramallah (or remote from Ramallah), which the board reads for two purposes — the salary-range law names (the Network Plan keyed the state thresholds to where the work is performed; a Ramallah posting triggers no US state's threshold on that key, and the field stays required universally by design, so the posting carries its range regardless and the "laws named when missing" line says "required by AFRP's rule for every posting" rather than naming a state) and the programme attribution (a posting with this location counts for AFRPWorks's pilot, §7). The range's currency and pay period for a Ramallah posting are not written — the board's field is a US-dollar field today and the minutes describe monthly full-time pay — and are raised (Q-157). The contract route the minutes describe (direct contract for a gig; a local employer of record for a full-time role) is a structured field on the posting chosen by the employer from the two the group named, shown on the posting so a candidate knows what they are being introduced to, and recorded on the introduction (§3.4); the platform states the route and never arranges it. The public posting page (slice 10's hybrid) stays public: a posting is an employer's offer, not a person's data, and the Network Plan's reason for the public page (indexing) holds for Ramallah; whether the working group wants Ramallah postings members-only instead is part of Q-155.

3.3 The candidate

A person in Ramallah is never a member (Article IV's membership is a different thing and the initiative does not offer it), never on any directory (P19's three formats are members only), and never an applicant in D61's sense (no résumé, essay or document enters). The platform holds a contact record on D60's pattern, as P4 and P12 §2 already use it: a name; one verified channel; the person's own consent rows (R27), given at the door in words the Legal Advisor approves in Arabic and English (Q-151); and, for this programme only, a profile the person types about themselves: skills as tags from a list the working group maintains, the sectors the group named as the first list; years of experience as a band; availability (long-term, contract or gig; hours); and location as Ramallah or the wider area once the pilot widens. No document — a CV is the person's to send an employer after an introduction, by their own means, and the platform does not store or forward one (design choice, from D61's principle). No identity document, no date of birth (the person attests to being an adult at the door; R7's possible-minor rule is for the directory and the contact record is on none), no salary history, no references, no photograph.

Verification. The announcement promises "verified" profiles and the minutes ask who verifies. The platform can record that a named party verified a profile on a date (the partner organisation under its agreement, §6; or the working group), as a dated attestation on the record, and can show "verified by on " to an employer; it cannot verify anything itself and says so. What verification means — identity, qualifications, availability — is the working group's to define (Q-150).

The partner's database stays the partner's. The minutes source candidates from the Ramallah organisation's existing database. The platform does not import it: a person enters the platform's contact record only by their own act at the door (D60; S1's verified channel; P4's rule that the platform sends nothing to a channel someone else typed). The partner invites; the person consents. The partner's own list, its consents and its law are the partner's, under the agreement (§6).

How long, and who sees. The record is silent on retention; this is a row for P15's schedule (contact records held for a programme), with a period the working group proposes and the Board adopts, and the person's withdrawal at any time closing the record (P15 §4; R27). Who reads the profile: the working group's seats (or the owning committee's once named, §5), for matching; an employer sees a profile only through an introduction (§3.4) and never by browsing; the Federation lens sees counts (S7). The profile never appears in search, in the directory, in the Magazine or in any export (P19 rule 6).

3.4 The introduction

The match is a brokered contact, the directory's broker pattern (the Network Plan's "contact through a broker so the address itself never ships") applied to a person who is not a member. An introduction is a dated row: the posting; the candidate; who proposed it (the working group's seat); the contract route the posting states; its state — proposed, candidate agreed, employer agreed, introduced (both channels released to each other, once, through the platform's message), declined (by either, no reason recorded), closed (an outcome recorded or the posting expired). Neither party's channel reaches the other before both have agreed; after introduction the two talk by their own means and the platform holds no CV, no interview, no offer and no contract. The outcome is one of a short list the group names (placed — gig, placed — full-time through the employer of record, not placed, unknown) recorded by the working group when it learns it, never by inference, and is the only thing the pilot's counts are built from (§7). The employer's "connect directly" of the announcement is this, with the broker in between; the register's take_part rung ("a match: a gig or role placed") is an introduction closed as placed.

What the introduction never does. Export a profile; forward a document; place the candidate on the employer's books (the employer-of-record arrangement is the employer's and the local firm's, §8); record pay agreed (the posting's range is the employer's statement; what was agreed is not the Federation's business and is not held); rank candidates (the working group chooses; the platform lists profiles matching a posting's tags for the group and sorts by nothing it would have to defend).

3.5 The programme seats

On CW1 (P1): the working group as a body of kind created committee (8.1.1) if the President created it, or of no kind until the owning committee is named — the record does not say who constituted the group, and the Board's minutes say the pilot is "intended to become part of RBPN", which would make the RBPN Committee the owner and the group its sub-committee (Q-147). Until answered the body is a register row "AFRPWorks working group; no appointing text", its seats typed by the office with "no appointing text" as P1 §2.2 allows, and its matching reads scoped to this programme alone (program:afrp-works), which keeps the candidates' profiles out of every other programme's and committee's reach (Four-Lens §6.2: programme scope is a sibling of everything else).

4. The fee model, as a blank

The record names no fee and no sponsor; the register says "Any fee to employers would be AFRP's"; the minutes framed the proposition to employers as savings and discussed no budget. The working group names the model (D64). The options the record itself names, listed and not chosen:

Option Where the record names it What the platform would do
None — a member service, costs in the General Fund The minutes (no fee discussed); the RBPN pattern (the free profession listing) Nothing on the ledger; the programme's cost is a General Fund line the Finance Committee proposes (8.7.1) if the Board wants one
A sponsor fee — a member business or donor underwrites the pilot The RBPN pattern (sponsorship desk, slice 10); the register's money line A sponsorship recorded through the existing desk as AFRP's receipt, attributed to the programme; the sponsor acknowledged as the desk already does
A placement fee — the employer pays on a placement The register's open question ("whether placements are recorded on AFRP's books"); the minutes' local-HR-agency model, where a fee is the agency's A fee as AFRP's General Fund receipt through the one payment door on an introduction closed as placed; this is the option with consequences the Legal Advisor and the CPA must speak to — a fee for brokering employment may bear on AFRP's 501(c)(4) activity and on state employment-agency licensing where the employer sits, and the record holds nothing on either

A fourth shape — a fee to the candidate — is listed only to be excluded: nothing in the record proposes it and the initiative's stated purpose (income to Ramallah households) argues against it; design choice, labelled, offered to the working group. Until the group and the Board decide, the platform's programme page says "fee: not decided" and the ledger refuses a receipt attributed to this programme by name (Q-148).

5. The owning committee, as a body

D64 leaves the owner to the working group. Once named — the RBPN Committee, as the Board heard; a new committee the President creates (8.1.1); or the working group constituted as one — the owner is a Body on P1's register with the programme linked (P1 §2.1's programme-committee kind, "so the committee's workspace and the programme's plan are one page from two sides"), its seats dated with their authority, its budget line if any (§4), its drive folder (D66), its seat channel, and its decisions queue carrying this note's questions as items (P1 §3.4; D43's one number in both places). Until then the working group's row of §3.5 stands and every question in §9 whose owner is "working group" sits on the Board's queue instead, because an unconstituted group has no queue.

6. The partner agreement

The register entry's agreements list two partners with "no signed agreement was found": the Ramallah organisation that sources candidates and leads outreach, and a local employer of record "to be evaluated". The first is the Federation's partner and belongs on P11's agreements register (§3 of that note: parties, kind, programme, term, renewal, obligations, the signed document on the drive) with kind partner memorandum, entity AFRP (D64), programme AFRPWorks, term the pilot's, and the obligations the minutes describe — the partner invites and sources, the Federation runs intake and matching — plus the ones this note needs the agreement to state: that a candidate enters the platform only by their own consent (§3.3); what the partner verifies (§3.3; Q-150); that the partner's database and its consents are the partner's; and what each side may say publicly about the other. The second is not the Federation's partner at all: the employer of record is the employer's contractor for the employer's own hire, and the Federation is not a party; the platform records the route on the posting and holds no agreement (design choice, from the minutes' own description of the model). Whether the Federation should nonetheless hold a list of such firms it has evaluated, as a referral and not an endorsement, is the working group's (Q-149).

7. The pilot as a dated cycle, with its exit counts

The pilot is a cycle on the programme (the selection engine's object, P12 §2, used here without its application door): a start (the soft launch), a review date (one to two months later, as the minutes say), a close, and the decision at review (widen the area, continue, stop) recorded as the owning body's decision item. Its counts are D67's kind — the two or three that exist from the objects above and need no measure invented: postings opened with the Ramallah location; candidates with a consented record; introductions made; introductions closed as placed, by route. All under the floor (S7); the Federation lens and the Board packet (P1 §4.3) carry them; the Leadership branch's year-one baseline includes them once the owner confirms (D67's "counts that exist today"). The minutes' own trigger — "if supply or match volume stays low" — is read off these counts at the review; the platform computes no threshold.

8. What the Legal Advisor must be asked, before an introduction is made

The platform is a US non-profit's system introducing US employers to workers in the West Bank. Four matters are outside the record and this note does not answer them; it names them at the level of "ask the Legal Advisor" (7.8.1 makes the Legal Advisor available to the Executive Director and the Board for exactly this), with the primary sources a reader can start from.

  1. Sanctions screening. The Treasury's West Bank-related sanctions programme under E.O. 14115 was terminated on 20 January 2025 (E.O. 14148 revoked it; OFAC removed the designations and unblocked the property on 24 January 2025 — see Sources). That removes one programme; it does not remove the general obligation of any US person not to deal with persons on the Specially Designated Nationals list under the programmes that remain (the counter-terrorism programme among them), and OFAC's public list search is the tool. Whether AFRP, as a broker that neither pays nor employs anyone, must itself screen candidates, employers and the partner organisation, or whether that duty is each employer's, is the Legal Advisor's to say; the platform can record that a screening was done by a named party on a date, as a dated attestation on the introduction, and can refuse an introduction without one if the Advisor says so (Q-152).
  2. Export controls. Remote work that involves a US employer's technical data or software reaching a worker abroad can be a "deemed" or actual export under the Export Administration Regulations for some data; this is the employer's obligation and is raised here only so that the posting's sector field (information technology, design) can carry a line the Advisor approves, if the Advisor wants one (Q-152).
  3. Employment and tax exposure. The minutes describe a direct contract for gigs and a local employer of record for full-time roles precisely so that the US firm avoids foreign-entity registration and withholding; that is the employer's arrangement and the Federation's only act is to state the route on the posting. Whether a placement fee (§4) makes AFRP an employment agency under the law of any state where an employer sits, and whether brokering is within AFRP's exempt purpose as the Board describes it, is for the Legal Advisor and the CPA (Q-148, Q-152).
  4. Immigration is out of scope unless travel is involved. The record describes remote work from Ramallah and says nothing about any placement involving travel to the United States; if a placement ever did, it would be the employer's visa matter and nothing in this note applies to it. The platform states, on every Ramallah posting, that the work is performed in Ramallah (§3.2) and holds no field for travel.

The Legal Advisor's answers become attested rows on P15's law register (that note's §2.5 pattern) and a line on the programme page; until they exist the introduction object (§3.4) is built but no introduction leaves the proposed state — the gate on AW2 — and the programme page says why.

9. Rules

  1. AFRPWorks is a programme on the Leadership branch held by AFRP; its money, if any, is the General Fund's (D64; D55; D65).
  2. A person in Ramallah is a contact record on D60's pattern — name, verified channel, consent, a self-typed profile of tags and bands — and never a member, never on a directory, never an applicant with documents (D60; D61; P19 rules 1 and 6; design choice for the profile's fields; D60 is written for the Arabic term and its use for a candidate is a reading, raised as one question for governance with P4's Q-70).
  3. A contact record exists only by the person's own act at the door; the partner's database is never imported and the platform sends nothing to a channel another party typed (D60; S1; P4 §2).
  4. An employer is a member whose business listing the RBPN desk has verified, until the working group says otherwise (slice 10; Q-154).
  5. A Ramallah posting is the job board's posting with a work location and a contract route; it carries its salary range, benefits, pay period and closing date like every posting (the Network Plan finding 4; slice 10; design choice for the two fields).
  6. The match is a brokered introduction: no channel ships until both parties agree; no document is stored or forwarded; the outcome is recorded by the working group from a short list, never inferred (the Network Plan's broker; design choice).
  7. Profiles are read by the owning body's seats at programme scope and by an employer only through an introduction; never searched, listed, printed or exported (Four-Lens §6.2; S7; P19 rule 6).
  8. Verification is a dated attestation by a named party; the platform verifies nothing (design choice; Q-150).
  9. The fee model is a blank until the working group and the Board fill it; the ledger refuses a receipt attributed to the programme until then (D64; Q-148).
  10. No introduction leaves proposed until the Legal Advisor's answers in §8 are attested (7.8.1; R40; design choice).
  11. The pilot is a dated cycle whose counts are D67's kind, under the floor; the platform computes no threshold for widening (D67; S7).
  12. The partner's agreement is a row on P11's register; the employer of record is the employer's contractor and not the Federation's partner (P11 §3; design choice).
  13. Retention of a candidate's record is a P15 schedule row with no value until set; withdrawal closes the record at once (P15 §3, §4; R27).
  14. Where the record is silent the platform refuses or says "not decided" and routes the question (R40). Every refusal names its rule.
