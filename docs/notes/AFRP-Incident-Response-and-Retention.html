2. The incident as an object

2.1 Kinds

An incident is a dated row on the incident register, an AFRP object in the federation scope (an affiliate's incident about its own store is recorded on its lens and reported up under its reporting duty — ARFHSN's six-monthly report, D63). Its kind is one of:

  • breach — unauthorised access to or acquisition of data the platform or the Federation holds (a compromised account, a stolen export, a vendor's breach reaching Federation data);
  • loss — data the Federation must hold is gone or unreadable (a database expiry, a failed migration, a drive folder deleted, a backup that will not restore);
  • wrong disclosure — the platform or a person showed data to someone the rules do not allow (a minor in a payload, an affiliate's row in the federation scope, a withheld member in a mailing, a follow-up seen by its subject, a printed roster with contact details);
  • a refusal that failed — a binding rule the platform enforces was crossed (the clock case; a refused export that succeeded; an override that should not exist) — a sub-kind of wrong disclosure or breach, kept separate so the build team can read the register by rule;
  • vendor incident — the hosting provider, the payment processor, the e-mail or texting provider, the drive's provider, a club's Breeze or the bought camp health record reports an incident that may reach Federation data;
  • account compromise — a mailbox, a sign-in or a role taken over, whether or not data was reached (the mid-2026 case);
  • near miss — found before any data was reached; recorded so the review step runs.

2.2 The clock

The clock runs from discovery: the moment a person with a Federation seat or a vendor's notice first knew. The row holds discovered on, occurred on (if known; "unknown" is a value), contained on, assessed on, notified on (per audience), closed on. Every law in §2.5 measures from discovery, so the platform shows elapsed time from discovery on the row and on the custodian's desk, with no deadline computed until the Legal Advisor attests which law's deadline applies (§2.5) — the same pattern as every unset parameter in the Hub (a refusal names its authority; the figure is not guessed).

2.4 The steps

Each step is a dated act on the row by a named seat; the row cannot close with a step missing; a step may be recorded as "not needed" with a reason by the seat that owns it (the Legal Advisor for notify).

  1. Contain. Take the affected thing out of reach: end the grant (R39's exact grants, ended never deleted — a compromised account's grants end that minute and the end is the audit line), revoke the session, rotate the secret (the secret store), take the export down, suspend the vendor connector, halt a send. The platform offers the first three as one act on the row ("contain this account") and logs each. The FTC's "secure your operations" is this step.
  2. Assess. What was reached: the data classes (§3's rows, ticked), the people (a count under the floor on the row; the list held on the row for the custodian and the Legal Advisor only, never on any report), whether any was encrypted or tokenised (card data is never in the platform — a payments incident reaches tokens, last-four and expiry only; the Delivery Status payments slice), whether a minor's data was reached (R7, R6), which states the affected people live in (read from the member records, computed once, shown as counts per state), whether a vendor is the maintainer of the data (MCL 445.72's owner/maintainer split). The technical findings are a drive document (D66). The assessment is the Legal Advisor's attested act.
  3. Notify. The Legal Advisor attests, per audience, whether, by when and under which law (§2.5): law enforcement (the FTC guide: call before notifying individuals if the investigation requires it; MCL 445.72 lets law enforcement delay notice); affected individuals (a letter on P3's register — LT-I1, class operational, a member must receive it, signatory the President's seat, wording open, carrying the FTC's content: what happened, what was taken, what the Federation did, what the person can do, whom to contact); the state Attorney General where a law requires it (California's sample copy above 500 residents, 1798.82(f)); the processor or bank where account data was involved (the FTC guide); the insurer; the affected entity's Board; the AFRP Board. Each notice is a dated act with the audience, the channel and the count; the letter's send runs through the comms desk's operational class with the withheld named — and a withheld member with no channel is listed for the office to reach by post (P3 §6, LT4), because a breach notice is owed regardless of marketing consent.
  4. Record. The row, its acts, the drive links, the counts, the law rows attested, the letter rows sent. Nothing about a person beyond the affected list held for the two seats. The record is frozen on close (R43's pattern); a later finding is a new linked row.
  5. Review. Within a period the Board sets (a parameter with no value; Q-139), the custodian, the Legal Advisor and the technical responder record the cause, the rule that failed or held, the change made (a locking test, a policy, a vendor change), and whether the retention schedule (§3) would have reduced the harm (a loss of data the Federation no longer needed is a schedule failure, not only a breach). The review is a drive document; the row holds its structured fields and the link. The Hub's own practice — a finding in the findings ledger with a fix and a locking test — is the review for a refusal that failed, and the row links the finding.

2.6 The register, and what the Board sees

The incident register is the custodian's desk: open rows with their clocks, the steps outstanding by seat, closed rows with their reviews. The Board sees, in its packet (CW4): the count of incidents by kind and state this period, every row of kind breach or wrong disclosure that touched a real person (the row's structured fields, never the affected list), every notice sent (audience, law row, date, count), every review's change, and the retention schedule's rows still silent (§3) — so the Board sees what it has not decided beside what it cost. The affiliates' Boards see their own scope's rows. Members see nothing of the register; a member sees the notice they were sent on their own record (LT-I1 as a dated letter row).

3. The retention schedule

The schedule is a register of rows per data class. Each row carries: the class; where it lives (the platform, the drive, a vendor); the keep period; the rule or law it rests on; the deletion method; who may order deletion; what survives (always the counts and the ledger); and the status — attested (a by-law, a decision, a ratified document or a law says it), proposed (a design document or an outside standard proposes it and no one has decided), or silent (nothing in the record says). No period in a silent row has a value, and the platform deletes nothing under it; the row shows "retention not set: nothing deleted" (P12 §2.8's pattern, made general). The schedule is read by the custodian, the Legal Advisor, the CPA and the Board; its attested rows are what the Board adopts as the Federation's retention policy, and this note's DRAFT (§7) wraps them.

4. A member's own requests

A member may ask four things of their own data. Each is honoured without breaking the ledger: a ledger entry is never deleted; a person becomes a closed record. Each request is a dated row on the member's record with its state and the custodian's act; a member sees their own requests and their outcomes; nobody else sees them except the custodian (and the Legal Advisor on a dispute).

Access. The member lens already shows a member everything the platform holds about them that they may see: their record, household, scopes, standings, consents, groups, attendance, their own gifts and receipts, letters sent to them, their own applications and their state. Two things the member cannot see by design: a follow-up about them (the club's, Breeze Parity §4 r2; whether that holds under some states' laws is the club plan §3.5's question, carried) and the committee's deliberations (P1 §3.3). An access request is therefore a request for the custodian to confirm that the member's own pages are complete and to add anything held elsewhere about the member (the drive's documents the platform links; a contact row from before they joined), as a written answer on P3's register (LT-I2, operational, wording open). Design choice: the platform answers access by showing, not by exporting (§4, export).

Correction. A member corrects their own fields on their own record (name, channel, address, interests, consents) — the built member lens. Facts the Federation states (standing, club, seat, the collection date) are read, never typed, and a member who disputes one opens a correction request to the owning seat (the club treasurer for a collection; the office for a scope; the tree committee for a tree line under D12 and D13) which answers by its own dated act; the platform changes nothing on the request alone. A correction to a line a decision was made on re-evaluates and flags, never auto-revokes (D12).

Erasure. A member may ask to be forgotten. The platform does: ends every grant and seat (ended never deleted), drops the directory row at once (no grace — the grace is for a lapse, not an erasure; design choice), withdraws every consent as a new dated row (R27), closes every follow-up and group membership, closes the contact channel, removes the person from every segment and roster, closes the member record — and keeps: every ledger line (dues, gifts, receipts, remittance, statement lines) with the person's identity reduced to the closed record's key; the frozen election rolls (R12); the alumni record's programme-year-club facts; the camp counts; the audit entries that name the person as the actor or the subject of an exercise of authority (R6). The personal fields of the closed record (name, channels, address, birth date) are blanked after a period the Board sets (Q-76; until set, nothing is blanked and the closed record says so), because a receipt may need to be reissued and a dispute may need the name. The record is silent on whether erasure is a right the Federation grants (no state law read here gives a non-profit's member one; the Network Plan says CCPA does not apply) — this note designs it as a courtesy the Federation offers by policy and raises whether it does (Q-140). Erasure never recalls a printed Magazine (the Network Plan's ICO reading): the next edition omits the name. A household member's erasure does not erase the household; a minor's erasure is requested by a standing holder under R6 and re-askable by the person at eighteen.

Export of their own data. The record refuses every bulk export (P19 rule 6; Breeze Parity §2; club plan §9.3) and R27's export consent governs whether others may take a member's field anywhere. A member's own copy of their own record is not in the record. Design choice, raised: the platform offers a member one download of their own record (the fields of the member lens, their consents, their gifts and receipts, their letters) as a file on request, logged on the member's record — the one export the platform makes — and nothing of any other person in it, a household member's fields included (they ask for their own). Whether the Federation offers this at all is Q-140's second half; until answered the request row exists and the download does not.

5. The eighteenth birthday as a scheduled act

What the record already holds: standing authority (R6 P1–P3) lapses on the eighteenth birthday with no action by anyone; the subject's consent is re-asked, not revoked (R6 §5; the Hub's binding rule 7); a minor's images and announcements run through the household and are re-asked of the person at eighteen (D22, D33); the young-person lens names "a refresh window before eighteen that asks them on their own channel"; P7 VI1 puts the interests re-ask "on the existing birthday job". This note makes the birthday one scheduled act with a named list, so that every note's re-ask runs from one place and the clock defect of round 39 cannot recur piecemeal:

  1. The window opens a parameter of days before the birthday (no value; Q-139): the platform asks the young person, on a channel of their own that they verify (S1), to take over their record — the verified channel of the join is the household head's, so the young person must give their own.
  2. On the day, computed on New York's local date (the clock rule; round 39's locking tests): every standing authority over the person lapses (R6); every consent the household gave on their behalf — display, contact, export per field (R27), images (D22), announcements (D33), interests (P7), programme consents (P12 §2.2) — moves to state re-ask pending and is treated as withheld until the person answers (nothing is assumed, R6 §5: "nothing is assumed on the young adult's behalf"); the directory keeps excluding them (R7's exclusion ends only when the person's own display consent exists); camp and programme screens show the household the refresh outstanding and the adult's grants ended.
  3. The person answers on their own record, consent by consent; a consent not answered stays withheld with no expiry (silence is not consent). The household's old rows stay as history (a retraction is a new entry).
  4. An adult may delegate back specific powers to a parent, withdrawable (R6 §5) — a new row, the opposite direction.
  5. Nothing is sent to the young person's own channel before they verify it; the household head is told the window opened (an operational letter, LT-P pattern, wording open).

The act is idempotent and recorded: the birthday job's run is a dated row per person (the person, the date, what lapsed, what moved to re-ask), readable by the person and the custodian. A run that finds a person with no birth date does nothing and lists them for the office (R7: no birth date means possible minor). The retention schedule gives the household's superseded consent rows the member record's rule (kept as history).

6. Backups and restore as a tested procedure

The cautionary example, at role level. The Hub's staging database runs on a plan that expires after a fixed period; everything a person entered on staging is lost on expiry and the release chain rebuilds only the seeds (slice 4c; the Hub record §3, four days before the date). The project owner decides whether to renew, pay or accept the reset. A production database on such a plan, after the first real join, would be a loss incident (§2.1) with no backup to restore from, and Q-15 — no maintainer, no budget line — is the reason it could happen. The break-glass was rehearsed on a database in staging's shape (slice 4c), which is the right habit; a production restore has never been rehearsed because production holds nothing real yet.

The procedure the platform records (the Enterprise Architecture's "an untested backup is not a backup", a proposal of August 2026 made a register here):

  • a store register: one row per store the Federation's data lives in — the production database, the secret store, the file store if any, the hosting provider's own backup, the drive (its provider's), the processor's vault (the processor's), the e-mail provider's, the accounting system, each club's Breeze until it migrates (the vendor's policy row from Breeze Parity §1a) — with the owner seat, the provider's stated backup and retention policy as a dated link, the plan it runs on and that plan's expiry if any (the staging lesson), and the last restore test;
  • a restore test as a dated act by the maintainer: which backup, restored where (never over production), what was checked (row counts against the previous day's frozen counts; a known member's record; the ledger's last close), the result, the time it took; on a cadence the Board sets (a parameter with no value; the architecture proposes quarterly; Q-139);
  • an expiry watch: any store whose plan has an expiry date opens a follow-up to the maintainer and the custodian a parameter of days before it, and the Board packet lists every store with an expiry inside the next period;
  • a loss drill once a year as a near-miss row: what would be lost if each store vanished tonight, and what a boot rebuilds — the question slice 4c answered for staging, asked of production.

The rule a build session needs now: no production data on a plan that expires; the production database's backup policy, plan and owner on the store register before the first real join or the first club's cutover (club plan §8.1's readiness list gains the row); Q-15's maintainer is the store register's owner and until Q-15 is answered the project owner is, and the register says so.

7. DRAFT — an incident and retention policy for the Board

This is a draft for the AFRP Board, written from the record and the laws read; it is not adopted and nothing in it is a rule until the Board adopts it and the register records the act. The ARFECF and ARFHSN Boards adopt the same text for their own stores or their own.

DRAFT · Data incident and retention policy of the American Federation of Ramallah, Palestine

  1. Custody. The Federation's records are in the custody of the Executive Secretary (By-Law 7.4.1), safeguarded by the Executive Director (7.9.3) and the Executive Assistant (7.10.2). The Board names one of them the data custodian for the purposes of this policy. [Q-137]
  2. What an incident is. A breach, a loss, a wrong disclosure, a failed safeguard, a vendor's incident, an account compromise or a near miss, concerning data the Federation holds about any person, in the platform, on the drive, in a mailbox, in a vendor's system or on paper.
  3. Duty to report. Any officer, staff member, committee member, club officer or volunteer who learns of a possible incident tells the data custodian the same day. Nobody is penalised for reporting a near miss.
  4. The steps. The custodian opens the incident on the platform's register and runs the steps: contain; assess, with the Legal Advisor, what was reached and whom; notify, as the Legal Advisor attests under the laws that apply, law enforcement first where the investigation needs it, then the people affected, the regulators the law names, the processor or bank where account data is involved, the insurer, the affected affiliate's Board and this Board; record; review within [a period the Board sets, Q-139].
  5. The laws. The Legal Advisor keeps on the platform the register of the breach-notification laws that reach the Federation's members, state by state, with Michigan's (MCL 445.72) and California's (Civil Code 1798.82) first, and attests each row yearly. [Q-138]
  6. Notice to a person. A notice says what happened, what was reached, what the Federation did, what the person can do and whom to contact, is signed by the President, and is sent to every affected person whether or not they have asked not to be mailed.
  7. Never held. The Federation's platform holds no card number, no Social Security number, no government identity number, no medical form, consent form or travel form of a minor, no free-text note about a person, and no biometric of anyone. A proposal to hold any of these goes to this Board, not to a build.
  8. Retention. The platform's retention schedule lists every class of data the Federation holds with its keep period, its basis, its deletion method and who may order deletion. A ledger entry, a frozen election roll, an audit entry and a committee's recorded decision are never deleted. A person who leaves, dies or asks to be forgotten becomes a closed record; their personal fields are blanked after [a period, Q-76]. A row with no period deletes nothing. The Board adopts the schedule's attested rows as the Federation's policy and decides the silent ones. [Q-76]
  9. The drive. The shared drive is the archive of record (D66). The custodian keeps its folders on the schedule; the IRS's permanent set (the determination letters, the articles and by-laws with amendments, Board minutes) is never deleted; a programme committee deletes the papers of past cycles on its own rule under D61.
  10. A member's own data. A member may see what the Federation holds about them, correct their own fields, dispute a fact the Federation states, ask to be forgotten, and [ask for a copy of their own record, Q-140], as this policy and the schedule provide; a request is answered within [a period, Q-139].
  11. Eighteen. On a person's eighteenth birthday every authority a household held over them ends and every consent given on their behalf is asked of them again; nothing is assumed.
  12. Backups. Every store of the Federation's data is on the store register with its owner, its provider's policy and its plan's expiry; no production data is on a plan that expires; a restore is tested [on a cadence, Q-139] and the result recorded.
  13. Accounts and training. Every holder of a Federation mailbox or platform seat uses multi-factor sign-in and completes the training the Digital Infrastructure Committee sets; a seat is ended the day its holder leaves.
  14. Report. The custodian reports to the Board at every meeting the incidents opened and closed, the notices sent, the schedule's silent rows and the stores' restore tests.

8. Rules

  1. A record anything points at is never deleted; death terminates it, a merge closes it, an erasure closes it (the registry's rule, Delivery Status; Breeze Parity refusals; design choice for erasure).
  2. A ledger entry, a frozen roll, an audit entry and a recorded decision are never deleted (D1's ledger; R12, R43; the Enterprise Architecture's append-only audit as proposed; P1 §3.4).
  3. Camp applications, consent, medical and travel forms never enter the platform; the health record is a bought system; the platform holds only whether a form is on file (D61 as amended).
  4. The drive is the archive of record; the platform's deletion of a row never deletes a drive document, and the drive's retention is the Board's (D66; 7.4.1, 7.9.3, 7.10.2).
  5. Every retention period is a register row with a status; a silent row has no value and deletes nothing, and says so (design choice, generalising P12 §2.8).
  6. A consent withdrawn or superseded is a new dated row, never an edit; the old row is history (R27; S5).
  7. At eighteen, standing authority lapses and every consent given on the person's behalf is re-asked, not revoked; nothing is assumed; the day is the New York local date (R6 §5; D22, D33; the Hub's binding rule 7; the clock rule of round 39).
  8. A minor never appears in any payload; no birth date means possible minor (R7); no biometric of a minor is ever held (the Network Plan; design choice for the whole platform).
  9. No card number, Social Security number or government identity number ever enters the platform (the payments boundary, Delivery Status; design choice for the rest; a widening is a Board decision).
  10. Bulk export is refused for everyone; a refused export is logged with the seat, the scope and the rule (R27's export consent; P19 rule 6; the Network Plan's export logging — design choice to build it).
  11. An incident is a row with a clock from discovery, kinds, seats and five steps; it cannot close with a step missing; the Legal Advisor attests the laws and the notices (design choice; 7.8.1).
  12. The laws are register rows attested by the Legal Advisor; the platform computes affected states and counts and decides no law (design choice; R40's shape).
  13. A breach notice is an operational letter a member must receive, signed by the President, with the FTC's content, sent regardless of marketing consent and listed for post where no channel exists (S1; P3 §2, §6; design choice).
  14. An affiliate's incident is its own scope's row; the federation scope reads nothing of an affiliate store (R39; D63 for the report).
  15. Every store of the Federation's data is on the store register with its owner, provider policy, plan expiry and last restore test; no production data on a plan that expires (design choice; the Enterprise Architecture as proposed; Q-15 as the gate).
  16. A member's access, correction, erasure and copy requests are rows on their own record, answered by named seats' dated acts; the platform changes a Federation-stated fact only by the owning seat's act (design choice; D12, D13).
  17. The public build and every first-party file carry no real living person (D15; the Hub's hygiene scanner); the day a real person enters, this note's register is live.
