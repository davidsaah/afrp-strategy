## Why the order matters

A programme cannot invite last summer's campers to the next step unless the
campers are on record with their households. A ballot cannot be trusted unless
standing is computed from real payments. A donor cannot be shown the return on
a gift unless the gift and the programme it paid for are in the same ledger.
Every strategic goal depends on the first layer being in service, with real
members in it. That is why the build started there.

## What the CRM replaces

Today one member can be five to seven unlinked records: a Dynamics 365 contact,
a record in each club's Breeze account, a Mailchimp subscriber, a payment
processor's customer, and an entry in the family-tree group. That is tolerable
for a mailing list. With digital voting approved by the Board, it becomes a
question of election integrity, and that is the finding that started this
platform.

## What the current portal and CRM already do

The research round of 2 October 2026 read how the Federation's present portal,
CRM and books work. These are practice, not rules (D41). Some of them already
move towards the design; some go further than the design would allow. In each
case the record is unchanged and the question that would settle the difference
is named.

- **The books are fed from the CRM.** In 2026 the Federation moved its books
  and the educational fund's to QuickBooks, fed from the CRM, with monthly
  statements and budget against actual; the human services network is to
  follow. Committee chairs are to get budget sheets from the books without
  seeing the full accounts.
- **One card-processing account per entity.** Card payments used to land in
  one Federation account and be moved to the affiliates by hand. There are now
  three processor accounts, one each for AFRP, ARFECF and ARFHSN, and each
  payment link in the CRM names the account it pays into. The Federation's
  books carry what it collects for the affiliates as money it owes them. That
  is close to the design's one door for every payment, with each entity's
  money kept as its own (D1, D56). How and when those balances are settled is
  Q-232; which of four roles the record means by "the CPA" is Q-231; whether
  dues are counted when paid or spread over the year is Q-233.
- **The host's event-coordinator role.** The registration system lets a
  Convention host's coordinator see every registered guest's contact details
  and export them, control the seating, and see every payment and who made it.
  The design gives the host far less (host agreements §2.4; P22 §8). Which the
  Board wants is Q-127.
- **People who are not members.** Anyone who registers on the portal becomes a
  CRM contact, with a profile, before any consent is recorded, and in 2026 the
  occupation field was used to find volunteers and donors. The design holds a
  non-member as one person record with dated roles, each with its own consent
  and retention period (P21). Whether that design is adopted is Q-70; the
  outreach is Q-236.
- **A club's view.** Today's portal shows a club president members' names and
  contact details. The design shows the Federation counts and shows contact
  fields only where each member has agreed (Q-237).
- **The CRM as an asset.** The current CRM is recorded in the Federation's
  books as a fixed asset and written down over time. Whether the Hub is
  treated the same way, and in which entity, gives Q-15 a shape.

## What the last system taught

The Federation has put in a CRM before. It went live in 2023, getting members
registered was hard, it ran over budget in 2024, and once its contractor left
nobody could maintain it. The Hub is built on the opposite assumptions:

- **Plain and long-lived.** The technology was chosen for "who maintains this in
  2031", not for novelty.
- **The design record is public.** Anyone who takes it over can read why every
  rule exists.
- **Nothing is guessed.** Where the by-laws or the Board are silent, the
  platform refuses and names the question rather than inventing an answer.

## What the record still does not say

Who maintains the Hub after launch, and what it costs each year, is written in
no document. It is open question [Q-15](../history/questions.html#Q-15), and it
belongs with the Board. The way the current CRM is carried in the books, and the
committee budget sheets planned in August 2026, are the first signs of what
that budget line could look like.
